## 1. Introduction and the month's question

Perovskite photovoltaics publishes faster than it can be read. In July 2026, 648 works passed the scope gate of this digest. No reader can absorb that volume, and no reader should have to. What determines progress is whether a reported result can be checked and reused. Efficiency numbers anchor most papers, yet their meaning depends on what was measured and how: certified or uncertified, a small laboratory cell or a large module, one scan direction or several, fresh or aged under a named stability protocol. When those details are missing, separate groups rebuild devices that were never comparable, pooled analyses average incompatible values, and corrections arrive years after the original claim. A field that produces 648 papers in a month cannot afford to learn how checkable its record is in a decade. A short audit of reporting practice, run every month while the literature is still current, is a cheap way to find out.

The month's question is how completely July's papers reported the measurements on which their own claims rest.

The audit rests on an explicit funnel. Of the 648 works that passed the scope gate, 453 had a usable abstract and were triaged. From those, 181 were read closely and 180 are cited here. The depth set is deliberately modest; its purpose is to cover the month's mechanism results and to measure reporting completeness, not to be exhaustive. The digest then does two things. It groups the mechanism findings into threads on composition and defects, interfaces and device architecture, and stability and scale-up. It then reports what these same papers chose to disclose about their own measurements, and what that pattern implies for how quickly the field can check itself.

The depth tier is based on abstracts, because a full-text retrieval probe succeeded for only 5% of the month's papers. ## 3. Composition, phases, and defects

Tightening. Composition was addressed in 271 of 648 works (41.8%), 38 read closely; defects in 101 of 648 works (15.6%), 45 read closely. Because this is the first month in the series, no month-over-month comparison is possible. On the composition axis, the dominant theme is cation and halide control of phase stability in wide-bandgap mixed-halide absorbers. A review frames photo-induced phase segregation as the root cause of bandgap inhomogeneity, listing composition design, crystallization control, and strain modulation as countermeasures [@we2032d3bab]. Experimental work converges on local halide anchoring: rubidium at the A-site induces lattice distortion that suppresses halide segregation and reduces mobile-ion concentration, giving a wide-bandgap sub-cell an open-circuit voltage of 1.46 V [@w874a83b658], while Sr2+ localizes halide anions, prevents I2Br- triple halide formation, and yields 20.18% at 1.77 eV and 19.02% at 1.84 eV [@w6f1100a18d]. Additives act similarly: TPPA manages residual PbI2 and retains 90% of initial PCE after 750 h of MPPT versus 270 h for controls [@wfef0182286], and HPA builds spacer cations in situ through hydrogen bonds with FA+ to form a 2D layer, with retention of 95.5% and 90% after 3000 h at 30C in N2 and 800 h at 85C in N2 [@w24b727a548]. Concurrent 4D-STEM and EDX mapping shows that inactive hexagonal polytypes are predominantly driven by local cesium deficiencies [@wb2c8efd090]. Design guidance comes from first-principles ensembles predicting about 29% peak efficiency for CsPb1-xSnxBr3 at x > 0.70 [@w62cd66534a] and an epsilon-field screen of 5043 compositions in 0.02 seconds [@w578ed705e2]. Tin composition work advances on several fronts. A high-n (n = 10) quasi-2D ACI platform modified with methylammonium bromide produces a nearly two-order-of-magnitude increase in ambipolar carrier mobility [@we0bd134229], and 3-furoic acid decouples nucleation from growth in tin perovskites through coordination with SnI2 and hydrogen bonding with FAI [@w6da87b41b4]. Flexible tin cells reach 15.56% PCE (14.67% certified) on 0.049 cm2 and 22.21% under 1000 lux [@wc76f973aa0]. Lead-free studies include Bi substitution in CsPbI3 and CsPbBr3, which modulates bandgaps but evolves toward phase segregation because trivalent cation incorporation is thermodynamically unstable [@w83ed2cdc38], hydrostatic strain tuning of CsGeF3 and CsSnF3 to 1.9 eV and 1.32 eV at 4% compression [@w31408838ca], and epitaxial CsSnI3 on Ag(100), where an interfacial SnIx layer templates terrace growth [@w39bdd2e787]. The defects literature is dominated by molecular passivation of undercoordinated Pb2+ and halide vacancies, with agreement that buried interfaces matter as much as top surfaces. An ultrathin SCP layer at the perovskite electron-contact interface delivers a certified 26.84% PCE and 90% retention after 1485 h of MPPT [@w835935cb8b]; the carbazole SAM BTP passivates undercoordinated Pb2+ via Lewis acid-base interactions and reaches 26.85% [@w6b39605681]; dithiopyr bridges adjacent Pb2+ sites, relieves lattice strain, and supports 26.9% champion and 21.28% on 30 by 30 cm2 modules [@w928e928ca3]; and AHBA regulates the SnO2 buried interface through a cross-linked network with 3.5 ps interfacial extraction [@w1281bc0fa4]. SHAP analysis of a curated SAM dataset identifies the head group as the dominant efficiency driver [@w2fdf9a6ead]. These studies disagree on the SAM tail: longer alkyl chains strengthen the interfacial dipole and raise open-circuit voltage, yet the same chains act as tunneling barriers that impede hole extraction [@w6583b47d81]. Grain-boundary and additive passivation forms a second, consistent cluster. Additives incorporated during crystallization accumulate precisely at boundaries and reach 26.32% champion PCE [@wa070423480]; BFDS and DMI polymerize in situ into dual-dynamic covalent networks, giving 22.00% in HTL-free carbon devices [@w20c87173a8]; and amphoteric ionic liquids passivate both Lewis acidic and basic defects, with GUDHP on 1.84 eV material reaching 20.06% at 85.74% fill factor [@w11acd6e2a3]. mTFMA treatment raises average carrier lifetime from 529.7 to 2479.4 ns [@w89d2eccbcd]; FTAC reconstructs the surface via Pb-N coordination and iodine-vacancy compensation with 91.16% retention after 1,000 h at 85C [@w838cf9d6c6]; TTA coordinates dangling bonds through its carboxyl group while sulfur binds free PbI2 [@w59b0282733]; and the 3D amino distribution of 4APM gives the strongest interaction with PbI2 and FAI among three candidates, with over 95% retention after 1000 h of MPPT at 65C [@w3cbf04b58b]. Choline chemistry appears twice: a choline-derivative anchoring ligand enables air-processed passivation with over 90% retention after 2000 h at 65C and 90% RH [@w3107ad06bf], and acetylthiocholine chloride fills vacancies and suppresses halide segregation in modules [@w13daad8f4e]. Vacancy-specific and non-molecular results sharpen the mechanism. Pb-I diatomic and iodine single-atomic vacancies on Cs-rich, Pb-poor evaporated surfaces are the primary origin of Voc loss, and BaI2 vapor passivates them without phase-transforming lattice changes [@w3f3e9f7935]. Nonadiabatic simulations show oxygen vacancies at the MAPbI3/NiO interface accelerate hole transfer while promoting nonradiative recombination [@w7b865bb739]. Plasma-based passivation forms conformal heterostructures on large-area films, with small-area devices retaining 98.1% of initial PCE after 2000 h of MPPT at 85C and a certified 23.5% on 65.05 cm2 modules [@wac6484e3b7]. A third theme links defects to strain: dithiopyr relieves lattice strain [@w928e928ca3], MPUA converts residual tensile strain into compression [@we13a074e7c], C60-azo photoisomerizes under UV to generate compressive stress at the buried interface, retaining 92.7% after 488 h [@wb72c7d9b9e], and a DOP-BTA chelation network passivates defects while relieving tensile stress in CsPbBr3 [@w42fa96498e]. THz-TDS adds a probe, with the 1.58 THz oscillator strength in MAPbI3 scaling linearly with XPS-quantified CH3NH2 defect concentration [@w083c55af92]. Stability claims are not directly comparable because protocols differ, though retention above 90% appears across the works cited above. A review argues that perovskite heterostructures reduce defect density, suppress ion migration, and relieve strain simultaneously [@w3288b227e5], and simulated champions of 29.08% and 29.64% from SCAPS-1D studies remain computational upper bounds [@w5937e52357] [@w7595cb237e].

## 4. Interfaces and device architecture

Of the 648 works screened this month, 119 (18.4%) address interfaces, 39 read closely, and 64 (9.9%) address device architecture, 22 read closely. This is the first month in the series, so no month-over-month comparison is possible.

The buried hole-selective contact of inverted cells is the underexplored factor controlling efficiency and stability [@wb92b8e6931]. Multidentate anchoring recurs among the best results. A bisphosphonate mediator reconstructs the NiOx/SAM interface and coordinates undercoordinated Pb2+, certified 27.31% [@wb081be2430]. A tetradentate Fe(III) porphyrin co-SAM reaches 27.06% champion and 26.82% certified on 21.54 cm2 [@w14e5e9203a]. The noncoplanar molecule TTA blocks pi-pi SAM aggregation, giving 33.3% champion and 33.1% certified, 23.7% in wide-bandgap junctions near 1.67 eV [@w6680994984]. A sulfonyl calixarene host-guest layer stabilizes the buried interface, 27.12% champion and certified 22.25% on 655.2 cm2 aperture [@w7312947de8].

Co-adsorption and dipole tuning form a second cluster. Proton-transfer-complex confinement improves carbazole SAM dispersion, 26.23% and 1368 h at 75 °C [@wc812c018cc]. The phosphine TFMP joins Me-4PACz and 4PABCz in a ternary SAM, bridging the layers by coordinating Pb2+ and stabilizing FA+, 26.78% [@w95fde3cb80]. Co-assembled 4AA with DB18C6 lifts coverage from 0.57 to 0.79 and the dipole from 2 to 7 Debye, 26.33% [@w7edb0dd547]. Fluorinated F-2PACz strengthens the dipole and passivates halide vacancies, 25.75% champion and 25.06% on 1 cm2 with only 3.11% relative loss [@w1c71423ecf]. Aggregation control is a shared target: BMIMBF4 ionic liquid lubricates the SAM, 26.82% with 92.3% after 800 h at 85 °C [@w7a4d838182], while Lindqvist polyoxometalates raise anchoring from 29.93% to 53.98% and cut desorption from 30.7% to 5.6%, 25.15% [@wb9b56a4401]. Tetraphosphorylated phthalocyanine SAMs reach modules, certified 23.1% and stabilized 22.9% on 20.3 cm2, no degradation over 61 days of ISOS-O-1 outdoor aging [@w26ec567716].

In n-i-p stacks the SnO2 interface dominates. A preburied 2D perovskite forms in situ at SnO2/perovskite, passivating defects and switching tensile to compressive strain, 25.67% [@w62aad890c5]. Dipotassium methanedisulfonate coordinates undercoordinated Sn4+, passivates oxygen vacancies, and induces a positive dipole, 25.76% [@w3e29e6072d]. The chelators PDA and PCA bind Pb2+ via phenanthroline nitrogens, closing bottom-interface voids, 25.15% on 0.04 cm2 [@wdeff9bc4f8]. An ultrathin PTS layer anchors the perovskite/C60 contact via sulfonate and pyrene pi-pi interactions, 26.53%, 96% after 1000 h of MPPT and 91% after 300 cycles from -40 to 85 °C [@w9e872c2711]. At the top contact, MeO-2PACz suppresses photoinduced phase segregation and I2 expulsion [@w4339a61ac8].

Non-SAM hole layers see similar work. NiOx at 20% PO2 doubles extraction, defect density falling from 3.01e11 to 1.71e11 cm-3, cells still at 13.7% [@w78416fe1d8]. SAMs on NiOx raise fracture energy from 0.94 to 4.82 J m-2, about tenfold over ITO [@wc6f5387bcd]. NaOAc exchange lifts the PEDOT:PSS work function from 4.27 to 4.38 eV and conductivity by 55%, 19.38% [@wc8deea5617]. In CsPbI3, BDA with 5-FBA lifts cells from 12.93% to 20.71% at fill factors to 83.92% [@wf3a60218cc].

SCAPS-1D names FTO/ZnO/BaSnO3/(FAPbI3)0.85(MAPbBr3)0.15/Au the best of seven HTL-free designs, 29.1% [@wd5438587ca], and a Pt/Cu2BaSnS4 stack peaks at 30.81% at 300 K, falling with temperature [@w64076f2023]. A surrogate model puts a tolerant CsPbBr3/CsPbI3/MASnI3 tandem at 29.61% versus 28.86% nominal [@w0eebdb7f88].

The architecture axis converges on two-terminal tandems, beyond the roughly 29.4% crystalline-silicon limit that laboratory devices have crossed [@w5e13e9a78f]. Sr2+ stiffens the metal-halide framework and weakens electron-phonon coupling, 31.66% on fully-textured perovskite/silicon tandems [@wd8a16c064a]. TPB-Br yields 23.63% in the 1.68 eV sub-cell and 32.03% on 1.05 cm2, retaining 90.1%, 81.2%, and 93.4% after 1200 h of storage, thermal aging, and light soaking [@w867cf96d60]. A radiation-hardened tandem keeps certified 27.49% on 12.56 cm2, nearly 80% after 1 MeV electrons at 1×10^14 e/cm2 and 93% after 150 keV protons at 1×10^12 p/cm2 [@we8f78b4430], though AM0 MPPT between -100 and +100 °C shows wide-gap segregation above 50 °C, only partly reversed in 45-min dark cooling [@w2c42f91d9d]. Co-evaporated perovskite/CIGS reaches 26.4% champion and 25.9% certified, rigid 26.01% with Voc 1.85 V without NiOx [@w4d51d06473], and NPAI-modified four-terminal semitransparent cells give a 27.7% stack with 21.4% cells, 61.3% average NIR transmittance over 800-1200 nm, and 94.5% after 2000 bends at 10 mm radius [@w9f8462c60f].

All-perovskite tandems advance through crystallization control. A solvent-resistant 3-TMA interlayer supports 33.21% champion and 32.13% certified, with 23.17% and 19.61% sub-cells at 1.67 and 1.84 eV [@w1270b8a2df]. Controlled MA incorporation suppresses Sn-Pb segregation and Sn2+ oxidation, 30.13% champion and 29.57% certified, a 23.90% certified Sn-Pb junction [@wa4106e691e]. SALC coordination with SnI2 homogenizes Sn/Pb, 29.44% champion and 28.84% certified tandems with 22.99% singles at 0.892 V [@wa7fe03f1a3]. A language model screened over 8000 solvents for a 32.5% tandem, picking gamma-valerolactone, which coordinates FA+ and orients grain growth [@w0f4b4521b7]. Dry-contact trimming removes Sn-rich species without a retained 2D cap, lifting Sn-Pb cells above 24% toward a 30% tandem [@we00dc1f2c7], a graphene oxide/SAM bilayer gives 27.3% champion and 27.0% stabilized tandems from 22.1% and 0.88 V single cells holding 90% after 770 h at 25 °C [@w2d8ea4c112], and zoledronic acid anchoring supports a 30.62% tandem whose 20.55% sub-cell keeps over 90% after 200 h of MPP [@wf21abbe510].

Tin chemistry closes the axis. Zero-valent Sn nanoparticles eliminate Sn4+ by redox shuttling, certified 23.26% on 4.05 mm2, over 90% after 500 h of MPP [@w684bdde0f6], while MnCl2 and ZnCl2 expel tin as volatile SnCl4, leaving grain-boundary passivation, 23.06% [@w73c1ad27f4]. Where SnF2 and reductants fail to tame crystallization in thick inverted absorbers, the (111)-orienting 4F-PEABr gives 21.5% on 0.128 cm2 with the best MPP stability [@w75d0264e76]. Ionic-liquid/polyurethane grain-boundary bridges suppress halide segregation, 20.85% wide-gap cells with T90 beyond 1000 h in a 25.92% stack [@w274df45f60].

## 5. Stability, scale-up, and deployment

This month, the stability axis appears in 66 of 648 works (10.2%), of which 25 were read closely, and the scale-up axis appears in 27 of 648 works (4.2%), of which 12 were read closely. Because this is the first month in the series, no month-over-month comparison is possible.

Certified records keep rising while active areas grow. The nonfullerene polymer 2PB-T replaces unstable fullerene electron transport layers in inverted cells, with a champion 27.8% and certified 27.3% [@w7893ea83ee]. Coassembly of Me-4PACz with CzOTf stabilizes the buried interface and gives certified 27.31% single cells, 32.84% perovskite/HJT-Si tandems, and 21.54% on a 766 cm2 module [@w8179612923]. FATU and GlyHCl dual additives yield Sn-Pb films with grains over 800 nm, a certified 27.33% tandem, and a blade-coated single junction at 21.02% with VOC raised from 0.797 to 0.850 V [@wc233e3a601]. Air-blade MA-free cells reach 26.14%, certified at 25.75% [@wac71174711]. Phase locking of alpha-phase FAPbI3 during large-area printing gave 100 cm2 rigid and flexible modules at 21.5% and 19.5% [@w1f48f1cf13]. Asymmetric BTI-derived SAMs reached 25.38% on 1 cm2 and 20.59% on a 655.2 cm2 module [@w4dcd09c9cc]. Indol-3-ylethylammonium spacer cations align the perovskite/C60 interphase, and a 25 cm2 module achieved a certified 23.21% [@w80b7120258]. TBPAA cells gave 26.12% on 0.09 cm2 and 22.41% on 14.0 cm2 [@wec46495650]. Phenylenediamine dihydriodide in a 1.93-eV perovskite raised the certified triple-junction efficiency to 26.18% with a Voc of 3.148 V on 1.17 cm2 [@wf8762b1025].

Operational stability is advanced at interfaces and in the bulk. 2PB-T devices retained more than 98.6% of initial PCE after 1752 h of MPPT at 85 degrees C in air [@w7893ea83ee]. PF2C, a multidentate fullerene that stabilizes PEABr on the surface, leaves encapsulated devices at 98.7% after 2100 h of MPP tracking and 95.8% after 1800 h at 85 degrees C [@wfce6bf6825]. Air-blade cells kept 93.11% after 1300 h at maximum power point under 1 sun [@wac71174711]. Rubidium evens the vertical Cs distribution in CsDMAMAFA films, and Rb-doped wide-bandgap cells reach 21.62% at 1.67 eV and 22.51% with PDAI2 treatment [@w3c40814114]. PAH retards FAPbI3 crystallization in antisolvent-free processing and grows grains to 1.11 plus or minus 0.01 micrometers [@w8894832d3f]. Phase-pure n = 1 2D perovskites form without thermal activation, with domains parallel and tilted to the 3D lattice [@w36fa990495].

Flexible and ambient routes overlap with stability. Open-circuit voltages and fill factors above 90% of the Shockley-Queisser radiative limit appear across 1.53 to 1.77 eV bandgaps, and ambient-air flexible cells reached 23.03% and kept 95% after 10,000 bending cycles [@w333bd88d15]. DPVP enables reproducible fabrication at 20 to 40% relative humidity, with 24.2% small devices and 20.5% on 12 cm2 mini-modules [@wa295aba58c]. TTB-modified precursors held 25.76% after seven days in ambient conditions and 23.88% after three months [@w0ff7166138]. Vapor-phase annealing gives additive-free inverted cells a champion 22.15% [@wff60f20998].

Encapsulation and degradation physics draw attention. Inkjet-printed PHPS with VUV conversion forms SiOxNy barrier films, and encapsulated cells kept 93% plus or minus 5% of PCE after 1000 h at 25 degrees C and 60% RH under ISOS-D-1 [@wc4ab35c5ef]. In carbon-based hole-transport-layer-free cells under ISOS-L-1 and ISOS-L-2, light-induced degradation starts at the top and buried interfaces and spreads into the bulk [@wb0e18eb1b0], while a PEACl-induced 2D capping layer raises such devices to 17.75% with a fill factor of 69.02% [@w3d0916681c]. Residual lithium from Li-TFSI doping of Spiro-OMeTAD migrates during operation, motivating the volatile dopant AM-TFSI, which leaves negligible residues [@wdeb25e9aee].

Protocol choice changes conclusions. Trapped charge triggers phase segregation, and wide-bandgap devices under MSPT retain approximately 90% of initial power after 700 h, unlike MPPT-operated devices [@w62d7795909]. The gap from the MPPT retention figures above suggests that tracking mode should be reported alongside every lifetime number.

Data-driven diagnostics are emerging. A deep-learning framework estimates retention from multimodal luminescence imaging during aging, with global luminescence carrying most of the predictive signal [@wc6f365abc6]. PerovSegNet reaches 87.25% mean Average Precision, beating the baseline by 4.08% at 25.22% lower computational cost, with zero-shot transfer to lead-free perovskites [@w3064848061]. One review argues for tracking stress and strain under fluctuating temperature, illumination, and humidity, favoring buried interface engineering and polymer incorporation [@w23dfb999c6]. In situ curvature and GI-XRD show tensile stress relaxing by diffusion-mediated inelastic deformation, and stress pre-relaxation cuts moisture uptake by up to an order of magnitude [@w24418e3008].

Scale-up is also economic. A cradle-to-grave LCA of an inverted PSC gives 0.20 kg CO2 eq./kWh, an EPBT of 0.53 years, an EROI of 9.50, and an LCOE of 0.29 EUR/kWh at a production cost of 0.67 EUR/cm2 [@w2262e9eab9]. European recycling of perovskite PV glass can save up to 7 dollars per square meter, with scalability assessed to 4 TW [@w2ff52e5fe9]. Upscaling to series-connected 64 cm2 modules is dominated by resistive transport and interconnection losses that match intrinsic material losses [@wab42a29022], and flexible module research still lags rigid modules, with efficient and scalable flexible modules unresolved [@w255a6446f2]. A photovoltaic cathode with Pt reduced to 5.60 micrograms per square centimeter delivered 19.8 mA per square centimeter at 0.0 V RHE and retained 77% after 160 h in acid [@w499ec5dd4e].

Some records tagged to these axes carry no usable data: two Zenodo entries are title lists about solar-powered electric vehicles [@w2cdf9b41af] [@w8c0f75e47d], and a duplicate Zenodo pair offers a spin-torsion theory of three-stage efficiency decline without experimental grounding [@w4acdb2a3c2] [@w73bd3f1663].

## 6. Reporting audit

This section reports how completely the July 2026 papers describe their own measurements. The audit reads abstracts, so it measures what authors chose to summarise, not what appears in an experimental section or in supporting information. Two tiers are covered: a corpus tier of 456 abstracts and a depth tier of 181 abstracts. Because the hand-labelled validation set for the detector is not yet complete, every percentage in this section is a lower bound and is written as "detected in at least", with no precision or recall figure attached.

In the corpus tier, an efficiency value was detected in at least 50.4% of abstracts. A stabilised or MPPT efficiency was detected in at least 15.4%. Certification was detected in at least 8.3%. A device area was detected in at least 9.2%. Hysteresis or scan direction was detected in at least 2.6%, and a labelled ISOS stability protocol was detected in at least 1.3%. The complete Voc, Jsc and FF triplet was detected in at least 2.9%. Roughly half of the month's abstracts therefore claim an efficiency, while very few report certification or the full parameter triplet that would let a reader check that claim against another paper's. The headline number is the quantity authors summarise most often, and it is also the quantity whose meaning depends most on the details left out of the summary.

The depth tier scores higher on six of the seven measures: efficiency detected in at least 61.3%, stabilised or MPPT efficiency detected in at least 26.5%, certification detected in at least 13.3%, device area detected in at least 13.8%, hysteresis or scan direction detected in at least 3.9%, and an ISOS label detected in at least 2.8%. The exception is the complete triplet, detected in at least 2.2% in the depth tier, while in the corpus tier it was detected in at least 2.9%. The general direction is expected rather than reassuring. The depth tier was selected partly on venue citation percentile, so better-reported work from high-visibility venues is over-represented by construction. The gap between the tiers therefore describes the sampling as much as it describes reporting practice, and its practical effect is that the corpus-level lower bounds understate how completely the strongest papers of the month disclose their measurements.

The main limitation of measuring reporting this way is structural. An abstract is a summary, and its silence is not proof that a measurement was not made. Certification, active area, scan direction and ISOS labels routinely appear in the experimental section without ever reaching the abstract, and naming a stability protocol in an abstract is not a field-wide convention. The percentages above therefore bound what was summarised, not what was measured. They remain worth tracking for two reasons. The abstract is what most readers screen and what automated databases ingest, so a quantity absent from it is invisible to the pipelines that aggregate the record. And the pattern of what does reach the abstract is itself informative: a single efficiency number travels easily into a summary, while the contextual facts that make efficiency comparable, certification, area, scan direction and protocol, mostly do not. A monthly audit of that gap costs little, and it is the part of this digest that measures the field's willingness to make its own claims checkable.

## 7. Gaps and suggested future research

The reporting audit above shows that this month's abstracts state headline results far more often than the details needed to check them. Four gaps follow, and each admits a concrete change in reporting or research practice.

The complete Voc, Jsc and FF triplet appears in 2.9% of abstracts, and device area in 9.2%. Without the triplet an efficiency cannot be decomposed or checked against device physics, and without the area a current cannot be told apart from a current density. The fix is a minimum record: journals and databases should require the triplet, the active area with its definition, and the scan conditions as machine-readable fields for every reported device, so that pooled analyses receive parameters rather than percentages alone.

Independent certification is mentioned in 8.3% of abstracts, so almost every efficiency in the corpus is a self-report. A workable rule is that at least one champion device per paper be measured by an independent laboratory, that the laboratory be named, and that certified and uncertified values be printed as separate numbers instead of merged into one headline figure.

An ISOS protocol label appears in 1.3% of abstracts. Stability claims therefore circulate without a shared testing frame, and lifetimes measured in different laboratories cannot be compared. The field already owns the ISOS vocabulary, so requiring a protocol code beside every stability number, in the abstract as well as in the experimental section, would turn degradation data into a cumulative record.

Finally, the scale-up axis holds only 27 of 648 works, so the corpus is dominated by small laboratory cells and module-level behaviour stays unresolved. Funding calls and journal scopes that explicitly ask for larger-area validation, together with routine reporting of champion-device area, would let the community track efficiency against area instead of inferring scalability from the smallest cells on record.

## 8. Limitations

Venue-impact-prioritised selection under-samples preprints, regional journals and non-English venues, so the depth subset is biased toward well-indexed English-language publishing. Work that circulates chiefly on preprint servers or in regional venues can carry mechanism results and reporting habits that this digest never sees. Abstracts in languages other than English were machine translated, and translation can blur technical wording, which is a second reason to read the small non-English share of the corpus with care.
