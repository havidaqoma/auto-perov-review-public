Angewandte Chemie International Edition

This work elucidates the molecular origins of interfacial degradation and establishes radical-scavenging interfacial engineering as a universal strategy to decouple synergistic degradation pathways, providing a robust framework for developing highly stable perovskite photovoltaic technologies.

Elementary Steps of Energy Conversion in Strongly Correlated Systems: Beyond Single Quasiparticles and Rigid Bands

Energy & environment materials
Energy conversion in materials can be considered as a sequence of elementary steps initiated by a primary excitation. While these steps are quite well understood in classical semiconductors in terms of quasiparticle excitations and interactions, their understanding in strongly correlated materials is still elusive. Here, we review the progress which has been achieved over recent years by studies of manganite perovskite oxides as a model system for materials with strong correlations. They show a subtle interplay of different types of correlations, that is, electron–phonon, electron–electron, and spin–spin, resulting in rich physical phenomena due to competition between different ground states accompanied by temperature‐ and field‐induced phase transitions. They strongly impact various types of energy conversion and transport processes including friction at surfaces, thermal transport, time‐, energy‐, power‐dependent optical excitations as well as photovoltaic energy conversion. The underlying microscopic processes can be broken down to the behavior of the low‐energy thermal and high‐energy optical excitations, their interactions, transport and conversion which are theoretically analyzed by using models of interacting and tunable quasiparticles: Their nature and interactions can change during excitation, transport and phase transitions, thus modifying electronic structure. At sufficiently high stimulation, quasiparticle excitations can even induce or actuate phase transitions. As a result, we obtained a comprehensive understanding of energy conversion steps going far beyond single quasiparticle pictures and rigid band approximations well known for conventional semiconductors.

How to label

Judge only what the abstract says. Not the title, not what the paper probably did, not what you know about the group. If the abstract does not say it, the answer is N.

Use U when the text genuinely could be read either way. U is data, not failure — it measures how often the abstract itself is ambiguous.

1. states_pce

Y if the abstract gives a PCE/efficiency number for a device. "efficiency improved substantially" is N (no number).

2. states_certified

Y only for independent certification: the word certified, or a named lab (NREL, AIST, Fraunhofer ISE, JET, Newport). "we measured 25.1%" is N. "uncertified" is N.

3. states_area

Y for a device/aperture area with a unit, e.g. 0.09 cm2, 1 cm², 65 cm2 module. Careful: 24.1 mA cm-2 is a current density, not an area → N.

4. states_t80

Y for an operational lifetime figure: T80, T90, "retained 92% after 1000 h". A storage/shelf number with no operation is still Y if it gives hours. "stable operation" with no number is N.

5. isos_protocol

Y only if an ISOS protocol is named (ISOS-L-1, ISOS‐D‐3, ISOS-L-2I). Note the hyphen may be a Unicode dash that looks identical. "ISOS protocols were followed" without a code is U.

An abstract may report its own device and also quote a record from another paper. Example: "our p-i-n cell reached 25.8%, approaching the certified 33.9% of perovskite/Si tandems".
