Reading the device classes side by side reveals that performance gains claimed in one architecture are frequently paid for through degradation pathways detailed in another. In single-junction cells and wide-bandgap (WBG) subcells, carbazole-based self-assembled monolayer (SAM) hole transport layers (HTLs) optimize interfacial dipoles and passivate buried substrate traps. Those contacts yielded a certified two-terminal (2T) tandem power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 28.78% [@10.1038/s41467-025-58111-y] and 31.47% on 0.945 cm2 [@10.1002/anie.202509782]. However, reading these contact modifications alongside large-area module manufacturing demonstrates a conflict: the molecular characteristics responsible for low interfacial recombination velocity directly impede scalable processing. The hydrophobic end groups and dipole moments of phosphonic acid SAMs form micellar agglomerates of roughly 460 nm that cause severe dewetting during solution casting [@10.1002/anie.202512660]. When translated from spin-coating to continuous blade coating across large substrates, these aggregates produce pinholes and non-uniform nucleation. Co-assembly strategies are then required, and they yield 20.16% on a 63.09 cm2 module [@10.1002/aenm.202502639] or 22.09% certified on 8.5 cm2 [@10.1021/acsnano.5c07208]. In addition, the redox susceptibility of SAM anchors on nickel oxide under operational bias creates an interfacial failure pathway. The high open-circuit voltage (Voc) demonstrated on small-area devices is purchased through mechanical and chemical fragility that drives rapid delamination under lateral hydrodynamic shear during scalable coating.

A parallel conflict appears at top interfaces, where surface passivators that optimize single-junction cells generate transport bottlenecks in monolithic tandems. Single-junction devices employ bulky organic cations to form two-dimensional (2D) or quasi-2D capping heterojunctions that passivate halide vacancies and suppress non-radiative decay. Those caps delivered a certified single-junction PCE of 26.96% with a champion of 27.02% and a stabilized output of 26.61% on 1 cm2 under continuous 1-sun maximum power point (MPP) tracking [@10.1038/s41566-025-01791-1], alongside 28.81% certified in 2T tandems [@10.1002/adma.202504321]. Yet when integrated into monolithic tandem stacks, the low out-of-plane conductivity and wide bandgap of these low-dimensional capping layers establish electrostatic potential steps and transport barriers. Under operational bias, these barriers trap photogenerated carriers and create electrostatic crosstalk across the interconnecting layer (ICL). In addition, transparent conducting oxides sputtered directly onto low-dimensional capping layers induce chemical and mechanical damage. Unshielded operational electric fields separately lower the activation energy for halide migration beneath the passivating cap. What single-junction studies classify as defect deactivation functions in tandem stacks as an extraction barrier that accelerates phase demixing under continuous operational load.

Comparing narrow-bandgap (NBG) mixed tin-lead (Sn-Pb) subcells with WBG mixed-halide top cells reveals that two separate literatures describe identical degradation physics under distinct vocabularies. The Sn-Pb literature attributes operational decay to chemical Sn(II) oxidation. Reducing agents such as tris(2-carboxyethyl)phosphine (TCEP) achieve 23.86% single-junction efficiency, 90% retention after 563 h of maximum power point tracking (MPPT), and an uncertified 28.86% 2T tandem champion [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c01995]. Hydrazine monohydrochloride instead achieves 23.21% low-bandgap single-junction PCE and 28.31% certified 2T tandem PCE [@10.1038/s41467-025-58810-6]. Concurrently, WBG top-cell studies treat halide demixing as a light-induced phase separation problem governed by crystallographic orientation. That literature utilizes (111) texturing to achieve a certified 2T tandem PCE of 30.26% with over 1,000 h stability [@10.1038/s41467-024-55377-6], or bidentate anchoring via methylammonium chloride derivatives (MAMCl) for (100) orientation to yield 28.06% certified 2T tandem PCE [@10.1002/adma.202513281]. Reading these literatures together reveals that local lattice microstrain is the primary driver in both subcells. Incorporating sodium 1,3,6-naphthalenetrisulfonate (NTS) or lattice-stabilizing networks into Sn-Pb lattices homogenizes microstrain. Those routes achieve certified 2T tandem efficiencies of 28.9% [@10.1038/s41467-025-64274-5] and 29.2% [@10.1038/s41467-025-62661-6], demonstrating that Sn(II) oxidation is secondary to vacancy formation caused by lattice distortion. In both architectures, unrelaxed microstrain lowers the kinetic activation barrier for ion migration: the same strain that permits phase segregation in mixed-halide top cells accelerates vacancy-assisted oxidation in mixed-metal bottom cells. However, monolithic 2T integration prevents applying both chemical remedies concurrently, as volatile reducing additives in the Sn-Pb subcell degrade during high-temperature deposition of subsequent layers.

Synthesizing findings across all device classes demonstrates that the year's evidence does not permit simultaneous claims of high certified efficiency, scalable area, and verified operational durability. The highest efficiency benchmarks are confined to small, uncertified apertures. One 2T perovskite/silicon tandem reached an uncertified champion PCE of 33.5% with an International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) ISOS-L-2 time to 80% of initial performance (T80) lifetime of 3,200 hours [@10.1038/s41467-025-66480-7], and an uncertified 25.86% cell was prepared via solvent coordination [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c01538]. Scaling to large areas causes efficiencies to drop to 18.73% certified on 100.15 cm2 [@10.1002/aenm.202500598] or 24.0% uncertified on 100 cm2 [@10.1126/science.adx9650], without accredited operational lifespans. Meanwhile, durable devices such as perovskite/copper indium gallium selenide (CIGS) tandems achieving 1,123 h T80 at 28% certified PCE [@10.1021/jacs.5c13264] require compliant interlayers that compromise absolute efficiency. Molecular passivators that suppress defects on static small cells cause dewetting during blade coating and electronic crosstalk across tandem interconnections. Gains reported within isolated device classes therefore cannot be combined additively.
