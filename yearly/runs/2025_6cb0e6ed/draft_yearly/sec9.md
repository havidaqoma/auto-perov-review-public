Reconciling the 2025 literature exposes an empirical disconnect: champion efficiency records and verified operational durability remain mutually exclusive. Across 7931 works meeting the scope gate and 450 read closely, only 66 reported an independently certified power conversion efficiency (PCE), only 15 reported a time to 80% of initial efficiency (T80) lifetime, and only 24 named an International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol. The twelve-month certified frontier demonstrates punctuated dispersion rather than continuous progress, between 2025-01 (best certified 30.26% in two-terminal (2T) tandems [@10.1038/s41467-024-55377-6]) and 2025-12 (best certified 30.1% [@10.1038/s41467-025-67350-y]), the net change in best certified efficiency was -0.16 percentage points. Uncertified self-reported claims outstripped certified benchmarks in eleven of twelve months, rising to 33.2% in 2025-05 and 33.5% in 2025-12 [@10.1038/s41467-025-66480-7], coinciding with certified data only in 2025-08 at 33.15% [@10.1038/s41467-025-62389-3]. This divergence reveals that the highest reported efficiencies reflect unverified, sub-0.1 cm2 laboratory artifacts where transient electrostatic screening conceals persistent defect vulnerabilities.

A primary mechanistic gap centers on the physical permanence of interfacial contact engineering, where studies diverge on whether molecular modifiers permanently heal deep electronic traps or establish resistive barriers that accelerate degradation under operational bias. At buried hole-transport layer (HTL) contacts, carbazole phosphonic acid self-assembled monolayer (SAM) molecules optimize dipole alignment and passivate interface states. Those contacts delivered certified 2T tandem efficiencies of 28.78% [@10.1038/s41467-025-58111-y] and 31.47% on 0.945 cm2 [@10.1002/anie.202509782]. However, strongly acidic phosphonic acid anchors corrode reactive nickel oxide (NiOx) contacts [@10.1038/s41467-025-59515-6]. Ultraviolet (UV) illumination irreversibly degrades monolayer hole extraction, with T80 limited to 500 h on an uncertified 26.0% cell [@10.1126/sciadv.adu3493], and precursor micellar agglomerates of roughly 460 nm cause dewetting pinholes [@10.1002/anie.202512660] [@10.1002/aenm.202405675] [@10.1002/anie.202519875]. At the top heterojunction, wide-bandgap two-dimensional (2D) perovskite capping layers suppress surface non-radiative recombination to reach a certified 26.96% PCE [@10.1038/s41566-025-01791-1] or champion 26% [@10.1002/anie.202502950]. Yet these low-dimensional overlayers introduce anisotropic out-of-plane transport barriers that trap photogenerated charges, and under operational illumination unshielded internal electric fields lower the activation energy for halide migration, accelerating phase demixing and vacancy aggregation under continuous maximum power point tracking (MPPT).

A parallel gap governs phase stability across tandem absorbers and large-area modules: in 2T tandems, current matching requires mixed-halide wide-bandgap (WBG) absorbers that remain susceptible to photoinduced halide segregation [@10.1038/s41467-025-57010-6] [@10.1021/jacs.5c10094] [@10.1039/d4ee05604c]. In narrow-bandgap (NBG) mixed tin-lead (Sn-Pb) subcells, chemical defect passivation delivers 28.31% certified PCE [@10.1038/s41467-025-58810-6]. However, unrelaxed lattice microstrain drives phase decomposition, necessitating strain-stabilizing networks to achieve certified values of 28.9% [@10.1038/s41467-025-64274-5] and 29.2% [@10.1038/s41467-025-62661-6]. Translating these materials to large modules imposes an uncompromising area penalty, visible across every reported aperture. The largest reported aperture reached 764 cm2 with a certified PCE of 26.65% [@10.1038/s41467-025-63389-z], and 2T tandems achieved 28.9% certified on 60 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-025-63673-y]. Single-junction modules nonetheless dropped to 18.73% certified on 100.15 cm2 [@10.1002/aenm.202500598] or 24.0% uncertified on 100 cm2 [@10.1126/science.adx9650]. None of these large-area certified devices demonstrated accredited operational lifetimes, and conversely the few works reporting T80 metrics rely on disparate thermal and electrical conditions that preclude direct comparison. Those works include 3200 h under ISOS-L-2 for an uncertified 33.5% tandem [@10.1038/s41467-025-66480-7] and 1000 h on a module with certified 25.2% PCE [@10.1002/adma.202505475]. They also include 1123 h on corrugated substrates with certified 28% PCE [@10.1021/jacs.5c13264], and 600 h on a 0.2 cm2 formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI3) cell [@10.1002/adma.202502142].

To resolve these contradictions, the field must measure how operational electric fields alter the kinetic activation barrier for ion migration across functional interfaces. This year's evidence could not support a rigorous comparison between two possibilities: chemical passivators may permanently suppress defect formation, or they may merely provide transient environmental encapsulation that masks accelerated internal field-induced breakdown. Current reporting cannot separate these mechanisms because studies evaluate stability through unencapsulated shelf-storage in dry air or dark nitrogen, while operational tracking also frequently omits temperature regulation, aperture masking, or reverse-bias characterization. Reporting that would have supported this comparison requires measuring the temperature-dependent activation energy of ionic conductivity using operando impedance spectroscopy under calibrated operating bias. That measurement must be paired with continuous MPPT under protocol ISOS-L-2 at 65 °C or 85 °C on designated apertures with declared geometric fill factors, alongside pre- and post-aging dark reverse-bias breakdown voltage sweeps. Quantifying ion migration activation energies directly against accredited T80 lifetimes under combined electrical and thermal stress is necessary to determine whether interfacial molecules permanently eliminate recombination centers or simply delay moisture degradation while introducing latent electronic instabilities.
