A sharp mechanistic division separates scalable perovskite deposition reports: at issue is whether film heterogeneity across large apertures is governed by fluid-phase drying and solvate-evaporation kinetics, or pre-emptively dictated by nanoscale molecular aggregation at the buried substrate. One body of work attributes macroscopic defect striations during blade or slot-die translation to convective transport and slow desolvation of high-boiling solvents [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c01538] [@10.1002/aenm.202500598] [@10.1126/science.adx9650]. The opposing body asserts that liquid drying reflects underlying contact topography. On that view, self-assembled monolayer (SAM) dewetting, micelle aggregation, and weak interfacial anchoring create lateral nucleation gradients that solvent tuning cannot resolve [@10.1002/anie.202512660] [@10.1002/aenm.202502639] [@10.1021/acsnano.5c07208]. This dispute persists because fluid-drying studies evaluate uncertified small-area cells (<0.1 cm2) without tracking lateral crystallization kinetics, whereas buried-contact studies rarely quantify ink shear forces during dynamic translation. Whether bulk evaporation or interfacial adhesion governs large-area film quality is therefore unresolved, and settling it requires synchronous operando grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS) and hyperspectral photoluminescence (PL) mapping across translating ink menisci under controlled shear rates, coupled to local adhesion profiling across decimetre substrates.

Within fluid-phase processing, conflicting strategies govern intermediate coordination complexes during the liquid-to-solid transition. One approach insists high-boiling polar solvents such as N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) must be expelled rapidly to avert defective solvates. Propionic acid weakens lead iodide (PbI2)-NMP coordination to accelerate evaporation in antisolvent-free coating, yielding an uncertified champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 25.86% on small cells [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c01538]. Volatile pyridine coordination purges solvent residues in narrow-bandgap tin-lead mini-modules to reach 22.0 +- 0.4% PCE across 10.4 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-025-64038-1]. Conversely, delaying desolvation is argued to ensure large-area uniformity. A ternary 2-methoxyethanol (2ME), dimethylformamide (DMF), and NMP system preserves intermediate coordination to retard premature crystallization, achieving a certified 18.73% PCE across 100.15 cm2 with 95.8% retention after 1,000 h at maximum power point (MPP) [@10.1002/aenm.202500598]. Thermal input presents an identical dilemma: traditional furnace annealing generates convective thermal gradients that precipitate secondary phases, whereas rapid laser annealing at 20 W/cm2 establishes a 123 +- 18 s degradation-free ambient processing window that suppresses 6H hexagonal phase accumulation, reaching 24.0% PCE over 100 cm2 [@10.1126/science.adx9650].

Additive sequencing and phase restoration present parallel conflicts between pre-deposition ink equilibria and post-deposition crystallization. Incorporating hexamethylphosphoramide (HMP) inverts the reaction sequence between formamidinium (FA+) and rubidium (Rb+) with the Pb-I framework during ambient blade coating (relative humidity (RH) <=55%), producing 25.8% PCE on an uncertified 0.09 cm2 cell [@10.1002/adma.202506291]. L-ascorbic acid sodium salt (L-AASS) complexation with PbI2 suppresses residual halide phases to deliver 25.23% PCE on a 65 cm2 module [@10.1021/acs.jpclett.5c01590]. However, relying on precursor additives risks phase segregation in multi-cation inks. In situ vapor post-treatment using formamidinium iodide (FAI) and methylammonium thiocyanate (MASCN) bypasses solution equilibria by converting residual PbI2 and delta-phase FAPbI3 into photoactive alpha-FAPbI3. That treatment reaches a certified 25.67% PCE (champion 26.05%, stabilized 25.41%) on small areas, 24.52% on 1 cm2, and 22.35% on a 17.1 cm2 mini-module [@10.1002/adma.202501057]. Compositional design during scalable coating shows that eliminating methylammonium (MA+) via formamidinium-cesium alloys (FACsPbI3) prevents volatile organic degradation [@10.1021/acsnano.4c18089]. Incorporating chlorine through PbCl2 or perovskite chloride additives into slot-die coated triple-halide wide-bandgap formulations widens the optical bandgap by tens of millielectronvolts, yet yields only 17.2% PCE on an uncertified 0.062 cm2 aperture [@10.1002/solr.202400750].

Interfacial defect arguments attribute scaling losses to morphological instabilities of hole-selective SAMs on metal oxides. As-prepared SAM solutions assemble into ~460 nm fibrous micellar aggregates that seed pinholes and potential fluctuations. Dispersing them with dipolar additives yields monodisperse layers, achieving an uncertified champion PCE of 26.58% (certified 25.81%) on 0.09 cm2 with T90 extrapolated to 11,259 h under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaics Stability (ISOS) ISOS-D-1 [@10.1002/anie.202512660]. In continuous blade coating over 10 cm x 10 cm substrates, 3-nitrobenzenesulfonic acid (3NS) co-adsorption enforces uniform SAM anchoring, yielding 20.16% PCE across a 63.09 cm2 module [@10.1002/aenm.202502639]. Co-assembly strategies similarly suppress aggregation: co-adsorbing salicylic acid (SA) with [4-(3,6-dimethyl-9H-carbazol-9-yl)butyl]phosphonic acid (Me-4PACz) fills pinholes to yield a certified 28.78% PCE (champion 28.94%) with 1.332 V open-circuit voltage (VOC) in a 0.087 cm2 wide-bandgap tandem cell [@10.1038/s41467-025-58111-y]. Co-assembling the co-adsorbent PDADI with [2-(3,6-dimethoxy-9H-carbazol-9-yl)ethyl]phosphonic acid (MeO-2PACz) avoids segregation to deliver a certified 22.09% PCE (champion 25.49%) on 8.5 cm2 [@10.1021/acsnano.5c07208]. Reinforced undecylphosphonic acid (UPA) anchors optimize energy alignment to attain 25.9% PCE [@10.1002/aenm.202502000]. Solid-vapor impregnation of the phosphonic acid derivative 4PADCB with glycine hydrochloride curbs SAM sublimation, yielding 19.18% PCE on a 5 cm x 5 cm module and 22.15% on 0.16 cm2 [@10.1021/acsnano.5c01479].

Interfacial mechanics further divides the field: chemical defect passivation fails if mechanical delamination occurs under thermal and scaling stress. In situ crosslinking of hexamethylene diisocyanate (HDI) from the perovskite ink with ethylene glycol (EG) on the electron transport layer (ETL) fastens the buried contact and relaxes tensile strain. The crosslinked stack yields 20.40% PCE on a 25 cm2 module alongside 26.04% on rigid and 24.16% on flexible small devices [@10.1002/adfm.202505921]. Potassium benzyl(trifluoro)borate (BnBF3K) linkers similarly enhance adhesive toughness at the tin oxide (SnO2)/perovskite interface, producing a certified 21.39% PCE (champion 24.15%) [@10.1002/adma.202419329]. Bilateral bond strength equilibrium through 2-(benzo[d]thiazol-2-ylthio)acetic acid (BTSA) on nickel oxide (NiOx) anchors sulfur, nitrogen, and aromatic rings, enabling a certified 26.65% PCE (champion 26.98%) across a 764 cm2 module [@10.1038/s41467-025-63389-z]. In n-i-p modules, anchoring SnO2 with methylglycinediacetic acid trisodium salt (MGDA·3Na) yields 23.27% PCE across 5 cm x 5 cm (26.43% on small cells) [@10.1038/s41467-025-66225-6]. Formamidinium salts at the buried interface instead assist charge extraction, reaching 21.72% PCE on an 18.55 cm2 mini-module (25.61% on single devices) [@10.1002/aenm.202501206]. Dual interface modification with Nd2O3 on SnO2 curbs lattice mismatch and ion migration to maintain 89% retention after 2,000 h continuous illumination, though restricted to 0.045 cm2 cells at 26.22% PCE [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c00074].

Module-level interconnects and transport layers introduce distinct failure modes. Slot-die coating of coordinated hole transport layers (HTLs) preserves conductivity across expanded areas, delivering 22.80% PCE at 23.25 cm2, 21.45% at 87.45 cm2, and 20.21% at 749.276 cm2 (certified 19.5% against a 26.02% cell at 0.0655 cm2). Those modules also pass module quality test (MQT) standards MQT 08, MQT 10, and MQT 13 under International Electrotechnical Commission (IEC) standard IEC 61215-2-2021 [@10.1038/s41467-025-64111-9]. Replacing hygroscopic lithium salts in spiro matrices with spiro-phenothiazine derivatives (PTZ-Fl) immobilizes mobile ions. That replacement delivers 22.1% PCE on a 5 cm x 5 cm mini-module (certified 25.2%, champion 25.8% on small cells) with >85% efficiency retained after 1,100 h under ISOS-D-1 [@10.1002/adma.202505475]. Fluorene-terminated spiro p-BM achieves 22.4% PCE on a 16 cm2 mini-module (25.5% on 0.0625 cm2) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.4c03233]. Light-accelerated oxidation of 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) via ammonium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (TFSI) salts eliminates mobile metal cations, yielding a certified 20.95% PCE on a 12.83 cm2 module with 7% degradation after 700 h unencapsulated operation [@10.1038/s41467-025-66752-2].

Top interfaces and grain boundaries govern remaining module voltage shortfalls. In situ grain boundary polymerization with dimethyl itaconate eliminates inter-grain gaps, enabling 30 cm x 30 cm modules to achieve 19.2% PCE (from an uncertified 25.9% small cell, certified at 25.24% on 0.09 cm2) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c01711]. Conjugated ionic additives in poly(triarylamine) (PTAA) passivate boundary traps to yield 22.96% PCE at 15.17 cm2 (champion cell 25.73%) [@10.1002/aenm.202504647]. Entropy-driven stabilization using 2-naphthalenethiol derivatives (2NTD) inhibits polyiodide defect formation to yield a certified 26.4% PCE (champion 26.63%) on 0.09 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-025-64728-w]. In situ grown two-dimensional (2D) CHEA2PbI4 capping layers on cesium lead triiodide (CsPbI3) absorbers align interfacial energy levels, delivering a certified 18.83% PCE (champion 19.32%) on a 12.44 cm2 mini-module [@10.1002/adfm.202423397]. Wafer-scale monolayer molybdenum disulfide (MoS2) buffers physically impede halide migration into transport contacts, retaining <5% PCE loss after 1,200 h damp heat (85 deg C and 85% RH) on 0.074 cm2 cells (certified 25.9%, champion 26.2%) [@10.1126/science.ado2351]. Radical molecular network-buffers limit VOC deficits to an energy loss of 0.297 eV, attaining 21.39% PCE on a 12.5 cm2 mini-module (25.28% on small cells) [@10.1002/adma.202417289]. Expanding chlorine-doped FAPbI3 absorbers on ultraviolet-insensitive SnO2 to a 16 cm2 photoelectrode platform demonstrates 140 h operation. That result confirms that large-area stability depends upon eliminating unreacted boundary defects rather than merely enlarging film dimensions [@10.1038/s41467-025-59597-2].
