The performance divergence between single junctions and tandems defines the first gap: the best certified perovskite/silicon tandem this month was 33.66% power conversion efficiency (PCE) [@10.1038/s41467-026-76266-0], while certified single junctions clustered near 27%, showing that the wide-bandgap (WBG) subcell limiting the tandem is not the one most papers optimise. While chemical strategies claim to homogenize mixed halides in pristine films [@10.1002/adma.74388] [@10.1002/aenm.71462], whether halide homogenisation in wide-bandgap absorbers suppresses phase segregation under operating bias remains unproven because photocarrier gradients trigger iodide clustering. Operando spatially resolved photoluminescence (PL) spectroscopy under continuous maximum power point tracking (MPPT) load at elevated temperatures would resolve whether compositional uniformity survives operational electric fields.

A second gap concerns buried-interface chemistry that survives thermal cycling rather than only lifting initial efficiency. Molecules and self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) passivate buried defects to lift open-circuit voltage (Voc) on pristine substrates [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3096] [@10.1002/adma.74438], but mismatched thermal expansion coefficients generate interfacial shear strain that induces delamination. Only 5 of 144 closely read papers reported a time to 80% of initial performance (T80) lifetime, the longest 1400 h [@10.1002/anie.6548693], short against a 25-year module warranty. In situ temperature-dependent adhesion testing with impedance spectroscopy during thermal cycling would reveal whether interfacial bonding survives thermomechanical stress.

A third gap lies in ion-migration timescales measured under load rather than in the dark. Passivation schemes report reduced ion drift [@10.1002/adfm.77637] [@10.1002/adfm.77855], yet extracting activation energies in the dark ignores how photogenerated carriers screen internal fields and accelerate vacancy migration under illumination. Operando transient ion-drift measurements under illumination during continuous MPPT would resolve ionic transit timescales and defect accumulation under load.

The fourth gap is whether passivation that lifts Voc on a 0.1 cm2 cell survives slot-die coating at module area. Only 27 of 531 works addressed scale-up at all, and the largest reported aperture was 61.2 cm2 [@10.1002/smll.75068], orders of magnitude below a commercial module. Additives that distribute evenly during spin coating aggregate under meniscus shear, creating non-uniform trap landscapes [@10.1002/adma.74469] [@10.1021/acsami.6c11722]. Operando PL imaging during slot-die coating would track drying kinetics and isolate spatial Voc losses. Standardizing stability assessments to International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocols under operational load and validating passivation across large-area deposition would make next month's literature considerably more useful.
