Operational degradation in inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) originates primarily from coupled interfacial redox reactions, lattice strain transitions, and electrode interdiffusion. At the buried hole-transport layer (HTL), acidic carbazole-based phosphonic acid self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) activate residual dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), driving iodide redox decomposition during wet processing [@10.1126/science.aeg8415]. Under subsequent light operation, this interfacial degradation cascades into the electron-transport layer (ETL): formamidinium iodide (FAI) photo-oxidation generates iodine radicals that induce phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) dimerization, while fullerene species accelerate FAI deprotonation in a self-reinforcing photochemical loop [@10.1002/anie.4221605]. Interfacial radical scavengers [@10.1002/anie.4221605], cross-linkable SAM linker architectures [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3096], and dipole-balancing molecular co-assemblies [@10.1038/s41467-026-76848-y] [@10.1002/adfm.77942] [@10.1002/adma.74438] mitigate these decomposition cascades. Nevertheless, stark discrepancies between uncertified champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) values and certified metrics, including a champion 27.06% PCE dropping to a certified 20.11% on small 0.09 cm2 apertures [@10.1002/adma.74438], reveal unresolved interfacial instability and measurement drift.

Within the perovskite absorber, vertical lattice heterogeneity accelerates lead and iodide vacancy formation under operating stress [@10.1002/adfm.77729]. Thermal and operational biases exacerbate internal lattice strain, promoting polymorphic degradation from the photoactive alpha phase to the non-photoactive delta phase; relieving this strain through interstitial incorporation of 2 mol% sodium [@10.1002/anie.2425823] or templating via two-dimensional (2D) intermediate phases [@10.1038/s41467-026-76425-3] stabilizes the active crystal lattice. At the rear contact, halide migration triggers severe metal interdiffusion, requiring diffusion barriers such as 5 nm chromium interlayers to prevent contact failure [@10.1002/aenm.71372] [@10.1002/adfm.77805].

A critical systemic weakness across these investigations is the severe lack of standardized aging protocols. Only 5 of 144 surveyed papers reported a time to 80% initial performance (T80) value: 1400 h [@10.1002/anie.6548693], 900 h [@10.1002/aenm.71514], 864 h [@10.1002/adfm.77805], 800 h [@10.1002/anie.2425823], and 720 h [@10.1002/solr.70454]. Concurrently, only 2 studies adhered to formal International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocols, specifically ISOS-L-1 [@10.1038/s41467-026-76848-y] and ISOS-L-2 [@10.1002/anie.4221605]. Most stability tests rely on unencapsulated continuous illumination without standardized maximum power point tracking (MPPT) [@10.1002/adma.74489]. Consequently, existing literature primarily probes short-term photochemical burn-in, obscuring critical field degradation pathways including reverse-bias breakdown, moisture-accelerated ion migration, and thermo-mechanical fatigue under diurnal operational cycling.
