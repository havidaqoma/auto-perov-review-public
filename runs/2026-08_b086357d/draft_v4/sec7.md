Interfacial chemical and photochemical degradation at selective contacts constitutes the primary failure mechanism addressed this month. Inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) suffer from coupled redox degradation: acidity in carbazole-based phosphonic acid self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) activates dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO)-mediated iodide redox reactions during ink deposition [@10.1126/science.aeg8415], while photo-oxidation of formamidinium iodide (FAI) yields iodine radicals that catalyze phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) dimerization alongside FAI deprotonation [@10.1002/anie.4221605]. Cross-linking SAM headers in situ [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3096], co-assembling binary monolayers [@10.1038/s41467-026-76848-y], or balancing dipole configurations [@10.1002/adfm.77942] [@10.1002/adma.74438] suppresses interfacial defect generation. Yet performance claims frequently exhibit severe certification discrepancies or rely on tiny device areas, such as a 27.06% uncertified champion dropping to 20.11% certified on 0.09 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.74438] and a certified 27.4% power conversion efficiency (PCE) tested on 0.06 cm2 [@10.1126/science.aeg8415].

In the bulk absorber, unrelaxed microstrain and defect formation accelerate phase degradation. Relieving lattice strain through 2 mol% sodium (Na+) interstitials [@10.1002/anie.2425823], two-dimensional (2D) intermediate phase templating [@10.1038/s41467-026-76425-3], or vertical lattice misalignment suppression [@10.1002/adfm.77729] increases defect formation energies for lead and iodide vacancies and prevents nonphotoactive phase transitions [@10.1002/anie.2425823] [@10.1038/s41467-026-76425-3] [@10.1002/adfm.77729]. At rear contacts, atomic interdiffusion is curtailed by inserting a 5 nm chromium (Cr) barrier beneath non-gold electrodes [@10.1002/aenm.71372] or using coordination modifiers to prevent direct perovskite contact with metal oxides [@10.1002/adfm.77805].

Standardized operational metrics remain conspicuously scarce. Across 144 examined papers, only 5 reported a time to 80% of initial efficiency (T80) and merely 2 adhered to International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocols, specifically ISOS-L-1 [@10.1038/s41467-026-76848-y] and ISOS-L-2 [@10.1002/anie.4221605]. Consequently, isolated metrics such as an 800 h T80 [@10.1002/anie.2425823] or an 864 h T80 on 0.08 cm2 [@10.1002/adfm.77805] reflect unstandardized evaluations rather than verified operational longevity.
