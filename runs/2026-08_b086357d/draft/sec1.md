Chemical coordination at buried contacts provides the most consistent mechanistic gains in perovskite photovoltaics, where Lewis bases and self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) coordinate undercoordinated lead species to suppress interfacial non-radiative recombination. However, the critical frontier has moved past unencapsulated power conversion efficiency (PCE) recorded on sub-millimetre laboratory substrates. The defining challenge has become threefold: establishing certified reproducibility, eliminating scaling losses across module-scale areas, and maintaining operational lifetime under continuous electrical and environmental bias. Without arresting lateral transport penalties, field-induced ion migration, and phase instability across expanded apertures, local passivation successes cannot deliver practical technology.
