Standard operational metrics fail to distinguish reversible ionic polarization from catastrophic degradation under electrical stress, where cells with poly(triarylamine) (PTAA) suffer irreversible breakdown under reverse current near maximum power point (MPP) while carbazole-based monolayers show recoverable decay [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00904]. This diagnostic ambiguity is exacerbated by unstandardized testing: only 6 of 167 closely read studies reported a time to 80% initial efficiency (T80), and only 5 specified International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocols [@10.1016/j.cej.2026.177834] [@10.1021/acsaem.6c00778] [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6596]. Unmonitored aging conceals whether decay originates from intrinsic soft lattice dynamics [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01303] or external weathering, where outdoor testing of a triple-junction cell showed efficiency losses to 80% at 4 months and 50% at 6 months [@10.1002/solr.70390]. Accelerated stress reproducing 20 months of outdoor operation demonstrates that varying light intensity and bias drives three coexisting, spatially non-uniform degradation modes: copper corrosion, edge patterns, and phase segregation [@10.1016/joule.2026.102538].

What survives this ambiguity is the mechanistic identification of non-recoverable interfacial failure. At electron-transport interfaces, phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) undergoes photo-induced [2+2] cycloaddition, causing dimerization and morphological instability that steric additives suppress in uncertified 26.6% efficiency devices [@10.1002/solr.70405]. At hole-transport interfaces, chemical degradation dominates: unanchored lithium ions migrate unless coordinated by sulfur centers, which preserve approximately 90% of initial efficiency across 800 h of MPP tracking (MPPT) under thermal and light stress, albeit on 0.04 cm2 areas [@10.1002/adfm.76257]. Concurrently, self-assembled monolayer (SAM) destabilization is prevented either by transient two-dimensional (2D) capping layers that scale to 22.34% efficiency across 615.7 cm2 modules or by hydroxylated MXene additives that strengthen SAM chemisorption on 0.0524 cm2 cells [@10.1002/adma.73561] [@10.1002/adma.73656].

Beyond interfaces, degradation is governed by deep-level rather than shallow defects: interstitial lead and formamidinium antisites dominate non-radiative losses despite concentrations three orders of magnitude lower than shallow states [@10.1002/adma.73679]. When exposed to operational heat, bulky surface passivators induce localized lattice expansion [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6596], whereas strain-regulating additives enable unencapsulated devices to retain 80.5% efficiency after 1000 h at 85°C under ISOS-D-2I and over 94.36% after 1500 h of MPPT under ISOS-L-1I [@10.1002/adfm.76756].
