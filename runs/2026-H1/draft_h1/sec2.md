A fundamental disagreement governs recent two-terminal (2T) tandem reports concerning the origin of non-radiative voltage deficits: while one investigation attributes performance limits to defective hole-extraction dynamics at the buried self-assembled monolayer (SAM) contact [@10.1126/science.aef5355], another concludes that phase segregation and deep trap formation within the bulk wide-bandgap perovskite dominate carrier recombination [@10.1038/s41467-026-73276-w]. Resolving this conflict requires operando absolute photoluminescence quantum yield (PLQY) imaging coupled with transient absorption spectroscopy under external bias, which can isolate interfacial recombination velocity from bulk quasi-Fermi level splitting collapse. These opposing mechanistic attributions yielded certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) values of 33.6% for contact optimization [@10.1126/science.aef5355] and 32.95% for bulk defect mitigation [@10.1038/s41467-026-73276-w]. Across the six-month period, independently certified 2T tandem efficiencies occupied a narrow corridor spanning 31.93% [@10.1016/j.esci.2026.100563] to 34.85% [@10.1002/adom.202503341], illustrating that divergent physical hypotheses are routinely invoked to explain marginal variations within the same narrow efficiency envelope.

Over the half year, the best certified PCE moved from 29.2% in January to 33.6% in June, representing a change of 4.4 percentage points. Rather than demonstrating an advancing technological frontier, this six-point monthly sequence reflects the stochastic noise of manuscript indexing schedules. The recorded progression of monthly certified peaks from 29.2% in January, to 32.46% in February [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04127], 31.93% in March [@10.1016/j.esci.2026.100563], 32.81% in April [@10.1002/adma.73106], 33.5% in May [@10.1038/s41467-026-72794-x], and 33.6% in June [@10.1126/science.aef5355] failed to establish a newly discovered physical regime. The highest independently certified efficiency of the entire half year, 34.85% [@10.1002/adom.202503341], appeared in March and exceeded every certified value reported in May and June. Consequently, the certified ceiling did not move outward, but repeated within established statistical bounds. Every leading certified result across this period was achieved on multi-junction devices, specifically 2T tandems, rather than single-junction absorbers. The verified boundary therefore reflects subcell current matching, optical coupling, and recombination-junction losses rather than fundamental breakthroughs in single-junction materials science.

A persistent gap separates uncertified claims from verified physical metrics: only 114 of 872 closely read papers reported an independently certified efficiency. In January, 15 papers reported certified values out of 475 indexed works, with the top self-reported efficiency of 31.85% standing near the certified 29.2%. In February, 17 papers reported certified values out of 499 indexed works, where self-reported metrics reached 32.73% alongside an independently certified 32.46% [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04127]. March included 14 certified papers among 559 indexed works, recording a best self-reported 32.43% against a certified 31.93% [@10.1016/j.esci.2026.100563]. In the second quarter, uncertified self-reported claims detached completely from verified performance, escalating to 41.6% in April across 589 indexed works (21 reporting certified values) and 44.36% in May across 583 indexed works (29 reporting certified values). By contrast, independently certified 2T tandem efficiencies converged into a narrow band, supported by shared interface passivation and additive treatments: April yielded certified values of 32.81% [@10.1002/adma.73106] and 32.45% [@10.1038/s41467-026-72160-x], while May produced a dense cluster with 33.5% [@10.1038/s41467-026-72794-x], 33.48% [@10.1126/sciadv.aec4431], 33.37% [@10.1002/aenm.71107], 33.04% [@10.1038/s41467-026-73656-2], and 32.1% [@10.1002/anie.5162616]. June recorded 14 certified reports among 623 indexed works, where self-reported metrics of 33.59% fell slightly below certified results at 33.6% [@10.1126/science.aef5355] and 32.95% [@10.1038/s41467-026-73276-w]. Independent certifications clustered tightly across these studies [@10.1038/s41467-026-72794-x] [@10.1126/sciadv.aec4431] [@10.1002/aenm.71107] [@10.1038/s41467-026-73656-2] [@10.1002/anie.5162616], confirming that uncertified claims above 40% arise from spectral mismatch errors or omitted aperture masking rather than verified material mechanisms.

The concentration of high efficiencies in small-area cells exposes the physical barrier of module scaling, where geometric parasitic losses depress performance. Across the half year, the share of closely read papers expanded from January (12.6%), February (15.0%), and March (16.7%) through April (18.8%), May (17.7%), and June (19.2%), reflecting increased publication activity around scalable manufacturing. However, active area expansion introduces severe lateral sheet resistance in transparent conductive oxide (TCO) electrodes, subcell interconnect dead zones, and non-uniform film drying, which systematically degrade the fill factor (FF). At 100 cm2, certified module efficiencies were limited to 26.59% [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238] and 24.7% [@10.1038/s41467-026-69685-6], where localized series resistance and laser-scribed interconnect defects constrain power extraction [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238] [@10.1038/s41467-026-69685-6]. At larger areas, efficiency diminished further to 27.12% across 655.2 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.73855], 26.62% across 651 cm2 [@10.1002/aenm.71111], and 23.3% across 694.5 cm2 [@10.1002/solr.70353]. At the extreme of reporting, a 900 cm2 device was presented without a certified efficiency [@10.1002/adfm.202532205]. These multi-junction and module data demonstrate that verified small-cell records above 33% fail to translate directly into scalable technology, where macroscopic transport resistance and deposition non-uniformities supersede nanometer-scale interface engineering.
