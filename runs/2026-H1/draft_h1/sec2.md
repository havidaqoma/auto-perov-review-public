Incompatible physical interpretations persist regarding whether open-circuit voltage (Voc) losses and operational decay in metal halide perovskites originate primarily from interfacial band misalignment or from unpassivated chemical defects. Proponents of electrostatic interface engineering argue that tuning interfacial dipole moments and work functions across hole-transport layer contacts eliminates non-radiative recombination and governs extraction kinetics [@10.1002/adma.202523338] [@10.1038/s41467-026-73426-0]. Conversely, coordination chemistry studies conclude that carrier losses are dominated by uncoordinated metal sites and localized phase segregation, which require multidentate Lewis-base chelators or redox mediators to stabilize the lattice [@10.1038/s41560-026-01993-z] [@10.1126/science.aeb7992]. Resolving this conflict requires operando bias-dependent drive-level capacitance profiling paired with depth-resolved photoluminescence quantum yield mapping under coupled electrical and thermal stress, isolating electrostatic potential steps from true defect deactivation. Interpreting performance metrics across different device categories requires establishing that a single-junction cell and a two-junction tandem are bounded by different physics, so a higher tandem number is not a better cell. The leading certified values belong to fundamentally different device architectures that divide the solar spectrum across distinct thermodynamic regimes rather than indicating superior absorber quality. In addition, a value certified on a square-centimetre cell and one certified at module area are different kinds of evidence, separated by interconnect resistance, geometric dead area, and spatial deposition non-uniformities.

For single-junction devices, the literature comprised 692 papers, but only 63 of them reported any certified value, demonstrating that uncertified laboratory measurements represent the vast majority of published claims. The certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) ceiling moved upward over the half year rather than repeating a static value. Certified single-junction PCE began at 27.1% in January [@10.1002/adma.202517596], registered 26.9% in February [@10.1038/s41560-026-01993-z], 26.78% in March [@10.1002/adma.202520432], and 27.03% in April [@10.1038/s41467-026-72097-1]. The ceiling reached a best certified 27.6% in May through entropy-regulating molecular locking of the crystal lattice [@10.1126/science.aeb9953], before recording 27.28% in June [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6596]. In contrast to efficiency gains, verified scaling and operational durability remained tightly constrained: the largest area verified was 4 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.73036] [@10.1021/acsami.5c23126], and the longest time to 80% of initial performance (T80) reached 4100 h [@10.1002/adfm.76110]. These values verify that high single-junction performance remains concentrated in small-area cells without standardized durability tracking.

Tandem architectures similarly verified moving performance ceilings, driven by distinct spectral-splitting mechanics. All-perovskite tandems were examined in 60 papers, with 15 of them reporting any certified value. Their certified ceiling moved across the period, recording 29.2% in January [@10.1038/s41467-025-68213-2], 20.02% in February [@10.1002/aenm.70755], 27.7% in March [@10.1038/s41467-026-70848-8], and advancing to a best certified 30.3% in April through chemical hardness synchronization [@10.1038/s41565-026-02165-6], followed by 29.44% in May [@10.1038/s41467-026-73210-0] and 28.79% in June [@10.1002/anie.4918900]. The largest area verified for all-perovskite tandems was 64.64 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-70848-8], while the longest T80 reached 1600 h [@10.1002/pip.70077], reflecting the persistent vulnerability of narrow-bandgap mixed tin-lead subcells to tin oxidation. Perovskite/silicon and other hybrid tandems encompassed 77 papers, with 19 of them reporting any certified value. Their certified ceiling moved upward from 28.25% in January [@10.1007/s40820-025-01962-3] to 32.46% in February [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04127], 31.93% in March [@10.1016/j.esci.2026.100563], 32.81% in April [@10.1002/adma.73106], 33.5% in May [@10.1038/s41467-026-72794-x], and reached a best certified 33.6% in June using an indium-free contact scheme [@10.1126/science.aef5355]. However, the largest verified area for hybrid tandems was 16 cm2 [@10.1016/j.solmat.2026.114223] and the longest T80 reached 1000 h [@10.1002/anie.5162616], demonstrating that hybrid records remain confined to small test apertures.

The module family totaled 43 papers, with 10 of them reporting any certified value, confirming that independent verification is exceptionally rare at large area. For monolithic perovskite modules, the certified ceiling repeated within a narrow efficiency band rather than establishing an upward trajectory. Certified monthly values tracked 25.62% in January [@10.1038/s41467-025-68231-0], 26.75% in February [@10.1002/anie.202525815], None in March, 25.55% in April [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238], and 26.64% in May [@10.1002/adma.73286], establishing a best certified module efficiency of 26.8% [@10.1002/adma.73855]. Although June recorded 32.95% [@10.1038/s41467-026-73276-w], that certification belonged to an integrated perovskite/silicon tandem cell architecture reported within a module study, not a monolithic perovskite module aperture, confirming that architectural categories cannot be combined into a single progression. The largest area verified across modules was 900 cm2 [@10.1002/adfm.202532205], and the longest T80 reached 1050 h [@10.1007/s40820-025-02058-8]. These metrics verify that module development remains governed by interconnect series resistance and film non-uniformity across large substrate areas rather than absorber efficiency limits.
