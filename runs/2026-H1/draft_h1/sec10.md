A fundamental disagreement in inverted perovskite solar cells (PSCs) concerns how self-assembled monolayer (SAM) hole-selective contacts degrade during operation: while one investigation attributes operational failure to out-of-plane molecular desorption from metal oxides that rigid planar conjugation suppresses [@10.1016/j.scib.2026.02.050], another concludes that degradation proceeds via field-induced lateral aggregation into isolated clusters [@10.1038/s41467-026-72097-1]. Resolving whether molecular detachment or lateral dewetting governs contact failure requires operando bias-dependent sum-frequency generation spectroscopy paired with in situ conductive atomic force microscopy under continuous maximum power point tracking (MPPT) at operational temperatures. Reconciling such conflicting degradation mechanisms across the literature is hindered by the scarcity of standardized testing protocols: when stability assessments rely on disparate stress regimes, distinguishing true interfacial stabilization from divergent testing conditions becomes impossible.

Across the six months, pooled reporting rates over 2238 indexed works reveal substantial deficits in primary performance and reliability metrics. Only 52.1% (1167 of 2238) of abstracts stated any power conversion efficiency (PCE) value. Independent verification was rare: only 8.5% (191 of 2238) reported an independently certified efficiency, leaving most performance claims susceptible to unverified spectral mismatch or contact overestimation. Stabilised performance tracking appeared in only 11.0% (246 of 2238) of abstracts, while reporting of current-voltage hysteresis was confined to 2.7% (60 of 2238), allowing capacitive transients and ion accumulation to obscure steady-state power output. Device geometry was similarly omitted, with only 8.4% (188 of 2238) stating the active area. Standardized operational aging was almost entirely absent, with only 1.7% (38 of 2238) identifying an International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol label. For a reader attempting cross-device comparisons, these low detection rates mean that nominally equivalent efficiencies often represent fundamentally incomparable physical measurements. These detection rates reflect automated parsing of published abstracts rather than an exhaustive audit of experimental methodologies; omitting a testing protocol or geometric parameter in an abstract does not establish that the underlying investigation failed to perform the measurement.

Examining monthly ranges demonstrates that reporting practices did not improve across the half year. Stating an efficiency sat flat within a narrow monthly range of 48.1% to 55.3%. Reporting of certified efficiencies oscillated between 5.9% and 11.6%, while stabilised efficiency remained confined to a tight monthly range of 9.2% to 13.5%. Active area disclosure sat flat between 5.8% and 10.3%, hysteresis identification stayed low within 1.5% to 4.3%, and ISOS labeling remained marginal between 0.6% and 2.7%. Although the monthly share of closely read papers expanded steadily from January (12.6%), February (15.0%), and March (16.7%) through April (18.8%), May (17.7%), and June (19.2%), this growing literature volume produced no upward drift in reporting completeness. Practice did not evolve; instead, reporting metrics occupied fixed corridors throughout the entire six months.

Exemplary reporting occurs where full geometric, certification, and operational parameters converge. Large-area module studies provide verified baselines by simultaneously stating aperture dimensions, certified efficiencies, and standardized degradation conditions, including an active area of 100 cm2 with a certified PCE of 25.55% under ISOS-L-1 tracking [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238] and 100 cm2 with a certified PCE of 23.55% under ISOS-D-1 storage [@10.1038/s41467-026-69685-6], establishing reproducible benchmarks for scalable interconnects [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238] [@10.1038/s41467-026-69685-6]. Standardized operational testing is also illustrated by small cells identifying an ISOS-L-2 protocol and certified PCE of 21.54% alongside a time to 80% of initial performance (T80) of 1210 h [@10.1038/s41467-026-72099-z], continuous operational stress under ISOS-L-3 [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c03999], or ISOS-L2 testing with a certified 26.78% PCE [@10.1002/adma.202520432], providing unambiguous tracking of photothermal decay [@10.1038/s41467-026-72099-z] [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c03999] [@10.1002/adma.202520432]. In sharp contrast, absent reporting obscures physical mechanisms. Extreme claims, such as an uncertified champion PCE of 28.49% [@10.1007/s40820-025-02006-6], omit certification, active area, and standardized aging protocols from the abstract. High champion efficiencies are frequently reported on sub-0.1 cm2 cells, such as 0.072 cm2 at 26.2% PCE [@10.1038/s41467-025-68207-0] or 0.058 cm2 at 26.87% PCE [@10.1002/aenm.70925], where lateral current collection and edge encapsulation distort planar transport physics [@10.1038/s41467-025-68207-0] [@10.1002/aenm.70925]. At the opposite extreme, scaling studies describe an aperture of 900 cm2 without providing an independently certified efficiency [@10.1002/adfm.202532205]. Uncertified claims evaluated on tiny cells without standardized protocols impede physical comparison and leave operational longevity unanchored.
