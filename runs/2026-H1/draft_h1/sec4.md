A fundamental contradiction defines recent investigations into carrier transfer across self-assembled monolayer (SAM) contacts at the buried interface of perovskite solar cells (PSCs). On one side, prevailing models treat hole extraction as a static electrostatic problem governed by ground-state vacuum work functions and interfacial dipole alignment [@10.1002/adfm.76291] [@10.1002/adma.202523338] [@10.1002/anie.8473744]. In this framework, introducing polar co-adsorbents such as cyanoacetic acid (CA) to raise the work function of [4-(3,6-dimethyl-9H-carbazol-9-yl)butyl]phosphonic acid (Me-4PACz) yields an uncertified champion power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 26.52% [@10.1002/adfm.76291], while assembling asymmetric Br-4PhCz monolayers with large dipole moments produces an open-circuit voltage ($V_\mathrm{oc}$) of 1.36 V in 1.77 eV wide-bandgap cells, delivering a certified tandem PCE of 28.98% (champion 29.35%) on a small 0.05 cm2 area [@10.1002/adma.202523338]. Similarly, terminal group tuning alone is reported to suppress non-radiative recombination to reach 95% and 91% of the Shockley-Queisser limit in 1.53 eV (uncertified champion PCE of 26.88%) and 1.72 eV devices [@10.1002/anie.8473744]. Conversely, direct operational interrogation reveals that charge transfer across SAM-modified interfaces is dynamic and governed by a self-limiting negative feedback loop [@10.1002/adfm.75295]. In this opposing mechanism, photo-injected holes accumulating at the buried contact establish a transient opposing electric field that induces rapid field saturation over short characteristic times, triggering a sharp decay in extraction efficacy regardless of favorable initial band offsets [@10.1002/adfm.75295]. These two positions cannot be reconciled: if transient space-charge accumulation dominates working conditions, static energy level optimization cannot prevent extraction decline under continuous illumination. Resolving this conflict requires operando, time-resolved Stark-shift spectroscopy or bias-dependent transient electro-absorption measurements under continuous illumination to determine whether static interfacial dipoles persist or undergo severe electrostatic screening during steady-state carrier flux.

Beyond dipolar tuning, modifying electronic coupling across buried hole transport layers (HTLs) has targeted both chemical linker architecture and non-radiative decay channels. Inserting thiophene rings into sulfur-modulated, fully conjugated monolayers (SMe-TPA-ThPA and SMe-TPA-PA) raises inverted device efficiencies from 24.31% in oxygen-linked controls to 26.52% and 25.40% by strengthening intermolecular pi-electron interactions and energetic alignment [@10.1002/anie.5319815]. Interfacial polarization can also be tuned through meta-substituted bifunctional molecules such as m-APCA, which suppresses SAM aggregation to achieve a certified 26.71% PCE (champion 26.77%) [@10.1002/adma.73306], or by introducing a 4-aminobutylphosphonic acid (4-ABPA) molecular bridge that limits interfacial voltage loss to 31 mV, yielding uncertified efficiencies of 25.56% in n-i-p and 26.45% in p-i-n configurations [@10.1002/adma.202519267]. Rather than relying solely on electrostatic barrier adjustment, resonant energy transfer channels have been established: incorporating the UV-312 molecule introduces a Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET) pathway that facilitates charge separation while suppressing ultraviolet-induced lead-iodide bond dissociation, achieving an uncertified 27.05% PCE with a $V_\mathrm{oc}$ of 1.186 V on a 0.09 cm2 aperture [@10.1002/anie.3596437]. However, the efficacy of these small-molecule modifiers depends entirely on substrate anchoring density and the absence of microscopic pinholes.

The second physical problem centers on anchoring density, molecular orientation, and phase segregation on rough or chemically heterogeneous substrates. Small-molecule phosphonic acids bind erratically to transparent conducting oxides because of non-uniform hydroxyl distributions. Over the past six months, synthetic strategies shifted from modifying underlying oxides toward catalytic assembly and macromolecular networks. Initial interventions altered substrate surface chemistry directly: depositing three atomic layers of aluminum oxide via atomic layer deposition (ALD) generates dense hydroxyl sites that align SAMs vertically, yielding an uncertified 26.63% PCE on 0.09 cm2 and 22.97% on a 14.40 cm2 mini-module [@10.1021/acsami.6c03372]. In two-terminal perovskite/silicon tandems, incorporating carbon into polysilicon tunnel recombination layers elevates surface hydroxyl density to anchor SAMs securely, producing a certified 33.5% tandem PCE (champion 33.84%) on 1 cm2 with a T80 lifetime of 800 h [@10.1038/s41467-026-72794-x]. Similarly, an annealed Ti3C2Tx MXene interlayer forms tridentate Ti-O-P bonds with phosphonic acid heads on indium tin oxide (ITO), attaining a certified 26.44% PCE (champion 26.78%) on a 0.08 cm2 area [@10.1002/aenm.70966]. Bypassing substrate pre-treatments, introducing 4-dimethylaminopyridine (DMAP) as a solution catalyst accelerates phosphonic acid condensation via a transient phosphorylpyridinium intermediate, densifying the SAM to achieve a certified 26.44% PCE (champion 26.93%) with 95% retention after 700 h [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01257]. Where pinholes persist on nickel oxide (NiOx), area-selective ALD of AlOx passivates exposed oxide regions without coating SAM-covered domains, enabling a 64.68 cm2 module PCE of 20.88% (champion 26.41%) [@10.1002/anie.202516537], while treating NiOx with cesium oxalate (CsOA) converts Ni3+ into [Ni(C2O4)3]3- and eliminates Ni4+ sites, supporting an 11.0 cm2 device PCE of 26.48% with 85.7% retention after 1560 h at 65 °C [@10.1002/anie.4012708].

Molecular shape has likewise been manipulated to regulate self-assembly thermodynamics. Incorporating dimethyl substituents onto spirofluorene cores imposes steric hindrance to force ordered molecular packing, yielding an uncertified champion PCE of 26.62% that maintains approximately 90% of its initial value after 1000 h of maximum power point tracking (MPPT) in nitrogen [@10.1002/aenm.71005]. Bisphosphonate anchors in MeS-BPADCP enforce vertical orientation through bidentate binding, reaching an uncertified 26.73% PCE with 94.2% retention after 1000 h under one-sun illumination [@10.1002/aenm.70814]. A butterfly-shaped dibenzazepine core (PATDBAz) enables pi-pi stacking while preventing aggregation, sustaining 97.5% of initial efficiency after 1000 h under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) ISOS-L-1 testing (champion 26.47%) [@10.1002/smll.74278]. Intermolecular ordering can also be modulated by doping mixed SAMs to alleviate tensile interfacial strain, achieving a tandem champion PCE of 30.18% and an inverted 1.68 eV cell PCE of 23.48% (>90% retention after 1500 h) [@10.1002/smll.74319], or by post-assembling MeO-ADCP onto pre-formed RS-2 monolayers to fill bare vacancies, achieving an uncertified 26.8% PCE with over 99% retention after 2000 h at 45 °C [@10.1021/acs.jpclett.6c01042]. Despite these ordering schemes, monomeric SAMs exhibit weak shear resistance and detach under thermomechanical stress. Consequently, research shifted toward polymeric networks. Replacing small molecules with an asymmetric polycarbazole phosphonic acid (Poly-3Ph-4PACz) homogenizes flexible hole-selective contacts, yielding a certified tandem PCE of 32.95% (champion 26.13%, stabilised 27.12%) and a 57.6 cm2 flexible module PCE of 22.22% [@10.1038/s41467-026-73276-w]. Multidentate phosphonic acid polymer networks (P-SAM) increase interfacial fracture toughness nearly eightfold to 9.11 MPa (certified 26.61% on 0.06734 cm2) [@10.1002/aenm.71159], while the phosphonic-acid-functionalized polymer PTPP combines continuous film formation with anchoring stability, sustaining 94.4% of initial performance after 800 thermal cycles (certified 26.4%, champion 27.1%) [@10.1002/adfm.75697]. In tin-lead absorbers, blending hydrophobic trifluoromethyl molecules (MTFP) into poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) suppresses moisture uptake to reach 23.36% PCE ($V_\mathrm{oc}$ of 0.88 V) and a tandem PCE of 28.67% [@10.1002/aenm.70910].

The third physical problem involves chemical coordination across the buried interface, which dictates heterogeneous nucleation, trap passivation, and ion migration. Buried contact chemistry directly regulates crystallization kinetics: imidazolium-functionalized SAMs guide crystal growth through pi-cooperative coordination with lead polyhalide complexes, retaining 93.8% of initial PCE after 936 h of operation at the maximum power point (champion 26.83%) [@10.1002/adma.73828]. On flexible substrates where poor precursor wetting impedes grain formation, modifying the SAM with NCGCl salt utilizes nitrile and guanidinium groups to promote heterogeneous nucleation and passivate defects, achieving a certified 26.64% PCE (champion 26.89%) [@10.1002/adma.73358]. Precursor pre-organization before coating also restricts phase competition: the zwitterionic additive CPTAC associates with formamidinium cations, contracting competing crystallization pathways to accelerate conversion into the photoactive alpha phase (uncertified 26.54% PCE on 0.1 cm2) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01291].

Passivating deep defects at the buried interface has converged on chelating agents, heterojunction formation, and diffusion barriers. Inserting an aromatic amino acid derivative (NBP) between Me-4PACz and wide-bandgap absorbers coordinates unbonded Pb2+ cations and passivates iodide vacancies, enabling wide-bandgap single junctions to reach 20.81% PCE with a $V_\mathrm{oc}$ of 1.371 V and tandems to reach 29.05% [@10.1002/smll.73595]. Bifunctional cyanoacetic acid incorporated into both the SAM and perovskite precursor passivates interface boundaries, suppresses ion migration, and nearly doubles the reverse-bias breakdown voltage, achieving a tandem champion of 32.43% (stabilised 24.43%) [@10.1002/adma.72738]. In situ growth of gradient Ruddlesden-Popper two-dimensional/three-dimensional (2D/3D) heterostructures suppresses interface defects, reducing tandem current mismatch from 5.5% to 0.1% (certified 32.46%, champion 32.73%) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04127], while conjugated TzTzI ligands construct 2D/3D networks that yield 24.5% on 1 cm2, 20.61% for 1.72 eV bandgaps, and 22.14% in mini-modules (champion 26.61%) [@10.1002/adma.202520251]. Multisite defect healing with thiazolidine derivatives (TZCl-CN) suppresses non-radiative recombination to reach an uncertified 26.39% PCE [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c03328]. Bypassing HTLs entirely, bimolecular co-modification with THDPH and PEABr manages the perovskite/electrode boundary, retaining 90% of initial efficiency after 1000 h (champion 26.55%) [@10.1002/adfm.76198]. To prevent lead leakage, polymerizing the crown ether DVB18C6 in situ yields a 95.14% Pb2+ sequestration efficiency (champion 26.78%) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00703]. Ultrathin inorganic interlayers provide physical barriers to ion migration: ALD HfOx layers anchor 3F-PEAI via Hf-F interactions to block halide and silver diffusion (certified 26.6%, champion 27.1%) [@10.1126/science.aea3339], paralleling SAM architectures that lose only 2% efficiency after 2100 h of MPPT at 65 °C under ISOS-L-2 conditions (certified 26.90%, champion 27.10%) [@10.1038/s41467-026-73426-0].

Buried electron transport layers (ETLs) present analogous chemical and electrostatic challenges. Dual passivation combining DMDPTF with cobalt-acetate-modified tin oxide (SnO2) produces an uncertified 26.41% PCE on 0.096 cm2 and 22.18% on a 10.04 cm2 module [@10.1002/smll.73891]. Reconstructing SnO2 nanocrystals with in situ generated [Al(OH)4]- ions rebuilds the interfacial electrical double layer, delivering a certified 26.64% PCE (champion 26.7%) and 21.91% on a 65 cm2 module [@10.1002/adma.73286]. For indoor applications, Mg-doping of SnO2 widens the optical bandgap and reduces recombination to yield 35.54% under light-emitting diode (LED) illumination and 20.28% under one sun [@10.1002/smll.202512808], while dual insulator contacts block shunting pathways to deliver an indoor PCE of 44.36% at 1000 lx and 43.08% at 600 lx ($V_\mathrm{oc}$ of 1.064 V, fill factor of 84.52%) [@10.1007/s40820-026-02225-5].
