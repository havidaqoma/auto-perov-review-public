Investigations into formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI3) phase stabilization reach conflicting conclusions on how low-dimensional templates suppress the alpha-to-delta transition. Contact-triggered cationic interaction (CCI) asserts that structural refinement, cation homogenization, and defect suppression proceed via non-destructive physical contact between separately crystallized two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) perovskites without chemical mixing, interdiffusion, or permanent bonding (certified 25.61% power conversion efficiency (PCE), champion 26.25%) [@10.1038/s41560-026-02027-4]. Conversely, formamidinium tetrafluoroborate (FABF4) recrystallization demonstrates that constructing stable mixed-dimensional networks requires active formamidinium (FA+)/methylammonium (MA+) cation exchange and Ostwald ripening rather than passive contact, achieving an uncertified 21.36% champion PCE that retains 88% of initial efficiency after 10,000 bending cycles in nitrogen and 82% at 60% ± 5% relative humidity (RH) [@10.1002/adfm.75673]. This conflicts with crystallographic interface models where the alpha-to-delta transition nucleates epitaxially at a tensilely strained, coherent interface between alpha-phase (011) and delta-phase (110) planes, demanding 2D perovskitoid interfaces to disrupt delta-phase orientation (uncertified champion 25.61% PCE) [@10.1038/s41467-026-73739-0]. Resolving whether stabilization requires passive contact, active cation exchange, or orientation disruption demands operando cross-sectional depth profiling (such as cryogenic secondary ion mass spectrometry) paired with transmission electron microscopy under thermal bias to establish whether bulky A-site cations stay segregated or interdiffuse across the interface.

Precursor stabilization strategies over the past six months divide over whether failure originates from proton transfer or physical volatility. Competitive proton-transfer suppression using methyl trifluoroacetate prevents FA+ deprotonation to extend precursor shelf life beyond 22 days, producing uncertified champion devices reaching 26.35% PCE that retain 94.75% efficiency after 1464 h of humidity exposure [@10.1038/s41467-026-73620-0]. In single crystals, citric acid similarly mitigates cation deprotonation, scavenges oxidized iodine (I2/I3-), and passivates lead (Pb2+) dangling bonds to yield 23.39% PCE [@10.1002/aenm.70939], while multidentate ZL1 anchors suppress volatile FA loss to lift PCE from 24.20% to 26.13% [@10.1007/s40820-026-02098-8]. In contrast, studies on volatile additives demonstrate that cation volatility dictates structural evolution: in thiocyanate systems, ammonium (NH4+) evaporates completely during annealing to drive bulk crystallization, whereas non-volatile phenylethylammonium (PEA+) segregates to grain boundaries for interfacial passivation (23.3% PCE) [@10.1021/acsaem.6c01329]. In cesium lead triiodide (CsPbI3), formamidinium acetate (FAAC) intermediate engineering lowers Cs4PbI6 formation energy, where transient FA+ intercalation into DMAPbI3 accelerates dimethylammonium (DMA+)/cesium (Cs+) exchange and dimethylammonium iodide (DMAI) volatilization (21.84% PCE) [@10.1021/acs.jpclett.6c01395]. Conversely, ethyl thioacetate post-treatment bypasses cation exchange to passivate uncoordinated Pb2+ through Lewis base coordination (17.07% PCE; T80 of 1360 h) [@10.1021/acsaem.5c04098]. Rubidium chloride (RbCl) guides phase transformations in printable mesoscopic methylammonium lead triiodide (MAPbI3), though performance is constrained to 18.48% PCE with only 300 s of steady-state tracking [@10.1002/solr.70363]. Machine learning via LEAP couples large language models with active learning to prioritize 6-CDQ and 2-CNA additives, lifting average PCE from a 19.25% control to 20.13% and 20.87% (21.32% champion) [@10.48550/arxiv.2605.20242], while vacuum-assisted urea coordination modulates triple-cation crystallization to reach 19.61% PCE [@10.1002/solr.202500906].

Lattice microstrain from A-site mismatch divides stabilization strategies between static coordination and dynamic mechanical regulation. Static passivators such as guanidinium methanesulfonate (GUAMS) bridge FA+ via hydrogen bonding while passivating Pb2+, achieving 26.11% PCE in inverted cells, 24.49% on flexible substrates, and a T80 of 1200 h [@10.1002/adfm.76197]. Zwitterionic Cl-PPS additives identically coordinate Pb2+ and hydrogen-bond with FA+ to reach 26.61% PCE [@10.1002/aenm.71105]. In n-i-p devices, thiacetazone yields 26.52% PCE in ambient air and 23.24% at 70% RH [@10.1002/adma.202523630], while fluoropyridine additives (DFPy) suppress non-radiative recombination to reach 26% PCE [@10.1002/adfm.202526679]. Because static bonds cannot accommodate cyclic thermal expansion, dynamic strain regulation via thermal disulfide ring-opening crosslinks grain boundaries, maintaining 95.7% of initial efficiency after 1,800 h under maximum power point tracking (MPPT) with day-night cycling (26.5% champion PCE) [@10.1038/s41467-026-70697-5]. Fluorinated misplaced-dipole elastomers similarly suppress thermal stress cracks, yielding 25.54% PCE on flexible devices versus 26.83% on rigid substrates [@10.1038/s41467-026-74285-5]. At module scale, out-of-plane strain homogenization through A-site doping and an in situ 2D stress-relief layer delivers a certified 25.55% PCE (26.59% champion) across 100 cm2 under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS)-L-1 testing [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238]. Solvent-free dry vacuum deposition independently demonstrates that minimizing microstrain yields a standard deviation of 0.36% across 90 cells (25.4% champion PCE) [@10.1002/cey2.70236].

In wide-bandgap (WBG) mixed-halide perovskites, light-induced halide segregation divides mechanistic interpretations between differential crystallization kinetics and lattice microstrain. Eutectic urea phosphate demonstrates that halide demixing stems from unequal growth kinetics; stronger affinity for bromine-based over iodine-based octahedra synchronizes crystallization, eliminating vertical and lateral phase segregation (22.27% PCE) [@10.1002/anie.2535666]. TTA additives corroborate this kinetic synchronization mechanism, achieving 20.55% PCE and an open-circuit voltage (Voc) of 1.339 V [@10.1002/smll.202513111]. In contrast, methylammonium-guanidinium (MA-GA) codoping demonstrates that balancing lattice strain directly stabilizes the lattice against halide demixing without kinetic agents, retaining over 95% of initial efficiency after 1400 h of continuous operation in 1.77 eV cells (21.5% PCE) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00328]. Halide substitution also dictates transport limits in thick absorbers: in Cs0.05(FA0.90MA0.10)0.95Pb(I0.95Br0.05)3 middle cells, performance drops above 450 nm, but chloride substitution permits thickness scaling up to ~700 nm without sacrificing fill factor (FF) or Voc (23.4% PCE on 1 cm2) [@10.1002/solr.70410]. Functional group-modulated benzoic additives suppress non-radiative recombination in WBG single junctions to exceed 22.9% PCE, enabling 2-terminal tandem cells surpassing 31% [@10.1002/adfm.75640]. Multifunctional ATAA molecules stabilize both FA+/MA+ cations and I-/Br- halides via hydrogen bonding, upshifting the valence band maximum (VBM) by 0.12 eV (25.82% PCE on 1 cm2) [@10.1002/adfm.202524302], while dual A-site 1D (PDA)0.6(GUA)1.4Pb6I16 bridges suppress iodide migration and interfacial recombination, attaining 20.46% PCE (Voc 1.328 V) in WBG cells and 28.43% in all-perovskite tandems with a T80 of 1000 h [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04243].

Low-dimensional heterojunctions introduce fundamental trade-offs between surface passivation and transport resistance. Precursor incorporation of long-chain oleylammonium ligands forms in situ 2D/3D heterojunctions yielding 26.22% PCE on small areas, 24.6% on 1 cm2, and 21.1% on 6.8 cm2 mini-modules (T80 of 1050 h) [@10.1007/s40820-025-02058-8]. Non-Dion-Jacobson (non-DJ) 2D perovskites using 3,3'-methylenediphenyldiammonium cations utilize organic bilayer pi-pi interactions to achieve 26.52% PCE on 50 cm2 modules [@10.1002/anie.2974414]. At single-crystal interfaces, layered 1D (4PPY)PbI3 blocking layers suppress iodide migration more effectively than isolated 1D or 2D phases, retaining 100% of initial efficiency after 1032 h continuous illumination (25.4% PCE) [@10.1002/adfm.75386]. Alkyl chain optimization with tetrahexylammonium iodide (THAI) generates 1D THAPbI3 heterojunctions that anchor FA+ via hydrogen bonding, suppressing outward cation diffusion and strain gradients (25.06% PCE) [@10.1021/acsaem.6c01363]. Grain-boundary encapsulation via 4-FAPyCl2 in PbI2 precursors forms quasi-2D coatings that protect 3D alpha-FAPbI3, limiting ambient degradation to less than 3% PCE loss (25.32% PCE) [@10.1002/adfm.202531852]. Precursor addition of 0.3% MeO-PEACl raises Voc from 1.11 to 1.16 V and PCE from 21.1% to 23.1%, maintaining the black phase after 3380 h at 65% ± 10% RH [@10.1002/smll.73351]. To overcome spacer insulation, thiophene-2-hydrazide (ThCH) induces interlayer orbital coupling in 2D Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) phases, though its certified 21.74% efficiency (22.41% champion) was evaluated on a diminutive 0.074 cm2 aperture [@10.1002/anie.4293157]. Pre-formed n=1 2D nanosheets seed 3D oriented growth to reach 21.36% PCE (20.75% stabilized output) with hysteresis below 1% [@10.1002/smll.74076], and poly(3-hexylthiophene) (P3HT)-modified 2D quantum dots suppress interfacial defects to reach 25.04% PCE [@10.1021/acsami.5c24915]. Isomeric cytosine additives outperform iso-cytosine through delocalized bonding in quasi-2D films (22.4% PCE; T80 of 3600 h) [@10.1021/acs.jpclett.6c01531], and propylammonium iodide in two-step blade coating directs (111) grain orientation in inverted cells (20.05% PCE; 95% retention after 1680 h MPPT) [@10.1002/solr.70385]. Conversely, unoptimized capping layers such as 2D (4-FBA)2PbBr4 yield an uncertified 18.4% PCE and retain only 77.82% after 10 h at 80 °C and 55.32% after 30 days in ambient air [@10.1021/acsaem.6c00285], while vacuum co-evaporated PEA-passivated films reach 17.79% PCE [@10.1002/ente.202502217].
