The primary mechanistic disagreement across monolithic hybrid tandems centers on the molecular packing and structural order of the buried hole-selective layer (HSL) at the interconnection junction. One camp contends that dense intermolecular pi-pi stacking and strict crystalline order within self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) are mandatory. On that account, crystalline order eliminates pinholes, establishes favorable interfacial band alignment, and accelerates hole extraction, securing an independently certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 32.45% (33.4% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72160-x]. In direct conflict, another study demonstrates that crystalline order in hole-selective monolayers precipitates nanoscale domain boundaries, induces molecular aggregation, and accelerates thermal dewetting [@10.1002/adma.73106]. That group instead establishes an amorphous and asymmetric molecular geometry to suppress phase separation and maintain conformal passivation, yielding a certified PCE of 32.81% and the family-record self-reported champion PCE of 34.02% [@10.1002/adma.73106]. Settling whether long-range crystalline coherence or steric amorphous disorder minimizes interfacial trap-assisted recombination requires two probes applied together: operando conductive atomic force microscopy (c-AFM) and temperature-dependent grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS). Running both under electrical bias and thermal stress would correlate nanoscale current extraction with molecular reorientation. Between these extremes, intermediate chemical strategies attempt to stabilize the buried junction. Sterically extended asymmetric conjugated hole-selective layers achieved a certified PCE of 33.04%, although the champion cell dropped to 24.05%, with operational durability tracked under the International Summit on Organic Photovoltaics Stability (ISOS) protocol ISOS-L-1 [@10.1038/s41467-026-73656-2]. Molecular pinning in composite SAMs on tunnel oxide passivated contact (TOPCon) silicon cells attained a certified PCE of 32.1% (32.26% champion, 1 cm2) alongside an operational lifetime to 80% of initial performance (T80) of 1000 h [@10.1002/anie.5162616]. Siloxane-mediated intermolecular interactions in co-assembled monolayers yielded a certified PCE of 33.37% (33.74% champion) [@10.1002/aenm.71107], and carbon-incorporated polysilicon interconnection layers achieved a certified PCE of 33.5% (33.84% champion, 1 cm2) with an 800 h T80 [@10.1038/s41467-026-72794-x]. Four-terminal tandem antireflection and SAM anchoring reached an uncertified 31.25% champion on 1 cm2 [@10.1002/aenm.71036]. Dimeric SAMs reached 26.09% certified (26.63% champion) [@10.1002/anie.5905435]. Dopant-free ultra-thin 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD) in an n-i-p architecture yielded 28.25% certified (29.73% champion) on a 0.135 cm2 aperture [@10.1007/s40820-025-01962-3].

Interconnection layer engineering exposes a parallel trade-off between optical parasitics, sputter damage, and series resistance. The single best certified device across the entire hybrid family reached 33.6% PCE (33.2% champion, 1 cm2) by discarding sputtered transparent conducting oxides entirely, reporting: "For RPD-SnO x as the recombination layer, we achieved a certified efficiency of 33.6%" [@10.1126/science.aef5355]. By depositing reactive plasma deposition tin oxide (RPD-SnOx) as both the recombination junction and transparent electrode, this architecture eliminates sub-bandgap absorption from indium tin oxide (ITO) and prevents high-energy ion bombardment damage to the underlying contact [@10.1126/science.aef5355]. In contrast, sputtered ITO remains ubiquitous despite persistent energetic penalties. Conformal ultrathin ITO interconnects yielded an uncertified 31.8% champion PCE [@10.1002/adfm.75311], and ultrathin electron-selective contacts yielded an uncertified 31.7% champion PCE [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04082]. Heated ITO deposition on poly-crystalline silicon (poly-Si) passivated contacts enabled monolithic integration on a 16 cm2 aperture, the largest physical area reported in this family, yet efficiency fell to an uncertified 22.1% champion [@10.1016/j.solmat.2026.114223]. Spatially resolved electrical characterization reveals that lateral sheet resistance across the interconnect and localized contact non-uniformities impose acute fill factor (FF) penalties, capping unoptimized monolithic output at an uncertified 29% champion PCE [@10.1002/smll.202513958]. While scalable deposition reviews compile certified efficiencies reaching 34.85% [@10.1002/adom.202503341], experimental monolithic scaling beyond 1 cm2 remains bottlenecked by interfacial resistive and optical losses.

In the wide-bandgap (1.67 to 1.83 eV) perovskite top cell, open-circuit voltage (Voc) deficits caused by phase segregation and defect-mediated recombination represent the primary thermodynamic loss channel. Across the 77 papers in this family, the dominant physical mechanisms addressed are architecture (46 papers) and defects (12 papers), followed by interfaces (9 papers) and stability (4 papers). Regulating preferred crystal facets of the wide-bandgap absorber minimized the Voc deficit to deliver a certified PCE of 31.93% (32.17% champion, 1.0 cm2), matching the median certified efficiency of the family [@10.1016/j.esci.2026.100563]. Controlling passivator intermolecular interactions to modulate surface energetics attained a certified PCE of 33.48% (33.63% champion, 1 cm2) [@10.1126/sciadv.aec4431], and targeted enhancement of passivator coordination activity enabled an open-circuit voltage of 2.00 V to reach a certified PCE of 31.52% (32.74% champion) [@10.1002/aenm.71077]. At the buried interface, in situ engineered gradient Ruddlesden-Popper (RP) 2D/3D heterostructures suppressed non-radiative recombination to produce a certified PCE of 32.46% (32.73% champion) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04127]. Uncertified champion claims addressed defect passivation and crystallographic control. Bifunctional cyanoacetic acid interface-bulk modulation achieved 32.43% [@10.1002/adma.72738], and synchronous interfacial and crystallographic engineering yielded 32.15% [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00166]. Grain boundary stabilization reported a 32.19% champion despite certifying at only 23.48% [@10.1002/adma.202521129]. In parallel, theoretical bromine and cesium substitution in formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI3) calculated a 32.1% champion potential for perovskite/TOPCon tandems [@10.26434/chemrxiv.15001694/v1]. Surface reconstruction through phenylethylammonium chloride (PEACl) modification and solvent etching reached 31.85% [@10.1002/aenm.202506619], imide passivation achieved 31.66% [@10.1002/aenm.70902], and volatile fluorination additives yielded 31.43% [@10.1002/adfm.202526009]. Pathway priming of intermediate phases in formamidinium cesium (FACs) absorbers reached 31.04% on 1.0 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-72094-4]. Simulated low-deficit wide-bandgap tandems claimed 30.97% against a certified baseline of 23.42% [@10.1007/s40820-025-01959-y]. Electron-deficient amine hydrogen bonding yielded 26.76% [@10.1002/anie.9708831], and crystallization modulation with defect passivation achieved 20.4% [@10.1038/s41467-025-68125-1].

Deposition pathways across the six-month window increasingly diversified to eliminate solvent-induced degradation on textured bottom substrates. Bandgap-tunable two-step vapor deposition produced monolithic perovskite/silicon tandems with an uncertified 29% champion PCE [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00156]. Solvent-free close-space sublimation (CSS) demonstrated conformal coverage over textured silicon wafers, achieving an uncertified 24.3% champion PCE [@10.1038/s41560-026-02068-9], while rapid laser-based deposition of lead iodide and cesium bromide (PbI2/CsBr) inorganic scaffolds enabled hybrid fabrication across 1 cm2 [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04010]. Expanding multijunction architectures, chloride substitution enabled thick perovskite middle absorbers in monolithic perovskite/perovskite/silicon triple-junction cells evaluated on 1 cm2 [@10.1002/solr.70410].

Hybrid tandems also incorporate non-silicon bottom partners, specifically organic bulk heterojunctions (BHJs), copper indium gallium selenide (CIGS), cadmium telluride (CdTe), and lead sulfide (PbS) colloidal quantum dots. These alternatives target mechanical flexibility, lightweight form factors, high-throughput roll-to-roll processing, and infrared photon harvesting. However, these benefits come at substantial physical cost: severe photovoltage deficits, non-radiative recombination at rough chalcogenide interfaces, narrow thermal stability windows in organic acceptors, and large conduction band offsets. In perovskite/organic tandems, localized 2D/3D heterojunctions enhanced photovoltage to yield a certified PCE of 26.3% (27.11% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-68904-4], absorption tuning and energy loss minimization achieved 25.56% certified (26.49% champion) [@10.1007/s40820-025-02037-z], and blade-coated wide-bandgap perovskites addressing halide ion homogeneity reached 25.6% certified (26.3% champion) [@10.1126/sciadv.aed2200]. Aggregation-induced emission luminogens incorporated into ternary organic bulk heterojunctions yielded a certified PCE of 25.8% (26.5% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-73743-4]. Uncertified configurations exhibited matching ceilings. Synergistic hole-selective contacts achieved 26.06% [@10.1002/aenm.70945], gradient-regulated crystallization reached 25.91% [@10.1002/aenm.71115], and defect passivation of 1.83 eV perovskites on flexible substrates yielded 22.44% [@10.1002/inf2.70132]. For chalcopyrite partners, coevaporated monolithic perovskite/CIGS tandems reached a certified PCE of 24.5% on 1 cm2 [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04203]. Oriented crystal growth on flexible CIGS achieved an uncertified 25.02% champion PCE [@10.1002/aenm.71102]. Four-terminal tandems coupling halide-tuned cesium lead mixed halide (CsPbX3) top cells with CdTe bottom partners achieved an uncertified 25.13% champion PCE [@10.1021/acsaem.6c00408]. Sulfur-supplemented PbS quantum dot cells reached an uncertified 14.34% champion PCE [@10.1002/adfm.75664].

Weighing these metrics against other photovoltaic classes reveals severe reporting discrepancies across the hybrid tandem family. Of the 77 papers published in this family over six months, only 24.7% (19 papers) reported an independently certified efficiency, with 11 papers excluded from empirical tallies as simulations or reviews. Active device area was documented in only 15.6% of publications, reaching a maximum of 16 cm2 [@10.1016/j.solmat.2026.114223]. Champion devices remain overwhelmingly restricted to 1 cm2 or sub-0.15 cm2 apertures. Operational durability reporting is exceptionally sparse: only 2.6% of papers reported a T80 metric, with the longest reaching 1000 h [@10.1002/anie.5162616]. Only a single paper (1.3%) specified a standard testing protocol (ISOS-L-1) [@10.1038/s41467-026-73656-2]. By comparison, single-junction devices (692 papers) demonstrated certified efficiencies up to 27.6% (63 certified) with operational stability reaching 4100 h on 4 cm2, and all-perovskite tandems (60 papers) reached 30.3% certified (15 certified) across areas up to 64.64 cm2 and 1600 h T80. Perovskite modules (43 papers) delivered 26.8% certified (10 certified) across 900 cm2 with 1050 h T80. Perovskite/silicon and hybrid tandems command the global photovoltaic efficiency frontier at 33.6% certified, yet their near-complete absence of standardized long-term stability testing and severe efficiency decline upon scaling past 1 cm2 present an urgent barrier that laboratory efficiency claims currently obscure.
