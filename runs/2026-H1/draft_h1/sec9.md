Incompatible conclusions govern buried hole-selective contacts across inverted and tandem architectures, dividing between crystalline molecular order and steric amorphous disorder. In monolithic hybrid tandems, dense pi-pi stacking and crystalline order within self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) are argued to eliminate pinholes and accelerate hole extraction. That route secured a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 32.45% (33.4% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72160-x]. Conversely, competing evidence shows that crystalline order precipitates domain boundaries and accelerates thermal dewetting, requiring an amorphous, asymmetric molecular geometry to suppress phase separation (certified PCE of 32.81%, family-record 34.02% champion) [@10.1002/adma.73106]. Failure pathways also conflict: one study attributes operational decay to out-of-plane molecular desorption from metal oxides [@10.1016/j.scib.2026.02.050], while another shows failure proceeding via field-induced lateral aggregation into clusters [@10.1038/s41467-026-72097-1]. The half year's evidence does not establish whether buried contacts require crystalline coherence or steric disorder, nor whether failure initiates by chemical detachment or lateral dewetting. Resolving this controversy requires operando bias-dependent sum-frequency generation spectroscopy paired with temperature-dependent grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS). It also requires in situ conductive atomic force microscopy (c-AFM) under continuous maximum power point tracking (MPPT) at elevated temperatures.

A second unresolved gap concerns low-dimensional capping layers in single-junction perovskite solar cells (PSCs), where wide-bandgap two-dimensional (2D) or quasi-2D caps over three-dimensional absorbers are reported to suppress non-radiative recombination without degrading collection. One such cap yielded a certified PCE of 27.02% (27.21% champion) under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol ISOS-L-2I [@10.1038/s41467-026-71845-7]. An in situ 2D/3D heterostructure delivered 26.2% certified PCE (26.49% champion) [@10.1002/anie.8890696], and quasi-2D passivation reached 21.69% certified PCE (22.76% champion) [@10.1002/adma.72756]. Opposing studies show that solution-processed 2D caps deposit disordered, insulating spacers with random quantum-well orientations, penalizing fill factor (FF) under illumination. Recovering that loss required vapor reconstruction (certified PCE of 26.15%, 26.71% champion on 1 cm2) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72790-1] or multivalent directing agents (certified PCE of 25.4%, 25.4% champion on 1.1 cm2) [@10.1126/science.aea0656]. Because steady-state photoluminescence cannot distinguish defect passivation from resistive charge accumulation, determining whether 2D caps act as extraction conduits or resistive dead zones requires operando depth-resolved transient absorption spectroscopy paired with cross-sectional c-AFM under operational bias and 1-sun illumination.

A third gap specific to all-perovskite tandems divides over whether narrow-bandgap (NBG) mixed tin-lead (Sn-Pb) degradation is an extrinsic crystallization artifact or an intrinsic electrochemical consequence of operating redox cascades. One view attributes Sn-Pb decay to differential crystallization where tin iodide precipitates prematurely. Synchronizing crystallization via chemical hardness engineering achieved a certified PCE of 30.3% (champion 30.3%) [@10.1038/s41565-026-02165-6], while suppressing Sn-rich precipitation yielded a certified PCE of 28.53% (28.77% champion) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00617]. Conversely, competing reports demonstrate that even in uniform absorbers, reverse bias triggers redox cascades between Sn2+ and oxidized halides (champion 29.03%) [@10.1002/adma.202522942]. Cyclic iodine reactions likewise regenerate triiodide that oxidizes Sn2+ to Sn4+ (certified PCE of 28.79%, 29.29% champion) [@10.1002/anie.4918900]. Current data do not establish whether crystallographic control prevents Sn2+ oxidation under bias. Settling whether oxidation initiates at localized Sn-rich phases or propagates electrochemically requires operando depth-resolved X-ray absorption fine structure (XAFS) spectroscopy coupled with in situ electrochemical impedance spectroscopy under operational bias and illumination.

A fourth gap centers on the efficiency penalty when scaling perovskite solar cells into perovskite solar modules (PSMs). Evidence divides on whether area-dependent deficits stem from laser scribe margins, convective drying instabilities, or bulk lattice strain. Regulating crystallization at laser scribes delivered a certified PCE of 23.55% (24.7% champion) across 100 cm2 under ISOS-D-1 dark storage [@10.1038/s41467-026-69685-6], identifying scribe damage as the primary loss pathway. Conversely, competing models show that convective drying instabilities and coffee-ring segregation dominate scaling deficits. Fluid-locking interlayers reach an uncertified 26.61% champion PCE [@10.1002/adma.202518582], and ambient fluid-crystallization control yields a certified PCE of 25.85% (26.31% champion) on 13.04 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.73373]. Contradicting both, other work attributes deficits to residual strain: strain homogenization achieved a certified PCE of 25.55% (26.59% champion) on 100 cm2 under ISOS-L-1 [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238], and strain coordination reached a certified PCE of 26.12% (26.76% champion) on 12 cm2 [@10.1002/adfm.76756]. Isolating geometric interconnect losses from hydrodynamic drying non-uniformities requires calibrated spatial photoluminescence quantum yield imaging synchronized with micro-beam X-ray diffraction across active strips and scribe lines under bias.

Underpinning all four device families is an acute deficit in standardized operational durability data. Across 872 closely read papers in the six-month corpus, only 39 reported a time to 80% of initial efficiency (T80) lifetime, longest 4100 h, and only 39 named any stability protocol. Independent certification remains sparse across every family. Among 692 single-junction papers (best certified 27.6%, largest area 4 cm2, longest T80 4100 h), only 63 reported certified values. Among 60 all-perovskite tandem papers (best certified 30.3%, largest area 64.64 cm2, longest T80 1600 h), only 15 were certified. Among 77 hybrid tandem papers (best certified 33.6%, largest area 16 cm2, longest T80 1000 h), only 19 were certified, and among 43 module papers (best certified 26.8%, largest area 900 cm2, longest T80 1050 h), only 10 were certified. Because stability assessments use disparate stress regimes, this half year's evidence could not support an equitable cross-family comparison of operational lifetime between single-junction cells and monolithic hybrid tandems. Higher tandem efficiencies therefore cannot be evaluated against single-junction durability. To make device categories comparative, the field must report accredited steady-state power conversion efficiency on verified apertures alongside continuous maximum power point tracking under standardized ISOS protocols (specifically ISOS-L-2 and ISOS-L-3 at 65 °C or 85 °C). Mandatory disclosure of spectral mismatch factors and fill factor loss mechanisms must accompany those measurements.
