A fundamental disagreement across recent multi-junction investigations concerns whether non-radiative voltage deficits originate from defective charge extraction at buried contacts or from trap-assisted recombination within bulk wide-bandgap (WBG) absorbers. In monolithic two-terminal (2T) perovskite/silicon tandems, one framework attributes performance bottlenecks primarily to hole-extraction resistance and halide accumulation at the buried contact, demonstrating that replacing indium tin oxide (ITO) with a reactive plasma deposition (RPD) tin oxide (SnOx) recombination layer directly anchored to self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) delivers a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 33.6% (champion 33.2% on 1 cm2) and a certified 31.0% on a 207.9 cm2 minimodule [@10.1126/science.aef5355]. Conversely, competing investigations conclude that carrier losses are dominated by photoinduced phase segregation and deep-level defect generation in the bulk WBG film, asserting that replacing monomeric molecules with polymeric polycarbazole phosphonic acid (Poly-3Ph-4PACz) relieves bulk-driven recombination to reach a certified 2T tandem PCE of 32.95% (champion 26.13%, stabilised 27.12% in single junctions; 22.22% on a 57.6 cm2 flexible module) [@10.1038/s41467-026-73276-w]. Standard steady-state photoluminescence (PL) and current density-voltage characterization cannot decouple these hypotheses because contact recombination velocity and bulk quasi-Fermi level splitting collapse produce identical terminal open-circuit voltage (Voc) losses. Deciding whether the primary voltage deficit resides at the contact or in the absorber requires operando, spatially resolved voltage-dependent photoluminescence imaging via the local transport and recombination study (LTRS) method coupled with bias-dependent transient absorption spectroscopy. By mapping series resistance to isolate a 1.9% efficiency loss in a 29% monolithic tandem while decoupling current mismatch, low shunt resistance, and luminescence coupling in finite-element simulations, operando LTRS determines whether carrier losses originate from interface extraction barriers or bulk non-radiative recombination [@10.1002/smll.202513958].

Reading the separate axes together reveals that performance gains claimed in one domain routinely impose physical penalties in another. At the perovskite top surface, constructing low-dimensional capping overlayers or deploying bulky organic ammonium passivators terminates dangling bonds and heals halide vacancies, achieving certified PCE values of 26.2% (champion 26.49%) with a two-dimensional/three-dimensional (2D/3D) guanidinium heterostructure [@10.1002/anie.8890696], 26.15% (champion 26.71% on 1 cm2) through one-dimensional (1D) TEAPbI3 phase reconstruction [@10.1038/s41467-026-72790-1], and a certified 27.6% via 3-PMPCl molecular locking [@10.1126/science.aeb9953]. However, evaluating these capping layers against operational degradation demonstrates an incompatible physical demand: bulky organic cations introduce anisotropic out-of-plane transport barriers that trap photogenerated carriers under operating electric fields, while operando structural measurements reveal that bulky passivators undergo pronounced local thermal expansion relative to the perovskite lattice. Under thermal stress, this expansion generates localized microstrain and interfacial fatigue, driving degradation even in unencapsulated devices that retain 87% of initial efficiency after 4092 h of maximum power point tracking (MPPT) under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol ISOS-L-1 with a time to 80% of initial performance (T80) of 3566 h [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6596]. Incorporating facet-engineering additives [@10.1002/adma.73539] or molecular buffering interlayers [@10.1002/adma.202519339] to relieve residual lattice strain demonstrates that chemical defect passivation cannot be decoupled from thermomechanical fatigue: chemical saturation of surface coordination bonds trades immediate electronic gains for latent operational instability under thermal cycling.

An analogous conflict governs the buried interface, where electrostatic optimization clashes with mechanical adhesion and lateral conductivity. Functionalizing carbazole phosphonic acid SAMs with polar cyanoacetic acid co-adsorbents nearly doubles the reverse-bias breakdown voltage to yield an uncertified tandem champion PCE of 32.43% (stabilised 24.43%) [@10.1002/adma.72738], while asymmetric Br-4PhCz monolayers deliver a certified tandem PCE of 28.98% (champion 29.35%) on 0.05 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.202523338]. Yet monomeric SAMs exhibit low fracture resistance and undergo chemical oxidation and mechanical delamination under operational stress. Polymeric crosslinking mitigates interfacial shear failure, as multidentate polymer networks (P-SAM) increase interfacial fracture toughness nearly eightfold to 9.11 MPa (certified 26.61% on 0.06734 cm2) [@10.1002/aenm.71159] and phosphonic-acid-functionalized polymers (PTPP) sustain 94.4% of initial efficiency after 800 thermal cycles (certified 26.4%, champion 27.1%) [@10.1002/adfm.75697]. Nevertheless, crosslinked polymeric interlayers create lateral transport resistance across textured silicon bottom cells, where conformal coverage on random pyramids requires either an 8 nm ITO interlayer with nickel oxide and polymeric SAMs (gaining 0.85 mA cm-2 in short-circuit current density to reach 20.82 mA cm-2 in a 31.8% champion tandem) [@10.1002/adfm.75311] or direct SAM anchoring on SnOx [@10.1126/science.aef5355]. Contact design therefore cannot optimize dipole energetics, mechanical adhesion, and lateral charge extraction simultaneously.

Finally, precursor coordination kinetics reflect an unbridged disconnect between sub-0.1 cm2 efficiency records and scalable manufacturing. While non-destructive contact-triggered cationic interaction (CCI) yields a certified 25.61% PCE (champion 26.25%) [@10.1038/s41560-026-02027-4] and microcrystal growth with polypropylene glycol achieves a certified 26.22% on 0.06 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.202522508], solvent-coordination models reach diametrically opposing conclusions. One framework demands rapid suppression of intermediate solvent adducts to avoid structural defects (uncertified 26.5% on 0.0665 cm2) [@10.1038/s41467-026-68439-8], whereas another insists that strong coordination by N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone is required to delay premature nucleation, increasing Voc from 1.08 to 1.09 V and fill factor (FF) from 75% to 80.5% [@10.1063/5.0297564]. These chemical mechanisms do not converge because the intermediate equilibria that suppress defect formation in static, small-area spin coating fail under the mechanical shear and drying gradients of scalable deposition [@10.20517/ss.2025.128]. Rather than converging on a shared physical mechanism, the literature remains bifurcated between small-area electrostatic optimization and the thermo-chemo-mechanical realities of operational modules.
