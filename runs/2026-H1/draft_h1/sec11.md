A fundamental disagreement in multi-junction investigations concerns whether non-radiative voltage deficits originate from defective charge extraction at buried contacts or from trap-assisted recombination within bulk wide-bandgap (WBG) absorbers. Anchoring self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) directly to a reactive plasma deposition (RPD) tin oxide (SnOx) recombination layer to suppress contact barriers and halide accumulation delivers a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 33.6% (champion 33.2% on 1 cm2) and certified 31.0% on a 207.9 cm2 minimodule [@10.1126/science.aef5355]. In direct contradiction, competing reports conclude that carrier losses are dominated by photoinduced halide segregation and deep trap formation within the bulk WBG film, asserting that polymeric polycarbazole phosphonic acid (Poly-3Ph-4PACz) suppresses bulk recombination to yield a certified two-terminal (2T) tandem PCE of 32.95% (champion 26.13%, stabilised 27.12% in single junctions; 22.22% on a 57.6 cm2 flexible module) [@10.1038/s41467-026-73276-w]. Standard current-voltage scans cannot resolve this conflict because contact recombination velocity and bulk quasi-Fermi level splitting collapse produce identical open-circuit voltage (Voc) deficits. Decoupling contact resistance from bulk non-radiative recombination requires operando, spatially resolved voltage-dependent photoluminescence (PL) imaging via the local transport and recombination study (LTRS) method paired with bias-dependent transient absorption spectroscopy under continuous illumination to isolate interfacial from bulk carrier losses [@10.1002/smll.202513958].

A second unresolved contradiction governs operational degradation at perovskite interfaces, where studies diverge on whether hole-selective contact decay proceeds via vertical molecular detachment or lateral dewetting, and whether bulky passivators relieve lattice strain or accelerate fatigue. One investigation attributes contact failure to out-of-plane molecular desorption that planar conjugated backbones suppress [@10.1016/j.scib.2026.02.050], whereas another concludes that degradation is driven by electric-field-induced lateral migration of SAM molecules into isolated clusters [@10.1038/s41467-026-72097-1]. Compounding this ambiguity, bulky organic passivators designed to raise phase transition barriers, such as 3-PMPCl reaching a certified 27.6% PCE in formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI3) [@10.1126/science.aeb9953], induce local lattice expansion under thermal stress at heterointerfaces, driving interfacial fatigue even while unencapsulated devices retain 87% initial PCE after 4092 h of maximum power point tracking (MPPT) under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol ISOS-L-1 with a time to 80% of initial performance (T80) of 3566 h [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6596]. Deciding whether contact failure originates from covalent bond cleavage, lateral dewetting, or thermal expansion mismatch requires operando bias-dependent sum-frequency generation spectroscopy coupled with in situ high-temperature grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering under continuous electrical load.

A third research gap concerns conflicting kinetic pathways for stabilizing the photoactive black alpha phase of FAPbI3 against polymorphic transition into the photoinactive yellow delta phase. Contact-triggered cationic interaction asserts that phase stability occurs through non-destructive physical contact between separately crystallized two-dimensional (2D) and three-dimensional (3D) perovskites without interdiffusion, yielding a certified 25.61% PCE (champion 26.25%) [@10.1038/s41560-026-02027-4]. In direct contrast, recrystallization using formamidinium tetrafluoroborate demonstrates that forming stable mixed-dimensional networks requires active formamidinium/methylammonium cation exchange, while 2D perovskitoid interlayers disrupt delta-phase nucleation to achieve an uncertified champion 25.61% PCE [@10.1038/s41467-026-73739-0]. Concurrently, low-dimensional passivators reach certified efficiencies of 26.2% (champion 26.49%) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72790-1], but whether these capping layers introduce resistive transport bottlenecks remains unproven. Resolving whether phase stabilization proceeds by passive interfacial templating or dynamic chemical exchange requires in situ time-resolved grazing-incidence X-ray scattering paired with operando nanoscale secondary ion mass spectrometry during thermal annealing and light soaking.

A fourth mechanistic gap lies in opposing kinetic models of precursor-solvent coordination during scalable meniscus coating. One framework contends that prolonged solvent-solute interaction during blade coating stabilizes detrimental solvent-adduct intermediates, demanding rapid adduct suppression to achieve 26.5% PCE on 0.0665 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-68439-8]. Conversely, competing investigations conclude that strong coordination by N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone or dimethyl sulfoxide is indispensable to retard premature nucleation and stabilize intermediate complexes, improving Voc from 1.08 to 1.09 V and fill factor (FF) from 75% to 80.5% [@10.1063/5.0297564], or achieving certified 26.22% PCE on 0.06 cm2 through polypropylene glycol microcrystal regulation [@10.1002/adma.202522508]. However, these coordination models remain demonstrated only on sub-0.1 cm2 apertures without verifying that intermediate equilibria persist across large areas under industrial drying and mechanical shear [@10.20517/ss.2025.128]. Closing this gap requires in situ optical reflectance and photoluminescence spectroscopy coupled with synchrotron X-ray diffraction during high-speed slot-die coating across modules exceeding 100 cm2.

Underpinning these physical gaps is a persistent empirical disconnect between certified peak efficiency, device area, and operational durability verification. Across the half year, the best certified PCE reached 34.85% in a 2T tandem, yet only 114 of 872 closely read papers reported any certified value. The best certified value moved from 29.2% in January to 33.6% in June, a change of 4.4 percentage points over the half year, while uncertified claims outpaced certified benchmarks, reaching 41.6% in April and 44.36% in May. Durability verification remains similarly sparse: only 39 papers reported a T80 lifetime, with the longest reaching 4100 h, and only 39 papers named an ISOS protocol. Although the largest reported aperture reached 900 cm2, champion efficiencies remain confined to sub-0.1 cm2 cells that mask sheet resistance, interconnect losses, and drying gradients. Crucially, this half year's evidence could not support a rigorous comparison between buried contact passivators and bulk wide-bandgap stabilizers because reported devices diverged across uncalibrated active areas and unstandardized aging regimes. Instead of unencapsulated room-temperature scans on micro-cells, the field must measure encapsulated devices under continuous operational load using standardized ISOS-L-2 or ISOS-L-3 protocols at 65 °C, reporting certified steady-state power output on apertures of at least 1 cm2 alongside spatially resolved luminescence maps of local contact resistance.
