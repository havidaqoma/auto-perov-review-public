Conflicting kinetic models across recent scalable coating studies reflect an unresolved physical disagreement over whether precursor-solvent coordination accelerates or impedes uniform perovskite crystallization. One framework asserts that prolonged solvent-precursor interaction time during blade coating stabilizes detrimental solvent-adduct intermediate phases that degrade film crystallinity, demanding rapid adduct suppression [@10.1038/s41467-026-68439-8]. In direct contradiction, alternative investigations conclude that strong coordination by N-methyl-2-pyrrolidone (NMP) in dimethylformamide (DMF)-free 2-methoxyethanol (2-ME) or isopropanol (IPA) is necessary to stabilize intermediate complexes and delay premature surface nucleation, thereby increasing open-circuit voltage (Voc) from 1.08 to 1.09 V and fill factor (FF) from 75% to 80.5% [@10.1063/5.0297564] [@10.1002/solr.70406]. This kinetic divergence extends across solvent systems: ternary dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO), acetonitrile (ACN), and 1,3-dimethyl-2-imidazolidinone (DMI) mixtures rely on volatile ACN to accelerate initial nucleation during slot-die printing to reach 20.62% power conversion efficiency (PCE) [@10.1016/j.matre.2026.100406], whereas reduced-temperature bar coating at 100 °C employs low translation speeds of 0.3 mm/s to sustain a metastable supersaturation zone rather than accelerating precipitation [@10.1002/solr.202600003]. Resolving whether solvent adducts degrade or preserve large-area films requires operando spatial diagnostics; coupling in situ photoluminescence (PL) and diffuse reflectance during quenching with neural networks captures non-uniform crystallization before localized shunts solidify [@10.3390/mi17030314] [@10.1007/s40820-026-02134-7]. Precursor colloid engineering via formate anions restricts cluster size to around 30 nm [@10.1002/adma.202520776], and polypropylene glycol (PPG) aids formamidinium lead iodide (FAPbI3) microcrystal synthesis [@10.1002/adma.202522508]. However, claims of defect suppression through adduct control remain restricted to uncertified, sub-0.1 cm2 cells (such as 26.5% PCE on 0.0665 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-68439-8] or certified 26.22% on 0.06 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.202522508]), without proving that intermediate coordination equilibria survive roll-to-roll drying gradients under mechanical shear [@10.20517/ss.2025.128].

A second major source of the area penalty originates in charge transport layer (CTL) non-uniformity and buried contact resistance across large apertures. For electron transport layers (ETLs), atmospheric-pressure spatial atomic layer deposition (AP-SALD) of tin oxide (SnOx) achieves 20% PCE following 180 °C annealing, yet conformal deposition replicates substrate roughness, which impairs perovskite crystallization [@10.1016/j.solmat.2026.114233]. Solution approaches attempt to mitigate interfacial roughness: electrostatic regulation of SnO2 nanoparticle assembly yields uniform coatings, delivering 24.70% PCE at 1 cm2 and over 21% on a 65.24 cm2 module (certified 26.03% on 0.0727 cm2) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00476], while multicycle infrared annealing of SnO2 quantum dot (QD) ETLs limits cracking, reaching 17.09% at 1 cm2 and 15.18% at 2 cm2 compared to 12.78% for conventional hotplate annealing [@10.1016/j.rineng.2026.111690]. For hole-selective contacts, self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) suffer severe dewetting during large-area coating; incorporating 3-BPIC-F into MeO-4PACz enhances dipole alignment and suppresses pinholes, raising module PCE from 16.43% to 19.80% (champion 23.64%) [@10.1021/acsami.6c03670], while low-polarity alkane and alcohol solvents enable blade coating of SAMs at 80% relative humidity (RH) to yield a certified 26.1% PCE [@10.1038/s41467-026-72581-8]. At the counter interface, non-uniform chemical doping induces spatial conductivity variances, which ferrocenium radical cation dopants in Spiro-OMeTAD eliminate to achieve 26.13% in cells and 22.21% in modules [@10.1016/j.joule.2025.102310].

Hydrodynamic instability and resistive interconnect losses govern the final translation from single devices to large modules. Drying flows induce severe thickness gradients; fluid-locking interfacial layers counteract coffee-ring segregation to yield 21.39% PCE on a 100 cm2 module [@10.1002/adma.202518582], whereas slot-die coated FAPbI3 without fluid control drops from 20.8% on small areas to 19.1% at 8.4 cm2 and 14.8% at 59.5 cm2 active area [@10.1021/acsami.5c22032]. Post-deposition passivation attempts to seal boundary defects: immersing hot-bladed films into cold strontium iodide (SrI2) isopropanol solutions produces 20.32% on a 10.13 cm2 module [@10.1038/s41467-026-69264-9], and pneumatic spray coating forms 1D PyPbI3 surface barriers (champion 21.64%) [@10.1016/j.mtsust.2026.101347]. Fully dry vacuum co-evaporation avoids wet-film fluid mechanics entirely, using PbCl2 additions to disrupt PbI2 stacking and reach over 38% efficiency at 900 lux on a 3.9 cm2 indoor module [@10.1002/smtd.70654]. Beyond 100 cm2, mechanical deformation and interconnect resistance dominate: dynamic covalent cross-linking monomers embedded in perovskites provide self-curing at 40 °C for 30 min, yielding a certified 26.8% and champion 27.12% PCE across 655.2 cm2 flexible modules [@10.1002/adma.73855], while liquid metal interconnects maintain continuity over a 900 cm2 platform [@10.1002/adfm.202532205].
