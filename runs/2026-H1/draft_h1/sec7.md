Studies of operational degradation reveal an unresolved mechanistic contradiction regarding whether bulky molecular passivators relieve interfacial lattice strain or accelerate degradation through local thermal expansion. Bulky passivators are widely applied to terminate dangling bonds and impede defect formation, with molecular locks such as 3-PMPCl raising the phase transition barrier in formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI3) by suppressing [PbI6] octahedral disorder to reach a certified 27.6% power conversion efficiency (PCE) [@10.1126/science.aeb9953]. Similarly, facet-engineering additives such as sulfonates relieve residual lattice strain by redirecting crystallization kinetics [@10.1002/adma.73539], and molecular buffering layers prevent mechanochemical fatigue under thermal cycling [@10.1002/adma.202519339]. However, direct operando structural analysis demonstrates that bulky anionic passivators induce local lattice expansion under thermal stress at heterointerfaces, driving interfacial fatigue even while unencapsulated devices retain 87% of their initial efficiency after 4092 h of maximum power point tracking (MPPT) under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol ISOS-L-1 with a time to 80% of initial performance (T80) of 3566 h [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6596]. This finding directly challenges the assumption that bulky organic modifiers serve as inert physical anchors. The contradiction cannot be resolved by post-mortem spectroscopy because lattice relaxation occurs upon cooling; settling it requires in situ, temperature-dependent grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering paired with depth-resolved photoelectron spectroscopy under operational bias to quantify local lattice expansion directly against defect formation rates.

A parallel divergence divides studies of buried hole-selective interfaces in inverted (p-i-n) architectures. One body of literature identifies out-of-plane molecular desorption from metal oxides as the primary degradation mode, which can be mitigated by enforcing spatial confinement with a rigid planar backbone achieving 3.72 Å pi-pi stacking [@10.1016/j.scib.2026.02.050] or by synthesizing polymeric self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) such as Poly-4PADCB to reduce steric hindrance [@10.1002/adma.72622]. Conversely, competing evidence attributes contact failure to spontaneous molecular aggregation that leaves bare oxide domains, an issue countered through asymmetric molecules with steric hindrance like PhBr-4PACz that retain over 96% efficiency after 2000 h at 65 °C under ISOS-L-2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-72097-1] or fluorinated confinement interlayers [@10.1038/s41467-026-72115-2]. A third perspective indicates that SAM degradation occurs predominantly during perovskite solution processing via solvent stripping, requiring transient 2D perovskite sacrificial barriers [@10.1002/adma.73561] or hydroxyl enrichment using MXene additives [@10.1002/adma.73656]. Although amino acid hydrochloride additives [@10.1002/aenm.70925] and tailored hole transport materials like WH13 [@10.1002/anie.202523665] yield stability claims, these evaluations frequently rely on tiny active areas (such as 0.058 cm2 in [@10.1002/aenm.70925]), where edge encapsulation masks real-world planar degradation. In perovskite/silicon tandem cells, co-assembled siloxane monolayers [@10.1002/aenm.71107] and amorphous asymmetric contacts [@10.1002/adma.73106] retain 87.12% and 87% initial PCE after 1000 h of MPPT at 85 °C, respectively, confirming that resilient chemisorption retards thermally activated interfacial delamination.

In the perovskite absorber, the consensus that device decay is governed primarily by bulk mobile ions has been contested by direct quantification of deep-level defect kinetics. Operational degradation is dominated by deep-level interstitial and antisite defects, specifically iodine on formamidinium sites (I_FA) and lead sites (I_Pb), which drive performance decay despite existing at concentrations three orders of magnitude below shallow defects [@10.1002/adma.73679]. Passivating these deep traps with 3TU2+ reduces rear-interface energy losses from 1.46% to 0.62% [@10.1002/adma.73679]. In contrast, widespread reports continue to focus on macroscopic ionic flux, using liquid additives such as 1-BP to suppress ion migration as tracked by time-of-flight secondary ion mass spectrometry (ToF-SIMS) [@10.1038/s41467-025-68231-0], positional methylation isomers like 5-MH that retain 92% efficiency after 2000 h under ISOS-L2 [@10.1002/adma.202520432], or eperisone hydrochloride to eliminate residual dimethyl sulfoxide (DMSO) and maintain 90.39% efficiency after 1200 h under ISOS-L-3 [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c03999]. Other interventions combat defect strain through trifluoroethylammonium chloride (3TFACl) additives [@10.1002/adfm.76756] or 1D (PCOZ)PbI3 interlayers that preserve 94.2% efficiency after 1000 h under ISOS-L-3 [@10.1002/adma.73161].

Operational stability is also restricted by charge transport layers, where conflicting failure mechanisms are assigned to identical materials. In inverted devices, phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) degrades via photo-induced [2+2] cycloaddition dimerization under illumination, inducing aggregation and morphological decay that nonplanar tetraphenylethylene additives prevent [@10.1002/solr.70405]. Yet separate studies treat fullerene decline as an interfacial corrosion problem solved by buffer layers like BAE-Phen that coordinate migrant metal ions to retain 90.5% PCE after 2000 h at 85 °C [@10.1002/adma.73529], solvent-free multicomponent fullerene composites [@10.1038/s41467-026-70022-0], or bridging interlayers tested on 0.045 cm2 cells with a T80 of 500 h [@10.1002/anie.9286497]. In normal (n-i-p) devices employing 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (Spiro-OMeTAD), a fundamental trade-off persists: promoting lithium bis(trifluoromethanesulfonyl)imide (LiTFSI) ion-pair dissociation is necessary to sustain electrical conductivity [@10.1002/aenm.202506760], yet uncoordinated lithium ions migrate rapidly into the absorber. Suppressing this degradation channel requires either phenothiazine ionic anchors to bind lithium, yielding 90% retention after 800 h of MPPT on 0.04 cm2 cells [@10.1002/adfm.76257], or lithium-based deep eutectic matrices that deliver 90.5% retention after 1800 h under ISOS-L-1I [@10.1002/anie.9551164].

These mechanistic debates unfold against an acute reporting deficit across the field: of 872 closely read papers published over the half year, only 39 reported a T80 value and only 39 specified an ISOS testing protocol. Although champion operational lifespans reached 4100 h [@10.1002/adfm.76110], 4000 h [@10.1002/adfm.74893], 3600 h [@10.1021/acs.jpclett.6c01531], and 3566 h [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6596], the rarity of standardized aging procedures leaves the relative severity of interfacial expansion, ion accumulation, and transport layer degradation obscured by divergent testing metrics.
