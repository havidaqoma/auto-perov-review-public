A fundamental disagreement across device architectures centers on whether charge extraction through buried hole-selective contacts requires strict crystalline molecular order or steric amorphous disorder. In monolithic hybrid tandems, one investigation asserts that dense intermolecular pi-pi stacking and rigid crystalline order within self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) are essential to eliminate pinholes, align bands, and accelerate extraction. That work secured an independently certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 32.45% (33.4% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72160-x]. In direct conflict, another study demonstrates that crystalline order in hole-selective monolayers precipitates nanoscale domain boundaries, induces aggregation, and accelerates thermal dewetting. Suppressing phase separation there required an amorphous, asymmetric molecular geometry, which preserved conformal passivation and reached a certified PCE of 32.81% (family-record 34.02% champion) [@10.1002/adma.73106]. In single junctions, this contact is interpreted instead through electrostatic dipole models where work-function shifts govern extraction (certified PCE of 26.23%, champion 26.18%) [@10.1038/s41467-026-69198-2]. Competing reports attribute open-circuit voltage (Voc) gains to chemical termination of undercoordinated ions and hydroxyl defects on nickel oxide (NiOx) (certified PCE of 26.2% on 0.0524 cm2) [@10.1002/adma.73656] [@10.1002/adma.73382]. Settling whether extraction is governed by long-range crystalline order, steric amorphous disorder, or chemical hydroxyl passivation requires operando conductive atomic force microscopy (c-AFM) and temperature-dependent grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering (GIWAXS). Both must run simultaneously under operational electrical bias and thermal stress, so that nanoscale current percolation can be correlated directly with molecular reorientation.

Reading the four device families together reveals that identical physical failures recur across architectures under divergent terminology. In single-junction cells, absorber decay is framed as lattice microstrain and octahedral distortion. Static molecular confinement (certified PCE of 26.79% under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol ISOS-L-1) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72115-2] conflicts there with dynamic photoswitchable isomers that dissipate cyclical thermal fatigue (certified PCE of 26.9%, champion 27.2%) [@10.1038/s41560-026-01993-z], and with dual-dynamic covalent seeding (certified PCE of 26.69%, protocol ISOS-L-2I) [@10.1002/adma.72823]. In modules, this mechanochemical instability resurfaces as convective drying gradients and heterogeneous out-of-plane residual lattice strain across macroscopic substrates. Homogenizing out-of-plane strain enabled an independently certified PCE of 25.55% (champion 26.59%) across a 100 cm2 flexible module under protocol ISOS-L-1 [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238]. Fluid-locking interlayers suppressed convective solute segregation to reach an uncertified 26.61% champion PCE [@10.1002/adma.202518582], and ambient fluid control achieved a certified PCE of 25.85% on 13.04 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.73373]. Interfacial module loss is concurrently assigned to laser scribe margins, where regulating crystallization kinetics yielded a certified PCE of 23.55% across 100 cm2 under protocol ISOS-D-1 [@10.1038/s41467-026-69685-6]. What single-junction studies isolate as microscopic octahedral distortion therefore drives the macroscopic drying fissures, scribe recombination, and strain gradients that penalize large modules.

Conversely, chemical interventions validated in one device family introduce severe transport penalties when transferred into another. In single-junction devices, depositing wide-bandgap two-dimensional (2D) or quasi-2D caps over three-dimensional (3D) absorbers suppresses non-radiative recombination and moisture ingress. That route yielded a certified PCE of 27.02% under protocol ISOS-L-2I [@10.1038/s41467-026-71845-7], a certified PCE of 26.2% via in situ guanidinium heterostructures [@10.1002/anie.8890696], and a certified PCE of 21.69% in quasi-2D films [@10.1002/adma.72756]. In modules, surface 2D/3D capping provides environmental defense, reaching a 26.22% champion PCE across 6.8 cm2 with a 1050 h time to 80% of initial efficiency (T80) [@10.1007/s40820-025-02058-8], and a 26.52% champion PCE on 50 cm2 using non-Dion-Jacobson diammonium cations [@10.1002/anie.2974414]. Transferred into monolithic two-terminal (2T) tandems, however, these low-dimensional caps introduce anisotropic out-of-plane transport barriers from disordered organic spacers. Single-junction cells tolerate minor extraction resistance, whereas series-connected tandems suffer acute fill factor (FF) losses and current mismatch under illumination. Single-junction and module devices avoided insulating phases through vapor-mediated reconstruction enforcing vertical transport (certified PCE of 26.15% on 1 cm2) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72790-1] or multivalent oriented ligands (certified PCE of 25.4% on 1.1 cm2) [@10.1126/science.aea0656]. Hybrid tandems instead required gradient Ruddlesden-Popper heterostructures to contract current mismatch to 0.1% and reach a certified PCE of 32.46% [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04127].

The four families also impose incompatible demands on the same functional layers. At the interconnection junction of hybrid tandems, minimizing optical parasitics favors discarding sputtered transparent conducting oxide (TCO) layers. Reactive plasma deposition tin oxide (RPD-SnOx) demonstrated this route, achieving the record certified PCE of 33.6% (champion 33.2% on 1 cm2) [@10.1126/science.aef5355]. Yet omitting lateral conductivity is unviable in modules and monolithic tandems, where current non-uniformities across textured pyramids or scribe lines create localized resistive bottlenecks. Those bottlenecks cap unoptimized monolithic output at an uncertified 29% champion PCE [@10.1002/smll.202513958], while sputtered indium tin oxide (ITO) on 16 cm2 drops hybrid tandem output to an uncertified 22.1% champion [@10.1016/j.solmat.2026.114223]. Monolithic integration also converts current mismatch into an electrochemical destruction route. In all-perovskite tandems, current mismatch drives the narrow-bandgap (NBG) mixed tin-lead (Sn-Pb) subcell into reverse bias, where hole injection triggers an autonomous redox cascade between divalent tin (Sn2+) and oxidized halides that degrades the lattice (champion 29.03%) [@10.1002/adma.202522942]. Cyclic iodine redox at buried contacts accompanies that cascade (certified PCE of 28.79%, champion 29.29%) [@10.1002/anie.4918900]. Synchronizing crystallization kinetics attains a certified PCE of 30.3% [@10.1038/s41565-026-02165-6], but cannot prevent this reverse-bias decay.

Read together, these four device families do not share a single optimization pathway. Single junctions operate in an electrostatic regime on sub-0.1 cm2 areas, hybrid tandems are governed by optical parasitics and buried contact order, and all-perovskite tandems are constrained by reverse-bias redox degradation. Modules remain bottlenecked by convective drying instabilities, scribe series resistance, and mechanical shear across expanded apertures up to 900 cm2 [@10.1002/adfm.202532205]. Treating them as sequential milestones of a single technology obscures the reality that each family occupies a distinct physical problem space.
