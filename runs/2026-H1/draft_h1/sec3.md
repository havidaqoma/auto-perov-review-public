Recent investigations into trap passivation and ionic transport diverge sharply regarding the structural configuration required to deactivate surface defects without impeding charge collection. One faction asserts that capping the three-dimensional (3D) perovskite absorber with low-dimensional phases is essential to eliminate residual reagents and suppress non-radiative recombination. Vapor-mediated dimensional reconstruction using triethylamine-pentafluorophenol (TEA-PFP) to react with excess lead iodide (PbI2) forms a one-dimensional (1D) TEAPbI3 interfacial phase, yielding a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 26.15% (champion 26.71% on 1 cm2) and a fill factor (FF) of 89.13% [@10.1038/s41467-026-72790-1]. In situ generation of a guanidinium derivative similarly templates a two-dimensional/three-dimensional (2D/3D) heterostructure, giving a certified PCE of 26.2% (champion 26.49%) with 95% retention after 1000 h of maximum power point tracking (MPPT) [@10.1002/anie.8890696]. Conversely, competing reports argue that low-dimensional capping phases introduce anisotropic transport barriers, carrier accumulation, and phase instability under operational bias. They demonstrate instead that non-2D organic co-crystal layers passivate defects via reinforced hydrogen-bonding networks and enhanced electron mobility, achieving a champion PCE of 26.35% with a T80 lifetime of 1392 h and 88% efficiency retention after 2040 h at approximately 50% relative humidity [@10.1002/adma.202519138]. This rejection of low-dimensional capping is corroborated by stereoelectronic ligand engineering: pyridine or pyrimidine rings forming dual lead-nitrogen (Pb-N) coordination and Pb-I-pi interactions drive planar ligand alignment, producing atomic-scale defect mitigation with sub-nanometre charge transfer and a certified PCE of 27.41% (stabilised at 26.85%) without dimensional reconstruction [@10.1038/s41586-026-10626-0]. Settling this dispute requires operando bias-dependent, depth-resolved grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering paired with local transient absorption microscopy under continuous thermal stress to establish whether 1D and 2D phases degrade into resistive barriers under operational electric fields or preserve a stable passivation contact.

Chemical passivation strategies have targeted undercoordinated metal cations, halide vacancies, and antisite defects through coordinated Lewis base and acid interactions. Carboxyl-group chelation of undercoordinated lead (Pb2+) by dipicolinic acid (DPA) passivates surface and bulk traps in 1.68 eV wide-bandgap (WBG) cells, reaching 22.91% PCE and a champion 31.66% in two-terminal (2T) perovskite/silicon tandems [@10.1002/aenm.70902]. Lewis acid-base coordination using 3-hydroxyflavone coordinates Pb2+ and increases the ion-migration barrier, though its champion PCE of 26.6% was measured on an uncertified, small area of 0.09 cm2 [@10.1002/smll.73673]. Adding meta-fluoro-phenylethylammonium iodide (m-F-PEAI) to ethyl acetate antisolvent employs the fluorine atom as a Lewis base toward Pb2+ while establishing directed hydrogen bonds with formamidinium iodide (FAI) to retard crystallization, achieving 26.41% champion PCE [@10.1021/acsaem.6c00562]. Redox-active dabrafenib (DRFN) coordinates multiple Pb2+ centers via its sulfonyl group and heterocyclic nitrogen, achieving peak PCEs of 25.28% in n-i-p and 26.62% in p-i-n devices [@10.1002/adfm.76623]. Molecular dipole passivators 2-amino-4,6-dichlorotriazine and 1-adamantanecarbonyl chloride hydrochloride (ACHCl) combine Lewis-base coordination with chloride filling of iodine vacancies (VI), correcting PbI6 octahedral distortion and reducing recombination to reach champion PCEs of 26.58% and 26.12%, respectively [@10.1021/acsmaterialslett.5c01678] [@10.1007/s40820-026-02150-7]. Multi-defect healing additives pairing pentafluorophenol (PFP) with 1,4-dicyanobenzene (DCB) target VI, lead vacancies (VPb), and lead-on-iodine (PbI) antisites, retaining 95% efficiency after 1300 h at 65 °C and 94% after 1000 h of 1-sun MPPT (champion PCE 26.47%) [@10.1002/smll.73436]. Complementary bimolecular passivation with 1,4-phenylenediammonium diiodide (PDADI) and 11-mercaptoundecanoic acid (MUA) reduces trap density from 3.00 × 10^15 to 1.65 × 10^15 cm^-3 (champion PCE 26.15%) [@10.1002/adfm.202532110], while quantitative inkjet printing of PDADI alone yields 26.1% PCE [@10.1002/smll.73886]. In the bulk, precursor dicyandiamide improves vacancy formation energies and crystallinity, producing a monolithic perovskite/silicon tandem champion PCE of 32.03% with an open-circuit voltage (Voc) of 1.95 V [@10.1021/acsami.6c01711]. Chiral configurations show that stereochemical orientation controls defect thermodynamics: (R)-(+)-alpha-phenylethylamine (R-Pye) exhibits higher binding energy (9.45 versus 7.33 eV) and increased VI formation energy (4.56 versus 2.46 eV) than (S)-(-)-alpha-phenylethylamine (S-Pye), reaching 26.13% champion PCE [@10.1002/aenm.71222]. To prevent ammonium deprotonation, sterically protected cations like N,N-dimethylcyclohexylammonium iodide (DMCHAI) inhibit side reactions with formamidinium (FA+), maintaining over 86% efficiency after 550 h at 85 °C (champion PCE 26.06%) [@10.1021/acsami.6c04956], matching steric and electronic tuning in trimetazidine (TMZ) that achieves 26.07% PCE with over 90% retention after 1400 h [@10.1002/adfm.75743]. Latent passivator 3-trifluoromethylphenylboronic acid pinacol ester (3-CF3-PhBpin) hydrolyzes in situ into 3-CF3-PhB(OH)2 to passivate crystallization defects, achieving 26.25% in rigid and 24.80% in flexible n-i-p cells [@10.1002/adfm.75370]. Machine-learning frameworks integrating domain-knowledge large language models with Bayesian optimization have independently proposed untested four-component recipes (3MTPAI, PDAI2, EDAI2, and PipDI) reaching 26.0% champion PCE [@10.48550/arxiv.2602.04914].

Over the six-month period, investigation of ion migration and halide segregation revealed an incompatibility between static immobilization and dynamic strain dissipation. Static strategies pin mobile ions through spatial confinement or covalent polymerization. Molecular confinement domains suppress halide segregation by constraining FA+ motion, enabling single-junction efficiencies of 24.21% (1.68 eV) and 21.20% (1.78 eV) and a 33.59% champion 2T tandem PCE [@10.1002/adma.73713]. Incorporating phenylethylguanidinium (PEGA+) salts suppresses cation drift in 1.55 eV perovskites, exceeding 26.2% PCE with over 96% retention after 1100 h and 89% after 800 h at 85 °C [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00737]. In situ polymerization of vinylphosphonic acid outclasses small-molecule organoammoniums, retaining over 90% PCE after 1600 h of 1-sun MPPT (certified 26.24%, champion 26.54%) [@10.1002/adma.202522825]. In situ polymerization of sodium thioctate (TANA) combined with sodium interstitial doping regulates crystallization and suppresses ion migration, retaining 92.3% efficiency after 1000 h under International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) ISOS-L-2I conditions (certified 26.75%, champion 26.9% on a 10.24 cm2 module) [@10.1002/anie.202525815]. Self-polymerizable fluorinated additives (PFPA) achieve a champion PCE of 26.01% on a 14 cm2 area, retaining 90.0% efficiency after 840 h at 85 °C in nitrogen and 95.1% after 1000 h of MPPT [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c03901]. Room-temperature condensation between amino and acyl chloride groups establishes a dual-anchor polymeric interlayer, preserving 85% efficiency after 2300 h of MPPT and 1680 h at 65 °C (champion PCE 26.12%) [@10.1002/anie.7579788]. At the transport interface, bisphosphonate interlayers (2OBCz) between the perovskite and hole transport layer (HTL) inhibit ion migration, retaining 90.7% efficiency after 1000 h under ISOS-D-2 conditions compared to monophosphonates (LBCz) (champion PCE 26.02%) [@10.1002/adfm.75412]. In narrow-bandgap tin-lead (Sn-Pb) cells, formamidine bisulfate (FBH) dipole management reverses top-surface grain-boundary states to induce downward band bending, preserving over 80% efficiency after 400 h at 85 °C and 90% after 1872 h in a glovebox (champion PCE 23.25%, tandem 29.67%) [@10.1002/adma.72755].

In direct conflict with the static immobilization premise, evidence indicates that rigid polymer networks suffer fatigue and microcracking under thermal cycling, opening pathways for ion accumulation. Dynamic networks resolve this failure mode: mechanochemically reinforced dual-dynamic covalent seeding (DDS) via orthogonal Diels-Alder and oxime-carbamate reactions constructs an interpenetrating covalent mesh at grain boundaries that heals under strain while passivating defects and blocking ion and moisture ingress (certified PCE 26.69%, champion 26.95%, ISOS-L-2I) [@10.1002/adma.72823]. Integrating photoswitchable isomers at grain boundaries suppresses lattice bond rupture and defect accumulation during cyclic illumination and thermal fluctuation, retaining over 95% of initial efficiency after 2,000 h of UV-containing light cycling at 65 °C and 500 thermal cycles between -40 °C and 85 °C (certified PCE 26.9%, champion 27.2%) [@10.1038/s41560-026-01993-z]. Dynamic methyl viologen (MV) interlayers similarly maintain 90% efficiency after 1,000 h of AM 1.5G MPPT (champion PCE 26.44%, Voc 1.167 V, FF 85%) [@10.1002/adfm.202530317]. Systematic temperature-dependent transient ion drift measurements across sustained thermal cycles are necessary to determine whether dynamic covalent networks surpass rigid polymers in preventing long-term mobile ion accumulation.

Surface passivation and ionic stabilization depend heavily on energetic alignment across selective contacts. In inverted p-i-n devices, modulating intermolecular N-H...N hydrogen bonding between two passivators tunes energy-level offsets, yielding a 24.04% wide-bandgap single-junction PCE and a certified monolithic 2T tandem PCE of 33.48% (champion 33.63% on 1 cm2) [@10.1126/sciadv.aec4431]. Self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) on nickel oxide (NiOx) suffer from phase aggregation and incomplete coverage. Co-adsorbing N-indoleacetic acid with [4-(3,6-dimethyl-9H-carbazol-9-yl)butyl]phosphonic acid (Ph-4PACz) forms phase-homogeneous binary monolayers via pi-pi stacking and hydrogen bonding, achieving 26.95% PCE (certified 26.57%) on 0.0717 cm2 and 25.61% on 1 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-71301-6]. Blending 4PABCz with Me-4PACz forms a Co-SAM that enhances coverage on NiOx, passivating bottom defects and suppressing non-radiative recombination (champion PCE 26.33%) [@10.1002/eem2.70443]. Positional isomerism provides structural control: selective chlorine placement on the peripheral phenyl ring of Ph-4PACz (ortho-, meta-, and para-chlorinated isomers) enables lattice-matched dual-site passivation (certified PCE 26.13%, champion 26.58%) [@10.1002/adma.202523249]. Indolocarbazole-based SAMs (M3PAICz-1) demonstrate versatility across bandgaps, delivering 26.12% PCE in 1.55 eV cells and 22.19% in 1.68 eV wide-bandgap devices [@10.1002/smll.202512942], while tripodal phosphonic acids anchor multidentately with face-on pi-stacking and segregate to grain boundaries to passivate undercoordinated Pb2+ (certified PCE 26.35%) [@10.1002/aenm.70806]. The underlying metal-oxide contact can also be chemically stabilized: bromoacetamide (BAA) undergoes in situ dissociative adsorption on sputtered NiOx, where bromide ions coordinate surface nickel to stabilize Ni3+ and suppress disorder (certified PCE 26.07%, champion 26.31%) [@10.1002/adma.73382].

At the electron-selective contact, isomer-free multi-adduct fullerene derivatives C60(NHR)4O (TATPC) blended into [6,6]-phenyl-C61-butyric acid methyl ester (PCBM) raise the lowest unoccupied molecular orbital (LUMO) level, strengthening interfacial interactions to reach 26.66% champion PCE on a 1.04 cm2 aperture [@10.1002/anie.4500167]. Fused-ring electron acceptor (FREA) additives similarly passivate defects and accelerate electron extraction, yielding a certified PCE of 26.35% (champion 26.66%) with under 8% degradation after 2150 h under continuous illumination [@10.1021/jacs.5c16976]. In tandem subcells, differential coordination using 1,10-phenanthroline coordinates Sn2+ more strongly than Pb2+, synchronizing crystallization to deliver a Sn-Pb single-junction Voc of 0.887 V, an FF of 81.27%, and a PCE of 23.63% (tandem champion 29.08%) [@10.1002/eem2.70453]. Dual-molecule precursor additives (AGS and AHC) similarly coordinate Sn-Pb crystallization, achieving 23.44% single-junction efficiency and a certified tandem PCE of 28.57% (champion 29.36%) [@10.1002/adma.73038], while halide-cation interactions tuned via chlorine and fluorine salts yield wide-bandgap subcells of 20.5% and 20.3% PCE, retaining 87% tandem efficiency after 700 h of MPPT (tandem champion 29.3%) [@10.1002/adma.202522668]. Interface passivation extends to the silicon bottom cell; dark annealing of n-type tunnel oxide passivated contact (n-TOPCon) silicon cells at 300 °C reduces defect-assisted recombination, raising subcell efficiency from 18.92% to 20.23% in four-terminal tandems with a 26.75% champion PCE [@10.1002/ente.70501]. In comparison, idealized one-dimensional numerical simulations (SCAPS-1D) for AgCdF3 absorbers with transition-metal dichalcogenide transport layers demonstrate that reducing bulk defect density to 1e13 cm-3 at 0.4 um thickness defines an efficiency benchmark of 31.92% [@10.1002/ente.70553].
