The single-junction frontier and its remaining losses reveal a sharp physical contradiction. Recent efforts to eliminate non-radiative recombination at the upper perovskite interface in perovskite solar cells (PSCs) divide over one question: whether low-dimensional capping layers suppress interfacial defect states without electronic cost, or establish resistive transport barriers that choke charge extraction. One body of work concludes that low-dimensional surface reconstruction passivates undercoordinated halide vacancies and blocks ion migration without penalizing collection. Dimensional control of low-dimensional perovskite hybrids achieved a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 27.02% (27.21% champion, tested under an unspecified International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability protocol, ISOS-L-2I) [@10.1038/s41467-026-71845-7]. Multivalent ligand-regulated dimensional engineering delivered a certified 25.4% PCE (25.4% champion on 1.1 cm2) [@10.1126/science.aea0656]. In situ formation of a guanidinium derivative two-dimensional/three-dimensional (2D/3D) heterostructure yielded a certified 26.2% PCE (26.49% champion) [@10.1002/anie.8890696]. Conversely, bulky organic spacer cations in two-dimensional (2D) capping networks introduce severe electronic insulation that impedes out-of-plane transport [@10.1002/anie.4293157], and solution-deposited 2D layers also form disordered quantum-well phase distributions that restrict extraction and depress fill factor (FF). To bypass this transport resistance, liquid-derived, solvent-free vapor-mediated dimensional reconstruction eliminated disordered insulating phases. That route achieved a certified 26.15% PCE (26.71% champion on 1 cm2) with a high fill factor [@10.1038/s41467-026-72790-1]. This conflict exposes an unverified premise: whether solution-deposited 2D capping acts as benign passivation or as a resistive bottleneck masked by small test areas. Settling the disagreement requires operando cross-sectional conductive atomic force microscopy synchronized with bias-dependent transient absorption spectroscopy. Together these probes map localized potential drops and carrier transit delays across the heterojunction under working illumination.

Across 692 single-junction publications evaluated over six months, non-radiative losses concentrate at interfaces (176 papers) and defect sites (196 papers), and the buried interface between the transparent conducting oxide and absorber remains a primary locus of non-radiative voltage loss and mechanical debonding. Self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) anchored to nickel oxide (NiOx) or indium tin oxide frequently suffer from island agglomeration, pinhole shunts, and labile coordinate bonds that dissociate under thermal stress. Strengthening substrate anchoring of polymeric self-assembled monolayers suppresses interfacial mechanical delamination, delivering a certified 26.5% PCE (26.9% champion on 1 cm2) [@10.1002/adma.72622]. Hydroxyl interfacial engineering for self-assembled monolayer anchoring on NiOx reduces interfacial trap densities to yield a certified 26.2% PCE (26.6% champion on 0.0524 cm2) [@10.1002/adma.73656]. Modifying assembly kinetics with amino acid hydrochloride-mediated self-assembled monolayers reached a certified 26.33% PCE (26.87% champion on 0.058 cm2) [@10.1002/aenm.70925], while indole-mediated binary monolayers homogenized interfacial assembly and eliminated local potential fluctuations, yielding a certified 26.57% PCE (26.95% champion on 0.0717 cm2) [@10.1038/s41467-026-71301-6]. Dual-site passivation within rationally designed self-assembled monolayers coordinates undercoordinated lead and halide vacancies simultaneously, delivering a certified 26.13% PCE (26.58% champion) [@10.1002/adma.202523249]. To decouple transport from recombination, hole-selective monolayer molecules with spatially separated carrier orbitals and twisted pi-skeletons reached a certified 25.11% PCE (25.61% champion) [@10.1021/acsnano.5c21709]. However, electrostatic shifts alone cannot eliminate chemical vacancies. Interfacial dipole engineering in both inverted (p-i-n) and normal (n-i-p) architectures attained a certified 26.23% PCE (26.18% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-69198-2]. Uncoordinated precursor species at the buried contact still require competitive-binding buried interlayers to maintain reproducible crystallization, and that route achieved a certified 25.8% PCE (26.3% champion on 0.07 cm2) [@10.1126/sciadv.aea7629]. Additionally, heterogeneous nucleation templates on the buried interface enable antisolvent-free deposition on flexible substrates, yielding a certified 26.64% PCE (26.89% champion) [@10.1002/adma.73358]. Stabilizing sputtered NiOx via in situ dissociative adsorption passivation curtails contact pinhole shunts to yield a certified 26.07% PCE (26.31% champion) [@10.1002/adma.73382].

Within the perovskite bulk, non-radiative decay reflects the competition between crystallization kinetics and structural defect formation, addressed by 143 papers on composition. Thermal annealing typically induces volatile organic cation escape and leaves tensile lattice strain from thermal expansion mismatch, accelerating ion migration under bias. Over the six-month survey, research shifted away from static additive screening toward mechanical and dynamic lattice regulation. That shift reflects the recognition that chemical passivation alone has plateaued near the 26.22% median certified efficiency. Molecular press annealing counteracts tensile distortion by applying physical compression during thermal treatment, compressing lattice strain and preventing volatile loss to yield a certified 26.5% PCE (26.6% champion) [@10.1126/science.aea8228]. Contact-triggered molecular interactions direct crystal growth to achieve a certified 25.61% PCE (26.25% champion) [@10.1038/s41560-026-02027-4], whereas mechanochemically reinforced dual-dynamic covalent seeding guides grain orientation and retards phase segregation, producing a certified 26.69% PCE (26.95% champion under ISOS-L-2I testing) [@10.1002/adma.72823]. Chemical additives targeting point defects also suppress non-radiative traps. Fused-ring electron acceptors regulate crystallization and passivate deep electronic states, yielding a certified 26.35% PCE (26.66% champion) [@10.1021/jacs.5c16976]. Multifunctional multi-interaction additives achieve a certified 25.66% PCE (26.1% champion) by coordinating undercoordinated lead ions [@10.1021/acs.nanolett.5c05593]. In wide-bandgap 1.68-electron-volt (1.68-eV) absorbers, grain boundary defect density was reduced through molten phosphonoalkanoic acid-driven passivation in hybrid evaporated/blade-coated deposition, delivering a certified 21.95% PCE (22.53% champion) [@10.1002/aenm.202505854]. Common chemical additives also homogenized optoelectronic properties across the film, yielding a certified 26.4% PCE on a 0.05 cm2 area [@10.1021/jacs.5c18303]. To bypass solvent-induced inhomogeneities, crystal-facet-directed all-vacuum thermal deposition yielded a certified 18.35% PCE (19.3% champion on 0.25 cm2 under ISOS-L-2 operational tracking) [@10.1038/s41563-026-02494-w], while polymer-mediated synthesis achieved scalable air-processed deposition, yielding a certified 26.22% PCE (26.5% champion on 0.06 cm2) [@10.1002/adma.202522508]. That value matches the single-junction median certified efficiency of 26.22%. At the compositional apex of bulk optimization, pure formamidinium lead triiodide (FAPbI3) single-junction architectures achieved the fleet-wide highest certified performance, stating that "We achieved a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 27.6% in FAPbI 3 -based PSCs" [@10.1126/science.aeb9953]. The authors reported no device aperture area.

Carrier collection losses at the upper interface between the perovskite absorber and charge transport layers represent an acute thermodynamic bottleneck. This bottleneck combines non-radiative Shockley-Read-Hall recombination with interfacial chemical degradation. Sputtered metal contacts and electron transport layers (ETLs) induce atomic interdiffusion and chemical reduction of lead halides. An ultrathin hafnium oxide interface stabilization layer, deposited between the fullerene electron transport layer and metal contact, forms an impermeable barrier against volatile iodide out-diffusion and metal atom ingress, achieving a certified 26.6% PCE (27.1% champion) [@10.1126/science.aea3339]. Molecular confinement engineering at the upper interface improves physical contact and blocks ion migration pathways, providing a certified 26.79% PCE (26.84% champion under ISOS-L-1 continuous illumination) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72115-2]. Chemical coordination at this interface is stabilized by bifunctional donor-pi-acceptor (D-pi-A) ligands that direct self-organized interface passivation, achieving a certified 26.37% PCE (26.56% champion under ISOS-L-2 testing) [@10.1021/jacs.6c02548], while spatial molecular engineering of hole semiconductors yielded a certified 26.24% PCE (26.6% champion under ISOS-L-1) [@10.1002/anie.202523665]. For the hole transport layer in inverted and normal architectures, spiro-buckybowl-structured hole-transporting materials optimize interfacial energy alignment and moisture resistance to attain a certified 25.36% PCE (25.54% champion under ISOS-L-2) [@10.1002/anie.202525625]. Conjugated amine ligand-induced interlayers reinforce mechanical durability and electrical conduction, delivering a certified 26.29% PCE (26.33% champion under ISOS-L-3 damp-heat conditions) [@10.1002/adma.73161], and replacing conventional dopants with lithium-based deep eutectics in 2,2',7,7'-tetrakis[N,N-di(4-methoxyphenyl)amino]-9,9'-spirobifluorene (spiro-OMeTAD) suppresses dopant aggregation and ion redistribution, yielding a certified 26.37% PCE (26.53% champion) [@10.1002/anie.9551164]. Photochemical degradation was mitigated by deuteration-functionalized self-assembled monolayers that resist carbon-hydrogen bond cleavage under ultraviolet (UV) irradiation, yielding a certified 25.9% PCE (26.34% champion) [@10.1002/inf2.70140]. Uncertified interface treatments also reported high values. Interfacial bridging engineering delivered an uncertified 26.87% champion PCE on a 0.045 cm2 area with an operational lifetime to 80% of initial performance (T80) of 500 h [@10.1002/anie.9286497]. Dip coating of passivation layers on formamidinium-cesium absorbers achieved an uncertified 22.5% champion PCE on a 0.105 cm2 area with 1200 h T80 stability [@10.1002/solr.70324]. Polar charge extraction was also assisted by ferroelectric heterojunctions, which establish internal polarization to boost the open-circuit voltage-fill factor product, attaining a certified 26.07% PCE (26.62% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-69391-3].

Beyond static defects, degradation mechanisms (investigated in 92 stability-focused studies) originate from dynamic lattice fatigue, operational temperature cycling, and defect propagation under illumination. Dynamic light-induced isomerization of photoswitchable molecular isomers repairs grain boundary bonds under continuous illumination, achieving a certified 26.9% PCE (27.2% champion) [@10.1038/s41560-026-01993-z]. Positional methylation isomer additives alter molecular packing and defect migration barriers, yielding a certified 26.78% PCE (26.84% champion under ISOS-L-2 testing) [@10.1002/adma.202520432]. Regulating lattice strain via molecular buffering dissipates thermomechanical stress under cryogenic cycling. That device yielded a certified 25.94% PCE and established the single-junction family's highest self-reported PCE of 28.01% under ISOS testing [@10.1002/adma.202519339]. In lead-free tin-based perovskites, rapid oxidation of tin from Sn2+ to Sn4+ introduces severe p-type self-doping and defect traps. Multidentate chelation by imine-linked covalent organic frameworks (COFs) suppressed tin oxidation and passivated coordination vacancies to yield a certified 15.88% PCE (16.29% champion) [@10.1002/adma.202519934], while multifunctional reducing molecular dopants delivered a certified 15.06% PCE (16.0% champion) [@10.1002/rar2.70162]. In mixed tin-lead cells, multifunctional MXenes integrated into NiOx hole transport layers retarded tin oxidation and reduced energy offset losses, achieving a certified 20.04% PCE (22.5% champion) with a 600 h T80 lifetime [@10.1002/adfm.202521919]. In all-inorganic cesium lead triiodide (CsPbI3), avoiding the phase transition to the non-photoactive yellow phase remains challenging. Three-in-one self-assembled molecules achieved a certified 20.62% PCE (21.1% champion on 1 cm2) by passivating halide vacancies and optimizing energy alignment [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00820], and moisture-responsive crystallization enabled CsPbI3 fabrication under high humidity to deliver a certified 22.02% PCE (22.6% champion) [@10.1038/s41467-026-69687-4]. In low-dimensional perovskites, synergistic passivation delivered a certified 21.69% PCE (22.76% champion) in quasi-two-dimensional (quasi-2D) cells [@10.1002/adma.72756]. Extending beyond pure halides, molecular lead halide perovskite layers bridging silver bismuth sulfide (AgBiS2) nanocrystals achieved a certified 11.22% PCE on a 0.022 cm2 aperture [@10.1038/s41467-026-72272-4]. Interparticle and interfacial electrostatic interactions maintained efficiency upon scaling, delivering a certified 26.03% PCE (26.47% champion on 0.0727 cm2) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00476].

Reporting practice across the single-junction literature remains deficient on certification, aperture area, and stability. Across 692 evaluated papers (with 161 excluded as simulations or reviews), only 9.1% (63 papers) reported an independently certified efficiency, with a median certified PCE of 26.22% and a certified peak of 27.6% [@10.1126/science.aeb9953], while self-reported claims reached 28.01% [@10.1002/adma.202519339]. Device aperture area was explicitly reported in only 6.6% of papers, and the largest reached 4 cm2. The vast majority relied on sub-0.1 cm2 test structures that minimize series resistance and mask lateral spatial inhomogeneities. Long-term operational stability was quantified via T80 in only 3.9% of studies, reaching a maximum of 4100 h, and merely 25 papers (3.6%) adhered to standardized protocols specified by the International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability. In contrast, all-perovskite tandems achieved a certified 30.3% PCE, with 15 of 60 papers reporting certified values, a largest area of 64.64 cm2, and a longest T80 of 1600 h. Perovskite/silicon and hybrid tandems reached a certified 33.6% PCE, with 19 of 77 papers reporting certified values, a largest area of 16 cm2, and a longest T80 of 1000 h. Perovskite modules attained a certified 26.8% PCE, with 10 of 43 papers reporting certified values, a largest area of 900 cm2, and a longest T80 of 1050 h. Additionally, indoor photovoltaics must be evaluated separately from standard one-sun metrics. The narrow, low-flux indoor emission spectrum permits high conversion percentages at drastically reduced absolute power densities. Wide-bandgap perovskite indoor photovoltaics achieved an indoor power conversion efficiency of 44.36% under indoor illumination, producing an absolute power output of 127.94 µW cm−2 with an open-circuit voltage of 1.091 V [@10.1007/s40820-026-02225-5]. Fully evaporated devices delivered indoor efficiencies of 41.60% at 900 lux and 41.22% at 300 lux illumination [@10.1002/smtd.70654]. A single-junction cell achieved an efficiency of 16.36% under 1000 lx illumination with operational stability exceeding 3000 h under nitrogen [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04174]. A stripped-down hybrid structure achieved an efficiency of 12.53% under 1,000 lux [@10.1016/j.rineng.2026.110377]. Several studies conflated illumination regimes by reporting indoor and one-sun efficiencies together in a single sentence. One report gave an efficiency of 35.54% under indoor light-emitting diode (LED) illumination and 20.28% under standard one sun conditions together [@10.1002/smll.202512808]. Another reported an efficiency of 22.27% under air mass 1.5 global (AM 1.5G) illumination together with indoor conditions [@10.1002/anie.2535666]. A third reported an efficiency of 18.40% under 1 sun together with 34.54% under indoor LED illumination (1000 lux) [@10.1021/acsaem.6c00285].
