Investigations into operational degradation in halide perovskites yield incompatible conclusions regarding the primary cause of open-circuit voltage loss under bias and illumination. One body of work attributes performance decay to electric-field-driven migration of halide ions, with barrier accumulation following at selective extraction contacts. An opposing set of studies instead identifies photo-induced bulk phase segregation and deep trap formation within the absorber as the governing mechanism. Correlative operando nanoscale chemical profiling paired with depth-resolved transient photoluminescence under operational bias is required to resolve whether performance decay originates at transport boundaries or in the bulk lattice. This controversy exemplifies how the frontier of perovskite photovoltaics has shifted away from the simple pursuit of initial power conversion efficiency (PCE). The central challenge now centers on operational endurance: mitigating coupled ionic-electronic degradation, maintaining interfacial band alignments, and arresting defect propagation under continuous operating conditions.

Structuring the contemporary literature by device family provides the only rigorous framework for evaluation. Each architecture operates under distinct physical boundary conditions and occupies a different tier of experimental maturity. The field is divided into four distinct categories: the single-junction frontier and its remaining losses, and all-perovskite tandems, where narrow-bandgap stability is the limit. The remaining two are hybrid tandems with silicon, cigs and organic partners, and modules and the cost of area. Single-junction devices confront intrinsic non-radiative surface recombination and remaining bulk voltage deficits, whereas in all-perovskite tandems performance is restricted by the oxidation susceptibility and high defect density of tin-lead narrow-bandgap subcells. Hybrid tandems pairing perovskites with silicon, copper indium gallium selenide (CIGS), or organic absorbers face current-matching restrictions, parasitic optical absorption, and thermal processing incompatibilities. Finally, module architectures incur substantial geometric fill factor losses, non-uniform deposition across expanded substrates, and resistive dissipation through transparent conductive oxides.

A critical audit of the published literature establishes a sharp contrast between unverified hero metrics and authenticated performance. Across the six-month survey, 3328 indexed works met the scope gate across six months and 884 were read closely. In the single-junction category, covering 692 papers, the best certified PCE was 27.6%, but only 63 of them reported any certified value, with a largest area of 4 cm2 and a longest time to 80% initial performance (T80) of 4100 h. For all-perovskite tandems across 60 papers, the best certified PCE reached 30.3% and 15 of them reported any certified value, with a largest reported active area of 64.64 cm2 and a longest T80 of 1600 h. In the hybrid tandem literature comprising 77 papers on perovskite/silicon and other hybrid tandems, the best certified PCE reached 33.6% and 19 of them reported any certified value, with a largest area of 16 cm2 and a longest T80 of 1000 h. For modules, represented across 43 papers, the best certified PCE was 26.8% and 10 of them reported any certified value. The largest area was 900 cm2 and the longest T80 was 1050 h. Across each category, this review separates what was verified by an accredited laboratory from what was asserted by the authors alone.
