Investigations over the past six months divide over whether narrow-bandgap (NBG) mixed tin-lead (Sn-Pb) sub-cell degradation is an extrinsic artefact of crystallization kinetics or an unavoidable electrochemical consequence of operating redox chemistry. One school of thought attributes Sn-Pb instability to differential crystallization kinetics, wherein tin iodide species nucleate and precipitate ahead of lead counterparts, generating Sn-rich localized phases that host high densities of tin vacancies and metallic tin [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00617] [@10.1038/s41565-026-02165-6]. Synchronizing crystallization rates through chemical hardness engineering achieved a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 30.3% (champion 30.3% in an unspecified device area) [@10.1038/s41565-026-02165-6], and suppressing Sn-rich phase precipitation enabled a certified PCE of 28.53% (champion 28.77%) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00617]. Hybrid seeding-template modulation [@10.1002/anie.5595275] (champion 29.18%, 500 h T80, representing the time to 80% of initial efficiency) and hard Lewis base coordination [@10.1002/adma.73682] (champion 29.44%, 1080 h T80) similarly operate on the premise that arresting disordered nucleation eliminates the primary initiation sites for chemical degradation. In contrast, competing evidence demonstrates that degradation proceeds through autonomous redox cascades that operate independently of initial film morphology [@10.1002/adma.202522942] [@10.1002/anie.4918900]. Reverse-bias operational stresses trigger self-propagating redox cascades between Sn2+ and oxidized halide species, driving structural breakdown even in dense, uniform absorbers [@10.1002/adma.202522942] (champion 29.03%). In parallel, cyclic iodine redox reactions at the buried contact continuously regenerate triiodide that oxidizes Sn2+ to Sn4+ [@10.1002/anie.4918900] (certified 28.79%, champion 29.29%). This mechanism rationalizes why external chemical interventions, such as bio-inspired antioxidants [@10.1038/s41467-026-73210-0] (certified 29.44%, champion 29.95%) or regenerable redox shuttles [@10.1007/s40820-025-02006-6] (champion 28.49%), provide sustained stability where structural templating alone degrades. Settling whether Sn2+ oxidation initiates at residual Sn-rich clusters or emerges uniformly via operando electrochemical cycling requires depth-resolved operando X-ray absorption fine-structure spectroscopy under simultaneous electrical bias and illumination, a measurement absent across these reports.

Efforts to stabilize Sn-Pb absorbers within monolithic two-terminal (2T) tandems concentrate heavily on grain-boundary and interfacial defect passivation. Modulating grain boundary energy through molecular dipoles reduces tin oxidation and defect accumulation, yielding a champion PCE of 29.67% [@10.1002/adma.72755], while synergistic bulk crystallization and defect management achieved 28.57% certified (champion 29.36%) [@10.1002/adma.73038]. Halide-cation coordination chemistries that suppress deep trap states reached a champion PCE of 29.3% [@10.1002/adma.202522668]. Eliminating methylammonium (MA) cations mitigates volatile thermal decomposition, where sustained solvent-extraction channels in MA-free formulations delivered a certified PCE of 29.2% (champion 29.2%, 800 h T80) [@10.1038/s41467-025-68213-2]. Molecular bridges targeting both bulk and interface defects produced a 23.3% champion PCE [@10.1002/adfm.74503]. Recombination losses at the recombination junction and hole transport layer (HTL) interface represent another severe bottleneck. Constructing low-acidity poly(3,4-ethylenedioxythiophene):poly(styrenesulfonate) (PEDOT:PSS) supramolecular complexes with self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) prevents interfacial tin etching, yielding 27.99% certified (champion 28.76%) [@10.1002/adma.73011]. In situ construction of PEDOT HTLs reached a 29.58% champion PCE [@10.1002/adma.202522790]. Concurrently, solution-processed tin oxide (SnOx) buffer layers inserted into inverted sub-cells yielded a certified PCE of 28.34% (champion 28.43%) with a 1000 h T80 [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00227]. Non-contact laser polishing of the perovskite surface suppressed roughness and pinholes, producing 23.47% certified (champion 29.8%) [@10.1038/s41467-026-71017-7]. These interface modifications suppress non-radiative recombination, yet their efficacy remains restricted by the underlying chemical susceptibility of divalent tin.

Current matching requires pairing the NBG sub-cell with an efficient wide-bandgap (WBG) top cell, which introduces secondary instabilities via photo-induced phase segregation into iodide-rich and bromide-rich domains. Aligning interfacial energy levels through asymmetric SAMs with high dipole moments delivered a certified PCE of 28.98% (champion 29.35%), though measured on a miniature 0.05 cm2 aperture [@10.1002/adma.202523338]. Vertically oriented SAM configurations achieved 19.95% certified (champion 28.59%) on an uncertified 0.0396 cm2 device [@10.1002/adma.202521898], whereas titanium carbide (Ti3C2Tx) MXene-SAM hybrids yielded 26.44% certified (champion 26.78%) on 0.08 cm2 [@10.1002/aenm.70966]. Hetero-spatial co-passivation in MA-free WBG absorbers produced a certified PCE of 20.02% (champion 28.34%) [@10.1002/aenm.70755]. Suppressing halide segregation remains reliant on lattice-locking chemistries: halogen bonding networks that regulate bromide crystallization achieved a champion PCE of 28.58% [@10.1002/adfm.74659], crystallographic plane pinning kinetics produced 28.24% [@10.1002/adfm.75395], and stoichiometry-regulated crystallization delivered 29.1% [@10.1021/acsnano.6c04740]. Selective ion-blocking interlayers attained 28.41% champion PCE [@10.1002/aenm.202506634]. Additional passivation strategies, including bulk polymer crosslinking (champion 23.69%) [@10.1002/smll.73404], hydrophobic trifluoromethyl surface anchoring (champion 28.67%) [@10.1002/aenm.70910], aromatic amino acid surface functionalization (champion 29.05%) [@10.1002/smll.73595], and universal composition stabilization (champion 26.75% on 0.09 cm2) [@10.1002/adma.72986], mitigate surface trap densities. However, many of these approaches report champion metrics on sub-0.1 cm2 areas without independent certification.

Critical assessment of the 60 papers published in the all-perovskite tandem family reveals that efficiency gains remain decoupled from standardized validation. Only 25.0% of publications (15 papers) reported an independently certified efficiency, with a median certified PCE of 28.34% and a peak certified value of 30.3% [@10.1038/s41565-026-02165-6], approaching theoretical three-dimensional finite element limits of 30.5% [@10.1002/solr.70302]. The remaining 75.0% relied entirely on self-reported measurements. Active area reporting is equally deficient: only 10.0% of studies stated device aperture area. The vast majority evaluated sub-0.1 cm2 areas, exemplified by flexible tandem modules yielding 27.5% champion PCE on 0.049 cm2 [@10.1002/flm2.70045]. The largest reported device area across the family reached 64.64 cm2 through the incorporation of an antimony oxide buffer layer, which achieved a certified PCE of 27.7% (champion 28.16%) [@10.1038/s41467-026-70848-8]. Operational stability assessments exhibit acute methodological shortcomings. Only 13.3% of papers reported a T80 value, and only 2 papers (3.3%) adhered to standardized International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocols. The longest reported T80 reached 1600 h [@10.1002/pip.70077], but was measured in a mechanically stacked four-terminal (4T) architecture with a 21.87% champion PCE. In monolithic or encapsulated 4T tandems, reported lifetimes include 1210 h under ISOS-L-2 for a 1D/3D heterojunction (certified 21.54%) [@10.1038/s41467-026-72099-z], 1080 h (champion 29.44%) [@10.1002/adma.73682], 1000 h for SnOx-buffered cells (certified 28.34%) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00227], 1000 h using a 1D perovskite interlayer (champion 28.43%) [@10.1021/acsenergylett.5c04243], 800 h for MA-free films (certified 29.2%) [@10.1038/s41467-025-68213-2], 500 h (champion 29.18%) [@10.1002/anie.5595275], and 300 h for 1.77 eV WBG absorbers (champion 27.26%) [@10.1002/solr.70387]. Moisture protection via hydrophobic capping layers tracked under ISOS-L-1 reached a 29.01% champion PCE [@10.1002/adma.72936], while rapid thermal cycling degraded performance to 24.31% champion PCE [@10.1038/s41467-025-68219-w]. In contrast to single-junction perovskites (692 papers, best certified 27.6%, 63 certified, largest area 4 cm2, longest T80 4100 h) and hybrid perovskite/silicon tandems (77 papers, best certified 33.6%, 19 certified, largest area 16 cm2, longest T80 1000 h), all-perovskite tandems trail in operational endurance. Perovskite modules have demonstrated 26.8% certified PCE on 900 cm2 with 1050 h T80 across 43 papers (10 certified). The distribution of investigative axes in this family, dominated by device architecture (34 papers) relative to defects (10 papers), interfaces (10 papers), and stability (5 papers), indicates that architectural tuning has outpaced resolution of the intrinsic chemical oxidation that limits narrow-bandgap sub-cells.
