A fundamental disagreement in inverted perovskite solar cells concerns how self-assembled monolayer (SAM) hole-selective contacts degrade during operation. One study attributes operational failure to out-of-plane molecular desorption from metal oxides that rigid planar conjugation suppresses [@10.1016/j.scib.2026.02.050], while another concludes that degradation proceeds via field-induced lateral aggregation into isolated clusters [@10.1038/s41467-026-72097-1]. Resolving whether molecular detachment or lateral dewetting governs contact failure requires operando bias-dependent sum-frequency generation spectroscopy paired with in situ conductive atomic force microscopy under continuous maximum power point tracking (MPPT) at operational temperatures. Reconciling conflicting degradation mechanisms across the literature is hindered by scarce standardized testing: when stability assessments use disparate stress regimes, distinguishing true stabilization from divergent testing conditions becomes impossible.

Comparing reporting practices across device categories demonstrates that the families do not verify themselves to the same standard. These detection rates reflect automated parsing of published abstracts rather than exhaustive laboratory audits. Omitting a testing protocol or active area in an abstract does not establish that researchers failed to execute the measurement. Across the 2238 indexed abstracts, 52.1% stated a power conversion efficiency (PCE) and 8.5% reported independent certification. However, these pooled averages conceal stark disparities among device families: single-junction devices encompass 692 papers, yet only 53.0% stated an efficiency and 9.1% reported independent certification. Single junctions dominate volume but exhibit the lowest certification rate, relying on unverified laboratory scans susceptible to capacitive transients and spectral mismatch. In contrast, multi-junction and module families face higher verification demands to justify architectural complexity: all-perovskite tandems (60 papers) stated efficiency in 71.7% and independently certified 25.0%. Perovskite/silicon and other hybrid tandems (77 papers) stated efficiency in 64.9% and certified 24.7%, and modules (43 papers) stated efficiency in 72.1% and certified 23.3%. Certification rates in tandems and modules are nearly triple that of single junctions, establishing a divided evidentiary standard.

Geometric reporting similarly divides module architectures from cell-level studies: modules report active area in 72.1% of papers, compared to only 6.6% for single junctions, 10.0% for all-perovskite tandems, and 15.6% for hybrid tandems. Because module performance is governed by geometric dead area and interconnect resistance, area disclosure is unavoidable; in contrast, single-junction literature routinely omits aperture size, obscuring whether efficiencies reflect scalable areas or microscopic crystals. Operational durability tracking reveals further divergent priorities: all-perovskite tandems lead time to 80% of initial performance (T80) reporting at 13.3%, compared to 3.9% for single junctions, 4.7% for modules, and 2.6% for hybrid tandems, a ranking that reflects scrutiny of narrow-bandgap mixed tin-lead subcells vulnerable to tin oxidation during operation. Standardized degradation protocols remain universally rare: an International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol was specified in only 3.6% of single-junction, 3.3% of all-perovskite tandem, 1.3% of hybrid tandem, and 7.0% of module abstracts. Hybrid tandems exhibit the sharpest disparity: despite achieving 24.7% certification, only 1.3% specified an ISOS protocol and 2.6% reported a T80 lifetime, substituting unstandardized illumination for standardized aging.

Exemplary reporting occurs where geometry, certification, and operational protocols converge, and modules establish reproducible baselines by reporting aperture dimensions, certified PCE, and standardized degradation conditions. One module combined an active area of 100 cm2 with a certified PCE of 25.55% under ISOS-L-1 tracking [@10.1126/sciadv.aec3238], another reported 100 cm2 with a certified PCE of 23.55% under ISOS-D-1 storage [@10.1038/s41467-026-69685-6], and a third achieved a certified 26.8% on a 655.2 cm2 module [@10.1002/adma.73855]. Standardized tandem benchmarks report certified efficiencies on designated apertures: hybrid tandems reached certified 33.6% on 1 cm2 [@10.1126/science.aef5355] and certified 32.1% on 1 cm2 with a T80 of 1000 h [@10.1002/anie.5162616], while all-perovskite tandems reached certified 27.7% on 64.64 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-70848-8] and a T80 of 1600 h [@10.1002/pip.70077]. Rigorous single-junction reporting achieves certified 27.6% PCE [@10.1126/science.aeb9953], certified 26.78% under ISOS-L2 [@10.1002/adma.202520432], or a T80 of 4100 h [@10.1002/adfm.76110]. In sharp contrast, absent reporting leaves claims unanchored: uncertified single-junction hero cells reach 28.49% PCE while omitting certification, active area, and standardized aging [@10.1007/s40820-025-02006-6]. Uncertified records remain confined to sub-0.1 cm2 cells, including 0.072 cm2 at 26.2% PCE [@10.1038/s41467-025-68207-0] and 0.058 cm2 at 26.87% PCE [@10.1002/aenm.70925], where edge collection distorts planar transport [@10.1038/s41467-025-68207-0] [@10.1002/aenm.70925]. Conversely, module studies report a 900 cm2 aperture without independent certification [@10.1002/adfm.202532205]. Without uniform reporting standards across device families, comparing headline efficiencies mistakes divergent verification rigor for genuine physical progress.
