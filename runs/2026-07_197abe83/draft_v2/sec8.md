The current literature focuses disproportionately on single-junction optimization, even though certified single junctions have reached 33.1% while the best certified perovskite/silicon tandem this month reached 33.1%, demonstrating that the subcell that limits the tandem is not the one most papers are optimising. In tandem configurations, overall efficiency gains depend on the wide-bandgap top cell, which suffers from severe voltage deficits and phase instability. Although numerous chemical treatments claim to achieve halide homogenisation in wide-bandgap absorbers, evidence that this homogenisation suppresses phase segregation under real operating bias remains thin. Most evaluations rely on uncertified open-circuit photoluminescence or passive light soaking, ignoring how carrier extraction and electric fields drive iodide-bromide demixing into low-bandgap iodide clusters that pin the open-circuit voltage. Operando nanoscale photoluminescence spectroscopy coupled with depth-resolved Kelvin probe force microscopy under active current extraction at maximum power point conditions would determine whether halide mixing persists when carrier gradients and operational electric fields act simultaneously.

A second critical gap concerns buried-interface chemistry that survives thermal cycling rather than only improving initial efficiency. Interfacial modifiers such as self-assembled monolayers, phosphonic acids, and Lewis bases improve open-circuit voltage on unaged substrates, but these claims almost universally rest on small devices evaluated at room temperature. Under thermal cycling, mismatched thermal expansion coefficients between the perovskite absorber and rigid transport contacts induce severe biaxial lattice strain. Evidence conflicts on whether interfacial molecules relieve this stress or promote delamination through weak electrostatic bonding and thermal desorption. In situ grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering paired with operando transient photovoltage measurements during continuous temperature cycling would directly monitor dynamic lattice strain relaxation and interface recombination velocities, identifying chemical anchoring motifs that maintain electronic contact across thermal extremes.

Defect dynamics also remain poorly understood because ion-migration timescales are measured in the dark rather than under load. Current literature routinely extracts activation energies from dark impedance spectroscopy or capacitance-voltage sweeps. These dark measurements fail to capture the influence of photogenerated charge carriers on defect formation enthalpies and electrostatic screening. Under operating conditions, large photogenerated carrier densities screen internal electric fields, while chemical potential gradients accelerate mobile halide vacancy redistribution toward selective contacts. Measuring timescales in the dark obscures how mobile ions alter carrier recombination during active operation. Transient optoelectronic impedance spectroscopy and intensity-modulated photovoltage measurements collected under continuous illumination at the maximum power point would extract mobile ion drift velocities and interfacial accumulation rates under load, providing realistic kinetic parameters for operational transport.

A pronounced disconnect persists between small-area interfacial engineering and scalable module manufacturing. Passivation that lifts Voc on a 0.1 cm2 cell rarely survives slot-die coating at module area. Passivation recipes designed for rapid spin-coating and antisolvent quenching depend on non-equilibrium crystallization. In slot-die coating, slow drying kinetics and fluid-flow instabilities cause passivating molecules to aggregate into inhomogeneous clusters, leaving pinholes and unpassivated boundaries. The literature reflects a profound neglect of this transition: only 27 of 648 works addressed scale-up at all, and the largest reported aperture area was 818 cm2, which is two to three orders of magnitude below a commercial module. High-speed operando photoluminescence imaging combined with in situ grazing-incidence small-angle X-ray scattering during slot-die printing would correlate ink drying dynamics and ligand distribution with spatial open-circuit voltage maps across large areas, establishing scalable processing boundaries for defect passivation.

Operational durability assessments are further obscured by the absence of a shared degradation protocol that would make lifetime claims comparable. Published studies alternate between unencapsulated shelf-life tests, open-circuit illumination, and room-temperature tracking without environmental control. Out of 169 closely read papers, only 4 reported a T80 lifetime, and the longest was 9800 h, which is short against a 25 year module warranty. These unstandardized, unlabelled stability evaluations disguise whether failure stems from intrinsic lattice degradation, interfacial chemical reaction, or contact corrosion. Adopting standardized consensus aging protocols that combine continuous maximum power point tracking under illumination with simultaneous thermal cycling and reverse-bias stress, accompanied by operando electrical impedance tracking, would isolate contact degradation from bulk absorber decay and enable rigorous lifetime comparisons.

The literature would become substantially more useful if research shifted away from reporting incremental efficiency gains on minute spin-coated cells toward mechanistic endurance under operational conditions. Priority must be given to operando evaluation of wide-bandgap subcells under bias, thermally stable buried interfaces that withstand cyclical strain, scalable deposition diagnostics that preserve passivation over large areas, and standardized accelerated aging metrics. Establishing these practices will bridge the divide between laboratory cell performance and commercially viable photovoltaic modules.
