The efficiency loss observed when expanding from small cells to large modules arises from coupled mechanisms: series resistance across interconnections and spatial non-uniformity within the absorber. Quantitative hyperspectral luminescence imaging on a 64 cm2 module demonstrates that resistive transport and interconnection losses dominate upscaling, with extrinsic integration losses matching intrinsic material losses in magnitude [@10.1002/solr.70375]. This partitioning defines monolithic layouts, where dead-zone scribing and transparent conductive oxide resistance depress the fill factor. Recent fabrications reflect these constraints across geometric scales. A small module reached 26.7% across 100 cm2 [@10.1126/science.aef1969]. Intermediate apertures yielded an uncertified champion efficiency of 27.8%, certified at 27.3%, across 625 cm2 [@10.1126/science.aed8175]. An efficiency of 27.12% was achieved across 655.2 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-74018-8]. Broader footprints recorded 27.31% across 684.75 cm2 [@10.1002/adma.73807]. At 766 cm2, efficiency reached 27.31% [@10.1126/sciadv.aeg1456]. Performance fell to 26.53% across 818 cm2 [@10.1021/jacs.6c06932]. Mitigating this area penalty requires eliminating microscopic defects that compound extrinsic resistive drops.

Controlling crystallization kinetics during scalable deposition directly addresses intrinsic material heterogeneity. In ambient blade coating, moisture and uneven solvent evaporation destabilize wet precursor films prior to annealing. A surface-confined protection strategy using DPTA stabilizes pre-annealing crystallization for air-processed blade-coated methylammonium-free films, yielding small devices with 26.14% efficiency, certified at 25.75%, that retain 93.11% of initial efficiency after 1300 h under continuous 1 sun illumination at maximum power point [@10.1038/s41467-026-75053-1]. Translating this strategy to modules on a 100 × 100 mm2 substrate drops efficiency to an uncertified 22.72% [@10.1038/s41467-026-75053-1], confirming that surface capping alone cannot resolve drying gradients across decimetre scales. Precursor degradation during storage introduces further chemical defects; suppressing organic-cation deprotonation via anion-π interactions with 1,3,5-tris(trifluoromethyl)benzene yields small cells with 25.76% efficiency from precursors aged seven days in ambient conditions and 23.88% from three-month-aged solutions, against an uncertified champion efficiency of 26.07% [@10.1002/adfm.77211]. These precursor gains remain restricted to small-area n-i-p devices without module testing. Modulating drying through vapor-phase annealing establishes a uniform thermal-humidity field promoting bottom-up crystallization, but additive-free inverted devices reach only an uncertified champion efficiency of 22.15% on small cells [@10.1021/acsami.6c07418]. For narrow-bandgap tin-lead absorbers, where oxidation and rapid nucleation induce high trap densities, dual-additive coordination using FATU and GlyHCl produces films with over 800 nm grains and minimized trap density [@10.1002/aenm.71336]. This lifts single-junction blade-coated efficiency to 21.02% by raising open-circuit voltage from 0.797 to 0.850 V, supporting two-terminal tandems with 27.84% champion and 27.33% stabilised efficiency that retain more than 75% of initial efficiency after 680 h continuous MPP tracking [@10.1002/aenm.71336].

A second contributor to the area penalty is non-radiative recombination and extraction resistance at charge-transport interfaces. In inverted layouts, evaporated fullerene layers exhibit pinholes and thermal vulnerability over wide areas. Asymmetric lamellar templating using indol-3-ylethylammonium iodide spacer cations creates lateral electrostatic anisotropy that aligns the lamellar interphase at the perovskite/C60 junction, suppressing interfacial recombination while preserving electron extraction [@10.1002/adma.74316]. Although small cells reach a certified efficiency of 26.94% and retain >93% efficiency after 1000 h at 85 °C and 85% after 1800 h under continuous 1-sun MPP tracking, scaling to a 25 cm2 monolithic module yields a certified efficiency of 23.21% [@10.1002/adma.74316]. This certified decrease highlights that interface templating alone cannot overcome the fill factor losses inherent to monolithic module interconnection. Replacing fullerene contacts with the n-type nonfullerene conjugated polymer 2PB-T incorporating perylene bisimide units provides conformal coverage and thermal stability across extended substrates [@10.1126/science.aed8175]. Small-area devices retain more than 98.6% of initial PCE after 1752 h of continuous MPPT at 85 °C in air, while a 625-cm2 module achieves a certified efficiency of 27.3% (champion 27.8%) and maintains 96.9% of initial PCE after 5900 h of outdoor operation [@10.1126/science.aed8175]. This demonstrates that conjugated polymer contacts simultaneously curb interfacial recombination and resolve outdoor degradation across decimetre-scale apertures.

Addressing spatial losses at scale also depends on automated characterization and life-cycle economics. Automated SEM analysis through PerovSegNet segments lead iodide, perovskite, and defect domains with a mean Average Precision of 87.25%, exceeding baseline methods by 4.08% at 25.22% lower computational cost [@10.1038/s41524-026-02211-w]. High-throughput segmentation provides a route to screen crystallization defects prior to module scribing. Minimizing these losses governs economic viability; life-cycle assessments confirm that power conversion efficiency and degradation rates are the main drivers of energetic performance in inverted cells, where cradle-to-grave analysis yields an energy payback time of 0.53 years, an energy return on investment of 9.50, a levelized cost of electricity of 0.29 €/kWh, a carbon footprint of 0.20 kg CO2 eq./kWh, and a cumulative energy demand of 4.88 MJ/kWh at a production cost of 0.67 €/cm2 [@10.1016/j.jclepro.2026.148924]. At multi-terawatt scale, bottom-up cost modeling indicates that recycling perovskite glass can reduce recycling costs by up to $7 per square meter with scalability up to 4 TW, managing the 10 to 25 million tons of glass projected annually in Europe from 2035 to 2070 [@10.1007/s44498-026-00134-9].
