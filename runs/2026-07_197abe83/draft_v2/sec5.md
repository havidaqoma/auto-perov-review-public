Monolithic tandem integration offers the primary route to surpass the ~29.4% theoretical efficiency limit of single-junction crystalline silicon [@10.1021/acs.chemrev.5c01014]. Yet, practical deployment remains constrained by coupled physical bottlenecks: phase segregation and lattice strain in wide-bandgap top cells, and tin oxidation and compositional inhomogeneity in narrow-bandgap bottom cells. In-operando thermal cycling between -100 and +100 °C under AM0 illumination reveals that wide-bandgap subcells undergo severe phase segregation above 50 °C with only partial recovery during 45-min dark cooling, escalating nonradiative losses and driving luminescence coupling from wide- to narrow-bandgap absorbers [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00992]. These thermal-optical instabilities demonstrate that phase segregation demands intrinsic lattice and crystallization control rather than external encapsulation alone. Drift-diffusion modelling across 1202 simulated cells (601 pairs) indicates that mobile ions have only a small impact on steady-state performance in efficient p-i-n and n-i-p devices [@10.48550/arxiv.2607.25951], indicating that operational losses originate primarily from interfacial barriers and phase segregation rather than internal ionic motion.

Thermodynamic stabilization of wide-bandgap lattices has centered on octahedral stiffening and halide homogenization. Incorporating Sr2+ into the lattice strengthens metal-halide bonding, forming a more rigid octahedral framework with weakened electron-phonon coupling [@10.1002/aenm.71317]. Fully-textured perovskite/silicon tandems using this Sr/Pb alloying achieve an uncertified champion efficiency of 31.66%, with encapsulated devices exhibiting less than 7% performance loss after more than 1391 h of maximum power point tracking (MPPT), projecting a T80 of approximately 6785 h [@10.1002/aenm.71317]. Complementing B-site alloying, molecular-extrusion-driven halogen homogenization with TPB-Br precursors suppresses phase segregation and nonradiative recombination, delivering 23.63% efficiency in a 1.68 eV sub-cell and an uncertified 32.03% tandem efficiency on 1.05 cm2 [@10.1002/anie.5530609]. Unencapsulated TPB-Br cells retain 90.1%, 81.2%, and 93.4% of initial efficiency after 1200 h of storage, thermal aging, and light soaking, respectively [@10.1002/anie.5530609]. At grain boundaries, ionic-liquid/polyurethane conductive polymer bridges passivate defects, suppress halide segregation, and increase carrier diffusion length [@10.1002/adma.74183]. Rigid wide-bandgap single-junction cells reach 20.85% efficiency with T90 exceeding 1000 h, while flexible perovskite-organic tandems reach an uncertified 25.92% champion efficiency, retaining 81% performance after 10,000 bending cycles and 87% after 1000 h of continuous light in rigid configurations [@10.1002/adma.74183].

Buried interfaces and precursor coordination govern wide-bandgap crystallization. Inserting a solvent-resistant 3-TMA layer between the self-assembled monolayer (SAM) and the photo-active layer regulates crystallization through interfacial hydrogen bonding [@10.1002/anie.1676645]. This molecular interlayer yields single-junction efficiencies of 23.17% (1.67 eV) and 19.61% (1.84 eV), resulting in a certified tandem efficiency of 32.13% (champion 33.21%) and retaining 92.3% of initial performance after 1000 h under continuous 1 sun illumination at room temperature [@10.1002/anie.1676645]. For 1-μm-thick perovskite absorbers in tandem architectures, screening more than 8000 solvents with a retrieval-augmented large language model identified gamma-valerolactone (GVL) as a non-toxic cosolvent that strongly coordinates FA+ cations, retarding nucleation and promoting oriented micrometer-scale grain growth to reach an uncertified 32.5% champion tandem efficiency [@10.1007/s40820-026-02291-9]. Inkjet printing similarly applies GVL as the primary low-toxicity solvent with DMSO as cosolvent, exceeding 17% efficiency in single-junction cells and surpassing an uncertified 28% efficiency in perovskite/silicon tandems [@10.1002/advs.76463].

In narrow-bandgap Sn-Pb absorbers, device efficiency is limited by rapid Sn2+ oxidation and disparate crystallization kinetics between Sn and Pb. Metallic zero-valent tin nanoparticles serve as redox shuttles that eliminate Sn4+ impurities during precursor preparation and crystallization [@10.1016/j.nanoen.2026.112222]. This approach achieves a certified efficiency of 23.26% on a 4.05 mm2 aperture, maintaining over 90% of initial performance after 500 hours of continuous MPPT under AM 1.5 G, with unencapsulated devices retaining over 80% efficiency after six days in 60% relative humidity [@10.1016/j.nanoen.2026.112222]. Chemical Sn4+ elimination can also occur via halide exchange: inert metal chlorides (MnCl2 or ZnCl2) react with SnI4 to produce volatile SnCl4 while forming MnI2 or ZnI2 grain-boundary passivation layers, inducing partial dissolution-recrystallization that enlarges grains and relieves residual stress [@10.1021/acsnano.5c21750]. The champion uncertified ZnCl2-modified device reaches 23.06% efficiency and retains 90% initial performance after 1000 h of continuous illumination in N2 [@10.1021/acsnano.5c21750].

Interlayer design has enabled multi-junction and flexible device architectures. A graphene oxide/SAM bilayer forms a chemically benign hole contact that yields 22.1% efficiency and an open-circuit voltage of 0.88 V in single-junction Sn-Pb cells, retaining 90% of initial efficiency after 770 h of operation at 25 °C [@10.1016/j.joule.2026.102575]. This bilayer architecture enables triple-junction all-perovskite cells with self-assembling hole contacts in all subcells to attain a champion efficiency of 27.3% and a stabilized efficiency of 27.0% [@10.1016/j.joule.2026.102575]. In flexible formats, monolithic perovskite/CIGS tandems fabricated by a two-step hybrid co-evaporation-solution process eliminate NiOx hole transport layers, achieving a certified 25.9% efficiency (champion 26.4%) on flexible polyimide substrates alongside 26.01% with an open-circuit voltage of 1.85 V on rigid glass [@10.1002/aenm.71267].

Device scaling, extreme-environment resilience, and circuit architecture define the practical transition to commercial tandems. Stabilizing monolithic wide-bandgap perovskite/silicon tandems with an ionic liquid additive yields a certified 27.49% efficiency on a 12.56 cm2 aperture, with the top cell reaching 24.0% efficiency (certified 23.58%) [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6600]. These monolithic devices display radiation tolerance for space photovoltaics, retaining nearly 80% performance after 1 MeV electron irradiation at 1×10^14 e/cm2 and 93% after 150 keV proton irradiation at 1×10^12 p/cm2, alongside stable operation generating up to 387.4 mW at ~30 km altitude during balloon flights [@10.1126/sciadv.aef6600]. Circuit architecture also governs operational tolerance: while nominal design of two-terminal CsPbBr3/CsPbI3/MASnI3 tandems targets a 0.009 mA/cm2 current mismatch, manufacturing thickness variations cause mismatches exceeding 2 mA/cm2 [@10.1002/solr.70434]. Surrogate modelling shows that an intentionally detuned design accepting a 0.516 mA/cm2 nominal mismatch attains a predicted efficiency of 29.61% versus 28.86% for the nominal design under thickness fluctuations [@10.1002/solr.70434]. Alternatively, three-terminal configurations with back-contact silicon circumvent current mismatch entirely and suppress parasitic absorption [@10.1002/eem2.70481]. The high voltages of perovskite/silicon tandems also support chemical synthesis: powering an iodine-doped copper electrolyzer with a tandem and a single-junction silicon cell achieves an 8.64% solar-to-C2+ conversion efficiency [@10.1126/sciadv.aec6434].
