Operational degradation in perovskite photovoltaics originates from coupled mechanical, chemical, and field-driven processes that initiate at interfaces. Depth-resolved profiling under ISOS-L-1 and ISOS-L-2 conditions reveals that photo-induced degradation begins near buried and top interfaces before penetrating the bulk, converting elastic lattice behavior into viscoelastic deformation [@10.1002/solr.70421]. Dynamic stress fluctuations accelerate this lattice degradation [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01837]. In situ measurements confirm that tensile stress relaxes through diffusion-mediated inelastic deformation, whereas pre-relaxing this stress reduces moisture uptake by up to an order of magnitude [@10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-128tn/v2]. Stabilizing the buried interface against structural disruption is critical. Coassembling Me-4PACz with CzOTf rigidifies molecular orientation, yielding certified single-cell efficiency of 27.31%, 32.84% in perovskite/silicon tandems, and 21.54% on a 766 cm2 module, with 92% efficiency retention after 2000 h of continuous light soaking under ISOS-L-1 [@10.1126/sciadv.aeg1456]. Asymmetric benzothienoindole self-assembled monolayers similarly improve molecular packing and alignment, delivering an uncertified champion efficiency of 26.76%, with 25.38% on 1 cm2 and 20.59% on a 655.2 cm2 module, but without reported operational stability under continuous illumination [@10.1002/anie.1678852].

At the top interface, non-radiative recombination and halide escape trigger decay unless arrested by low-dimensional capping phases. Multidentate fullerene PF2C stabilizes surface PEABr, suppressing defect formation so that encapsulated devices retain 98.7% of initial efficiency after 2100 h of maximum power point tracking and 95.8% after 1800 h at 85 °C under ISOS-L-3 conditions [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01517]. Synthesizing phase-pure n = 1 2D perovskite caps without thermal activation creates a mixed-orientation architecture retaining over 98% performance after 2000 h of maximum power point tracking, matching an uncertified champion efficiency of 26.61% [@10.1002/adma.74247]. In hole-transport-layer-free carbon devices, an in situ 2D Ruddlesden-Popper layer formed via PEACl preserves over 93% efficiency after 550 h in humid air, though efficiency is limited to 17.75% with a fill factor of 69.02% [@10.1002/solr.70447]. Interfacial hydrophobic networks protect unencapsulated devices in ambient air, achieving T95 exceeding 2000 h and retaining 95% efficiency after 10,000 bending cycles in 23.03%-efficient flexible cells [@10.1007/s40820-026-02295-5]. External barrier layers provide complementary protection; inkjet-printed SiOxNy encapsulation films demonstrate 93% ± 5% efficiency retention after 1000 h of storage at 25°C/60%RH under ISOS-D-1 [@10.1002/smll.74816].

Beyond interfacial degradation, internal operational failure is governed by mobile ions and bias-induced phase segregation. In n-i-p configurations, lithium ions from Li-TFSI migrate across the hole-transport layer during illumination; substituting volatile AM-TFSI eliminates ionic residues and delivers 25.53% efficiency [@10.1002/smll.74289]. In wide-bandgap mixed-halide perovskites, trapped-charge accumulation under bias triggers severe halide segregation [@10.1002/aenm.71358]. Operating wide-bandgap cells under maximum stability point tracking prevents trapped-charge buildup and maintains the alloyed phase, retaining approximately 90% power after 700 h, whereas standard maximum power point tracking induces rapid phase segregation [@10.1002/aenm.71358]. Chemically raising the migration barrier provides an alternative: incorporating 1,4-phenylenediamine dihydriodide into a 1.93-eV absorber suppresses halide demixing, yielding certified efficiency of 26.18% with an open-circuit voltage of 3.148 V on a 1.17 cm2 triple-junction tandem that retains 96% performance after 1500 h of ambient illumination [@10.1038/s41467-026-75305-0]. Conversely, incorporating rubidium into CsDMAMAFA 1.67 eV absorbers homogenizes cesium distribution, reaching 22.51% efficiency, yet unencapsulated devices maintain 88% after only 400 h under ISOS-L-1, leaving long-term phase stability unproven [@10.1021/acsnano.6c04084].

Defect control during crystallization further dictates ion migration along grain boundaries. Amino acid amide hydrochloride retards FAPbI3 crystallization by coordinating Pb2+ and hydrogen-bonding FA+, expanding grains to 1.11 ± 0.01 µm; nevertheless, encapsulated inverted cells retain only 82.44% of initial efficiency after 1000 h of maximum power point tracking [@10.1002/adfm.77164]. Aromatic carbonyl molecules with electron-donating cores passivate defects, achieving 26.12% efficiency on 0.09 cm2 and retaining 95% after 1200 h at 65 °C under ISOS-L-2 conditions [@10.1002/anie.5216345]. However, this claim rests on a tiny 0.09 cm2 area, and efficiency drops to 22.41% on 14.0 cm2 [@10.1002/anie.5216345]. Large-area printing requires additives that buffer moisture during crystallization. Phase-locking networks enable homogeneous crystallization of α-phase FAPbI3, allowing printed 100 cm2 modules to reach 21.5% efficiency on rigid substrates and retain more than 90% of initial performance after 1500 hours of tracking at 85 °C in ambient air [@10.1126/science.aef1969]. Lewis base additives enable blade-coated 12 cm2 mini-modules processed at 20-40% relative humidity to retain over 85% efficiency after 900 h at 65 °C [@10.1021/acsami.6c09922]. Flexible module stability continues to lag behind rigid architectures under mechanical stress [@10.1002/inf2.70159].

The evidence base for operational stability remains compromised by non-standardized testing. Across 169 closely read papers, only four reported a T80 lifetime: 9800 h [@10.1002/adma.74243], 6785 h [@10.1002/aenm.71317], 720 h [@10.1021/acsaem.6c01637], and 200 h [@10.1002/smll.74531]. Only six investigations specified an ISOS protocol: ISOS-O-1 [@10.1126/sciadv.aee4175], ISOS-L-1 [@10.1021/acsnano.6c04084, @10.1126/sciadv.aeg1456], ISOS-L-3 [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01517], ISOS-L-1l [@10.1021/jacs.6c06932], and continuous maximum power point tracking [@10.26599/pom.2026.9140147]. Most published studies terminate tests prematurely after 400 h to 2000 h while efficiency remains above 85% or 95%, concealing wear-out kinetics. Physical degradation pathways differ fundamentally depending on applied stress. Unilluminated tests such as ISOS-O-1 isolate encapsulation barrier quality but fail to activate photocarrier recombination or field-induced ion drift [@10.1126/sciadv.aee4175]. Room-temperature continuous light soaking under ISOS-L-1 captures carrier trapping and interfacial charge accumulation, but suppresses thermally activated ion diffusion and electrode reactions [@10.1021/acsnano.6c04084, @10.1126/sciadv.aeg1456]. Only protocols combining illumination, electrical load, and elevated temperatures of 65 °C or 85 °C, such as ISOS-L-2 or ISOS-L-3, simultaneously engage coupled chemical, ionic, and interfacial failure modes [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01517, @10.1002/anie.5216345, @10.1126/science.aef1969]. Machine-learning analysis of multimodal luminescence time series indicates that global luminescence provides the primary predictive indicator of degradation [@10.1002/solr.70433]. Without universal adoption of standardized ISOS protocols and long-term T80 metrics on large-area devices, reported stability milestones remain unrepresentative of operational lifespans.
