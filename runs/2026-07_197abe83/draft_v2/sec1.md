The maturation of metal-halide perovskite photovoltaics has reshaped the fundamental objectives of solar energy research. With perovskite single junctions now certified above 27% and perovskite/silicon tandems above 33%, the open problems have shifted from raw efficiency to whether a claimed efficiency is verifiable, whether it survives at module area, and whether it survives operation. Efficiency records achieved on small-area cells under idealized conditions do not provide reliable indicators of commercial viability when decoupled from certified testing protocols, reproducible interfacial chemistry, and standardized degradation analyses.

The literature of July 2026 reflects this transition, producing a heavy volume of reports evaluating synthetic, structural, and operational strategies. In total, 648 indexed works met the scope gate in July 2026 and 181 were read closely to identify the physical and chemical mechanisms governing device behavior. These studies reveal that efficiency and stability are governed by coupled physical processes rather than isolated bulk properties. Understanding these phenomena requires examining four mechanistic threads alongside the practical constraints imposed by scalable manufacturing.

The first thread concerns buried-interface and contact chemistry. Charge extraction efficiency and non-radiative recombination rates depend heavily on the chemical environment established between the bottom transport layer and the growing perovskite absorber. Substrate-induced strain, interfacial dipole formation, redox reactions, and uncoordinated surface species at these hidden junctions govern carrier transport long before top-surface passivating agents are deposited. Resolving the coordination chemistry, energy-level alignment, and mechanical adhesion at these buried contacts is essential for suppressing interfacial losses and preventing mechanical delamination under thermal stress.

The second thread addresses defect tolerance and ion migration. Although halide perovskites exhibit shallow point defect formation energies that suppress deep trap-assisted recombination, mobile ionic species, particularly halide vacancies and interstitial ions, cause electric field screening, phase segregation, and local lattice distortion. Under applied electric fields and illumination, mobile defects accumulate at grain boundaries and selective contacts, altering extraction barriers and opening non-radiative recombination pathways. Decoupling electronic trap passivation from ionic transport suppression represents a necessary requirement for extending carrier lifetimes and eliminating current-voltage hysteresis.

The third thread centers on wide-bandgap absorbers designed for tandem integration. Widening the optical bandgap through mixed-halide compositions introduces photoinduced phase segregation, where iodide-rich and bromide-rich domains separate under illumination and trap carriers within lower-gap regions. Stabilizing these mixed-anion lattices against phase separation requires precise control over compositional homogeneity, cation alloying, and local lattice strain. Without resolving these thermodynamic and kinetic drivers, open-circuit voltage deficits in wide-bandgap subcells will continue to limit practical tandem performance.

The fourth thread evaluates operational stability under coupled environmental stressors, including heat, moisture, light soaking, and electrical bias. Operational reliability requires stable crystallographic phases, chemically inert contact materials, and barrier architectures capable of retaining volatile decomposition species. Crucially, scale-up acts as the governing physical constraint that couples all four threads. Deposition methods compatible with large-area manufacturing alter crystallization kinetics, defect densities, and contact uniformities compared to laboratory-scale processing. A passivation strategy or compositional mixture that succeeds across a fraction of a square centimeter frequently fails when translated to large modules due to drying gradients, non-uniform nucleation, and resistance losses along interconnects.

Evaluating progress across these domains requires rigorous critical scrutiny. A substantial proportion of reported efficiency gains rests on uncertified in-house testing, small active areas, unmasked aperture geometries, or stability evaluations that omit standard aging protocols, temperature control, and continuous maximum power point tracking. The month's headline numbers are strong, but the evidence supporting them is unevenly reported, and this review separates the two. By distinguishing physical mechanisms from measurement artifacts, this assessment identifies genuine advances toward commercial operation while isolating unverified claims and experimental ambiguity.
