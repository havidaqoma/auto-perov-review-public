This month, the stability axis appears in 66 of 648 works (10.2%), of which 25 were read closely, and the scale-up axis appears in 27 of 648 works (4.2%), of which 12 were read closely. Because this is the first month in the series, no month-over-month comparison is possible.

Certified records keep rising while active areas grow. The nonfullerene polymer 2PB-T replaces unstable fullerene electron transport layers in inverted cells, with a champion 27.8% and certified 27.3% [@10.1126/science.aed8175]. Coassembly of Me-4PACz with CzOTf stabilizes the buried interface and gives certified 27.31% single cells, 32.84% perovskite/HJT-Si tandems, and 21.54% on a 766 cm2 module [@10.1126/sciadv.aeg1456]. FATU and GlyHCl dual additives yield Sn-Pb films with grains over 800 nm, a certified 27.33% tandem, and a blade-coated single junction at 21.02% with VOC raised from 0.797 to 0.850 V [@10.1002/aenm.71336]. Air-blade MA-free cells reach 26.14%, certified at 25.75% [@10.1038/s41467-026-75053-1]. Phase locking of alpha-phase FAPbI3 during large-area printing gave 100 cm2 rigid and flexible modules at 21.5% and 19.5% [@10.1126/science.aef1969]. Asymmetric BTI-derived SAMs reached 25.38% on 1 cm2 and 20.59% on a 655.2 cm2 module [@10.1002/anie.1678852]. Indol-3-ylethylammonium spacer cations align the perovskite/C60 interphase, and a 25 cm2 module achieved a certified 23.21% [@10.1002/adma.74316]. TBPAA cells gave 26.12% on 0.09 cm2 and 22.41% on 14.0 cm2 [@10.1002/anie.5216345]. Phenylenediamine dihydriodide in a 1.93-eV perovskite raised the certified triple-junction efficiency to 26.18% with a Voc of 3.148 V on 1.17 cm2 [@10.1038/s41467-026-75305-0].

Operational stability is advanced at interfaces and in the bulk. 2PB-T devices retained more than 98.6% of initial PCE after 1752 h of MPPT at 85 degrees C in air [@10.1126/science.aed8175]. PF2C, a multidentate fullerene that stabilizes PEABr on the surface, leaves encapsulated devices at 98.7% after 2100 h of MPP tracking and 95.8% after 1800 h at 85 degrees C [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01517]. Air-blade cells kept 93.11% after 1300 h at maximum power point under 1 sun [@10.1038/s41467-026-75053-1]. Rubidium evens the vertical Cs distribution in CsDMAMAFA films, and Rb-doped wide-bandgap cells reach 21.62% at 1.67 eV and 22.51% with PDAI2 treatment [@10.1021/acsnano.6c04084]. PAH retards FAPbI3 crystallization in antisolvent-free processing and grows grains to 1.11 plus or minus 0.01 micrometers [@10.1002/adfm.77164]. Phase-pure n = 1 2D perovskites form without thermal activation, with domains parallel and tilted to the 3D lattice [@10.1002/adma.74247].

Flexible and ambient routes overlap with stability. Open-circuit voltages and fill factors above 90% of the Shockley-Queisser radiative limit appear across 1.53 to 1.77 eV bandgaps, and ambient-air flexible cells reached 23.03% and kept 95% after 10,000 bending cycles [@10.1007/s40820-026-02295-5]. DPVP enables reproducible fabrication at 20 to 40% relative humidity, with 24.2% small devices and 20.5% on 12 cm2 mini-modules [@10.1021/acsami.6c09922]. TTB-modified precursors held 25.76% after seven days in ambient conditions and 23.88% after three months [@10.1002/adfm.77211]. Vapor-phase annealing gives additive-free inverted cells a champion 22.15% [@10.1021/acsami.6c07418].

Encapsulation and degradation physics draw attention. Inkjet-printed PHPS with VUV conversion forms SiOxNy barrier films, and encapsulated cells kept 93% plus or minus 5% of PCE after 1000 h at 25 degrees C and 60% RH under ISOS-D-1 [@10.1002/smll.74816]. In carbon-based hole-transport-layer-free cells under ISOS-L-1 and ISOS-L-2, light-induced degradation starts at the top and buried interfaces and spreads into the bulk [@10.1002/solr.70421], while a PEACl-induced 2D capping layer raises such devices to 17.75% with a fill factor of 69.02% [@10.1002/solr.70447]. Residual lithium from Li-TFSI doping of Spiro-OMeTAD migrates during operation, motivating the volatile dopant AM-TFSI, which leaves negligible residues [@10.1002/smll.74289].

Protocol choice changes conclusions. Trapped charge triggers phase segregation, and wide-bandgap devices under MSPT retain approximately 90% of initial power after 700 h, unlike MPPT-operated devices [@10.1002/aenm.71358]. The gap from the MPPT retention figures above suggests that tracking mode should be reported alongside every lifetime number.

Data-driven diagnostics are emerging. A deep-learning framework estimates retention from multimodal luminescence imaging during aging, with global luminescence carrying most of the predictive signal [@10.1002/solr.70433]. PerovSegNet reaches 87.25% mean Average Precision, beating the baseline by 4.08% at 25.22% lower computational cost, with zero-shot transfer to lead-free perovskites [@10.1038/s41524-026-02211-w]. One review argues for tracking stress and strain under fluctuating temperature, illumination, and humidity, favoring buried interface engineering and polymer incorporation [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01837]. In situ curvature and GI-XRD show tensile stress relaxing by diffusion-mediated inelastic deformation, and stress pre-relaxation cuts moisture uptake by up to an order of magnitude [@10.26434/chemrxiv-2025-128tn/v2].

Scale-up is also economic. A cradle-to-grave LCA of an inverted PSC gives 0.20 kg CO2 eq./kWh, an EPBT of 0.53 years, an EROI of 9.50, and an LCOE of 0.29 EUR/kWh at a production cost of 0.67 EUR/cm2 [@10.1016/j.jclepro.2026.148924]. European recycling of perovskite PV glass can save up to 7 dollars per square meter, with scalability assessed to 4 TW [@10.1007/s44498-026-00134-9]. Upscaling to series-connected 64 cm2 modules is dominated by resistive transport and interconnection losses that match intrinsic material losses [@10.1002/solr.70375], and flexible module research still lags rigid modules, with efficient and scalable flexible modules unresolved [@10.1002/inf2.70159]. A photovoltaic cathode with Pt reduced to 5.60 micrograms per square centimeter delivered 19.8 mA per square centimeter at 0.0 V RHE and retained 77% after 160 h in acid [@10.1002/adfm.77308].
