The verified performance ceiling reaches a certified power conversion efficiency (PCE) of 33.1% in two-terminal (2T) tandems [@10.1126/sciadv.aei2945] and 27.31% in single junctions [@10.1126/sciadv.aeg1456] [@10.1002/adma.73807], yet the certified reporting share was 5.9% in 2026-06 and 8.3% in 2026-07, with only 22 of 169 closely read papers reporting any certified value. Peak metrics do not license operational durability, as uncertified microscale sweeps mask interfacial breakdown, phase demixing, and scaling penalties.

First, reported contact stabilization does not establish whether interfacial dipoles and multidentate coordination withstand thermo-mechanical fatigue. While transparent oxide modification with self-assembled monolayer (SAM) contacts and multidentate chelators elevates initial open-circuit voltage (Voc), thermal expansion mismatches induce cyclic shear stresses triggering delamination [@10.1126/sciadv.aei2945] [@10.1002/adma.73807] [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01489]. Resolving bond survival under operating fields requires operando double-cantilever beam fracture testing paired with bias-dependent Kelvin probe force microscopy during thermal cycling.

Second, chemical treatments do not demonstrate permanent suppression of halide segregation in wide-bandgap (WBG) absorbers. Although molecular interlayers and B-site alloying achieve high initial efficiency in tandems [@10.1002/anie.1676645] [@10.1002/aenm.71317], in situ thermal cycling reveals phase segregation above 50 °C with only partial recovery upon cooling [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00992]. Differentiating thermodynamic phase stability from kinetic demixing arrest demands operando nanoscale photoluminescence spectroscopy synchronized with diffraction profiling under continuous electrical bias and elevated temperature.

Third, unstandardized testing leaves the boundary between reversible ionic polarization and irreversible decomposition unquantified. Only 4 papers reported a time to 80% of initial efficiency (T80) lifetime, the longest 9800 h [@10.1002/adma.74243], and only 4 papers named an International Summit on Organic Photovoltaic Stability (ISOS) protocol [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01517] [@10.1126/sciadv.aeg1456]. Quantifying ion accumulation rates under operation requires operando intensity-modulated photovoltage spectroscopy coupled with secondary-ion mass spectrometry during maximum power point tracking (MPPT).

Fourth, microscale defect passivation fails to establish scalability across large apertures. Only 27 of 648 works addressed scale-up, and the largest reported aperture reached 818 cm2 [@10.1021/jacs.6c06932]. Passivators eliminating traps on sub-0.1 cm2 apertures aggregate during meniscus drying, intensifying non-radiative recombination across laser-scribed modules [@10.1126/sciadv.aed6327] [@10.1002/solr.70375]. Calibrating spatially resolved electroluminescence imaging against local series-resistance maps during coating would isolate interconnect losses.

Direct comparison between molecular passivators and strain compensators remains blocked because published studies evaluated contact modifications under disparate illumination levels without extracting defect formation enthalpies. Differentiating their stabilizing effects demands extracting ion-hopping activation barriers from temperature-dependent transient capacitance profiles, alongside operando mapping of internal potential distributions under continuous bias stress.
