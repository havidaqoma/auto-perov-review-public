The performance divergence between single junctions and tandems defines the first gap: the best certified perovskite/silicon tandem this month was 33.1% power conversion efficiency (PCE) [@10.1126/sciadv.aei2945], while certified single junctions clustered near 27%, showing that the wide-bandgap (WBG) subcell limiting the tandem is not the one most papers optimise. Chemical treatments claimed to homogenize mixed halides in pristine WBG absorbers leave unresolved whether halide homogenisation suppresses phase segregation under operating bias, as carrier gradients trigger iodide clustering [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c00992] [@10.1002/aenm.71358]. Operando nanoscale photoluminescence spectroscopy during continuous maximum power point tracking (MPPT) load under elevated operating temperatures would establish whether compositional uniformity survives operational electric fields.

A second gap concerns buried-interface chemistry that survives thermal cycling rather than only lifting initial efficiency. Molecules and self-assembled monolayers (SAMs) passivate buried defects to lift open-circuit voltage (Voc) on pristine substrates, but thermal expansion mismatches induce cyclic shear stresses that cause delamination [@10.1126/sciadv.aei2945] [@10.1021/acsenergylett.6c01489]. In situ grazing-incidence wide-angle X-ray scattering with operando transient photovoltage measurements during temperature cycling across thermal extremes would quantify strain relaxation and resolve whether interfacial bonding withstands thermal fatigue.

A third gap lies in ion-migration timescales measured under load rather than in the dark. Only 4 of 169 closely read papers reported a time to 80% of initial performance (T80) lifetime, the longest 9800 h [@10.1002/adma.74243], short against a 25-year module warranty. Extracting kinetics from dark impedance ignores how photogenerated carriers screen fields and alter vacancy formation enthalpies under illumination [@10.1038/s41467-026-75305-0] [@10.1002/aenm.71358]. Intensity-modulated photovoltage spectroscopy under bias illumination would capture true ionic drift velocities and accumulation rates under load.

The fourth gap is whether passivation that lifts Voc on a 0.1 cm2 cell survives slot-die coating at module area. Only 27 of 648 works addressed scale-up at all, and the largest reported aperture was 818 cm2 [@10.1021/jacs.6c06932], orders of magnitude below a commercial module. Meniscus coating promotes passivator aggregation, forming pinholes across large areas [@10.1002/adma.74316]. Operando photoluminescence imaging synchronized with optical reflectometry during slot-die deposition would track drying kinetics and isolate spatial Voc losses across modules. Next month's literature would gain immediate utility if studies paired small-cell efficiencies with operando stability under load and reported spatial coating uniformity.
