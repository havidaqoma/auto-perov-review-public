Perovskite single junctions are now certified above 27% power conversion efficiency (PCE) and perovskite/silicon tandems above 33%, so the field's open problems have moved from raw efficiency to whether a claimed efficiency is verifiable, whether it survives at module area, and whether it survives operation. High peak efficiencies on small-area cells increasingly diverge from the operational demands of modules, where non-radiative recombination losses at defect sites, interfacial delamination, and volatile species desorption dictate practical viability.

In July 2026, 648 indexed works met the scope gate and 181 were read closely. This review follows four mechanistic threads: buried-interface and contact chemistry, defect tolerance and ion migration, wide-bandgap absorbers for tandems, and operational stability, with scale-up as the constraint coupling them. Interfacial chemical interactions govern carrier extraction and charge accumulation, defect landscapes dictate electric-field-driven ion migration and halide segregation, and wide-bandgap absorbers remain vulnerable to photoinduced phase separation, with all these processes subject to the physical boundaries of scalable deposition.

The month's headline numbers are strong, the evidence behind them is unevenly reported, and this review separates the two. Critical evaluation distinguishes verifiable physical mechanisms from uncertified champion values, non-standardized aging procedures, and uncharacterized degradation modes.
